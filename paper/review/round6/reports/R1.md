# R1 — Methodology Skeptic Review (Round 6, AEI blind review)

Reviewer role: rigorous experimental design, defensible statistics, honest novelty positioning.
Paper: `paper/main.tex` (68 pp). All ground-truth paths below are relative to the repository root.
I verified the paper's quantitative claims against the released skills, benchmark scripts, git
history, and `results/benchmark/*.json`. I read the rendered pages (PNG) for all figures in my
scope (Figs. 3, 4, 5, 6–8 and Tables 2–4, 8–9 as rendered on pp. 33–48).

---

## 1. Overall assessment

This paper is, by the standard of LLM-diagnosis submissions, unusually honest: it publishes a
same-model ablation (bare LLM 9/9) that **exceeds** its own pipeline (6/9), discloses single-run
evidence, refuses significance tests across mismatched scenario sets, and narrates its three
non-resolved verdicts as misses rather than wins. The machine-checkable evaluation contract
(truth isolation sentinel, gate-graded scoring, deterministic rubric, execution proofs) is real
and mostly matches the released code. The statistical mechanics (Wilson intervals, small-n
framing) are used correctly where I could recompute them.

However, I found **four claim-vs-reality defects**, each touching a load-bearing methodological
claim, plus one formalization that does not match the released protocol. None invalidates the
central honest narrative (the ablation bounds accuracy regardless), but each must be corrected
before the paper's protocol descriptions can be trusted at face value. Hence major revision.

## 2. MUST-FIX (blocking)

### MF-1. The five-factor confidence weights are misstated — and the three ground-truth sources disagree with each other
- **Paper (p. 13, §4.1, main.tex:284):** five factors "weighted 25/20/20/20/10, totalling 100".
  25+20+20+20+10 = **95**, not 100 — the sentence is internally contradictory on its face.
- **Released artifact** (`workspace/diagnostic-runs/202609141854259_bench_tep_d04_reactor_cooling_step/04_diagnostics/confidence.json`,
  `confidence_breakdown.H1.five_factor_breakdown`): maxes are statistical **25**, physical **25**,
  temporal **20**, confounds **20**, symptom **10** (sum 100). This is 25/**25**/20/20/10 — not the
  paper's 25/20/20/20/10.
- **Skill, source A** (`.claude/skills/industrial-diagnostician/resources/diagnosis_method.md:69-73`):
  25/20/20/20/**15** (sum 100).
- **Skill, source B** (`.claude/skills/industrial-diagnostician/references/agent-protocol.md:168-174`):
  **20**/25/20/20/**15** — statistical and physical swapped relative to source A.
- So the paper matches *neither* released artifact *nor* either skill document, and the two skill
  documents contradict each other. Since §4.1's `c ≤ min(five-factor, c̄(v))` is the paper's
  central calibration mechanism, the weights must be stated exactly as implemented (artifact:
  25/25/20/20/10), and the skill-internal inconsistency should be fixed and disclosed.

### MF-2. The C1 keyword-timing claim is contradicted by the repository's own git history
- **Paper (p. 18, §5 C1, main.tex:326):** "the keyword set used for scoring is the one committed
  **before the final pipeline re-execution**."
- **Ground truth:** all 12 canonical graded runs are dated 2026-09-14 18:43 – 2026-09-15 04:53
  (run-dir timestamps; matches the disclosed 14–15 Sept window). Commit `221d061`
  (2026-09-15 **19:20** +0800) later revised `scripts/benchmark/cases/benchmark_cases.json`
  scoring keywords: it deletes weak generic tokens (`入口`, `温度`, `水`, `压力`, `cooling`, `阀`)
  and **adds true-mechanism tokens** (`阀阻`, `吸入侧`, `冷却水`, `cooling water`, `进料配比`,
  `阀粘滞`, `ph 控制`, …), adding a `keyword_revision` note that states the revision was applied
  uniformly "先于 v2 最终聚合" — i.e., **after the runs, before scoring**.
- The gradings were written 2026-09-16 17:43 (+0800), *after* the revision, and their `kw_hits`
  contain the newly added tokens (e.g., `results/benchmark/gradings/tep_d04_reactor_cooling_step.json`:
  `kw_hits: ["反应器冷却","冷却水"]` — tokens that did not exist in the pre-revision keyword set).
- Post-execution editing of the truth-comparison instrument is exactly the researcher degree of
  freedom C1 exists to foreclose. Mitigations exist — deletions are anti-inflationary, the note
  asserts no per-case tuning, per-case `kw_hits` are released, and pre-revision keyword sets are
  in git history — but the paper's sentence is factually false as written. It must be reworded to
  match the released history ("keywords were revised once, uniformly, after execution and before
  scoring; the note is released") **and** a keyword-sensitivity analysis (headline Top-1/Top-k
  under the pre-revision sets, recomputable from released `kw_hits` + git) should be added.

### MF-3. The "15/15 within-era stability" headline is inflated by runs from the disowned pre-audit era
- **Paper (p. 20, §7.4 / §8.4, main.tex:601, 609):** "within-version verdict-type and Top-1
  agreement is 15/15 case-version pairs (pre-discipline era 12/12; current era 3/3 pairs recorded
  so far)."
- **Ground truth:** `results/benchmark/stability_report.json` (summary: 15/15, `min_runs_required: 2`)
  counts 12 v1-era pairs + 3 v2-era pairs. The v1 era (2026-09-12/13 run dirs) was produced by the
  scripted direct pipeline that the paper's own §5 C4 disowns: legacy gradings
  (`results/benchmark/legacy_note_era/gradings/*.json`) carry
  `judge_score_source: "note-self-declared"`, and `docs/benchmark/AUDIT-2026-09-13.md` §0 states
  the benchmark chain of that era "不执行多 agent 管线" (did not execute the multi-agent pipeline)
  with synthesized artifacts/events. The stability report marks these v1 runs `proven: true` on
  the strength of finalize PASS — which the fabricated-artifact shortcut could satisfy.
- The paper describes v1 only as "pre anti-oscillation/cap discipline (system revision)"
  (stability_report.json `era_semantics`, quoted approvingly) — a benign characterization that
  hides that 12 of the 15 pairs come from execution modes the paper elsewhere repudiates. The
  stability claim should be re-based on the current era (3/3 pairs, n=3 scenarios) plus the
  outstanding seeded retest, with the v1 pairs either excluded or explicitly labelled as
  retired-pipeline provenance. The IDV14 signature divergence disclosure (≈12 min vs ≈30 min
  period) is good practice and should be kept.

### MF-4. Eq. (1)'s ceiling function does not match the released protocol
- **Paper (p. 13, Eq. 1, main.tex:271-274):** c̄ = 0.90 (DET) / 0.70 (CS) / 0.50 (anti-oscillation)
  / 1.0 "no binding ceiling" (ND); plus the 0.65 INDISTINGUISHABLE subclass (main.tex:284).
- **Ground truth:** `.claude/skills/industrial-diagnostician/SKILL.md:106` documents
  "INDISTINGUISHABLE ≤ 65, COMPETING_SET ≤ 70, [PARAM_AMBIGUITY] ≤ 50" and
  `references/agent-protocol.md:176` repeats 65/50. **No 0.90 DETERMINED ceiling and no
  NEEDS_DATA=1.0 convention exists anywhere in the skill** (repo-wide grep: absent). The 0.50 in
  the diagnostician is the *PARAM_AMBIGUITY* rule; the *anti-oscillation* ≤0.50 lives in the
  orchestrator (`.claude/skills/industrial-analysis-auto/SKILL.md:473`). Eq. (1) silently
  collapses two different rules and invents two undocumented values.
- Worse, the released artifact shows an **additional evidence-derived ceiling that Eq. (1) omits**:
  the canonical IDV4 `confidence.json` records `confidence_ceilings_applied: [{ceiling: 75,
  reason: "DIRECT_MEASUREMENT"}]` — the reported 0.75 *equals* this undocumented cap, yet §7.2
  (p. 37) narrates 0.75 as merely "under the 0.90 determined-verdict ceiling". The operative
  binding constraint in the released record is a mechanism Eq. (1) does not contain. Formalize
  what ships (verdict-state caps + evidence-rank caps + oscillation cap) or stop calling Eq. (1)
  "the confidence ceiling".

### MF-5. The elimination rule |X_i| > |E_i| is the paper's own formalization, not the implemented contract
- **Paper (p. 13, main.tex:275):** "h_i is eliminated when its contradictions outweigh its support,
  |X_i| > |E_i| under the evaluator's weighting" — presented as the quantitative elimination
  contract.
- **Ground truth:** the diagnostician protocol implements "eliminate at least 2 [hypotheses],
  exclusion_confidence ≥ 90, record revival_condition" (`.claude/skills/industrial-diagnostician/SKILL.md:95`,
  agent-protocol.md Phase 5) via the four-condition test (SKILL.md:126-129). The cardinality
  inequality appears nowhere in the released skill. Presenting it as the governing rule
  overstates the protocol's mechanical strictness; either implement/verify it or describe the
  actual rule.

## 3. SHOULD-FIX (non-blocking)

1. **Abstract overstatement (p. 1, main.tex:52):** "nine fault scenarios with per-fault literature
   baselines" — only the 6-fault TEP subset has literature baselines at all, and FE scores only 4
   of them. Contribution 4 (main.tex:78) states it correctly; align the abstract.
2. **Retest-draw history incomplete (p. 20, main.tex:601):** `results/benchmark/retest_selection.json`
   records **four** draw rounds (seeds 141354613, 2066730807, 503865577, 149290398 — the last
   three all drawing tep_d11). The paper cites only round 4 and claims the scenario "cannot be
   hand-picked"; since `select-retest-case.mjs` generates a fresh seed per invocation, repeated
   draws are possible. Disclose the full draw history and the seed-derivation method.
3. **Pre-audit history narration is selective (p. 18, §5 C4, main.tex:332):** the paper discloses
   the artifact-fabrication shortcut but not that the same audit found (a) ground-truth leakage in
   5/6 TEP briefs and 2 weak-keyword scenarios (AUDIT-2026-09-13.md §0; fix commit `a149ba2`,
   2026-09-14 00:22, which rewrote briefs for skab_valve1_1, tep_d03_hard, indpensim_batch093),
   and (b) a degenerate self-declared judge gate (AUDIT D1). The v1→v2 "epistemic tightening" that
   produced the capped-verdict flips includes these brief rewrites; say so.
4. **Mechanism-level "weak" pair description (p. 20, main.tex:601):** "share the verdict type and
   the implicated channel" — `results/benchmark/consistency_audit.json` shows `primary_tag: null`
   for both SKAB-valve v2 runs and cause-token overlap **0.076**; channel-sharing is interpretation,
   not machine-verified. Soften or cite the mechanism_class only.
5. **Control-pass CI lower bound (Table 2, main.tex:432; Fig. 7, p. 42):** Wilson 3/3 lower bound
   is 43.85 → 43.8, not 43.9 (recomputed with `aggregate.mjs`'s own wilson(), z=1.96).
6. **Verdict-type compliance is weakly powered (Table 2):** 8/9 scenarios' `expect_type_set`
   (`scripts/benchmark/cases/benchmark_cases.json`) admit both DETERMINED and COMPETING_SET, so the
   8/9 metric can only fail in one direction; state this next to the rubric-ceiling caveat of §6.2.
7. **Fig. 5 (p. 37):** bottom evidence-chain strip has overlapping text (the chain line collides
   with "verdict: capped COMPETING_SET 0.65" and "feedback"). Legible but sloppy; fix spacing.

## 5. Assessment of my assigned focus areas

**§1 novelty positioning.** Fair and above-average for the genre. FaultExplainer is described
with its two prompt regimes, its alias/related-acceptance leniency, and the paper explicitly
transcribes FE's 8/11 figure while flagging FE's internal inconsistency ("we transcribe the
reported figure", main.tex:90) — the right posture. AgentRCA and the AEI-venue precedents are
credited; Table 1's "no prior system combines (i)–(iv)" is defensible given the stated reading,
and §2.4's intellectual-ancestry section (Heuer, Hill, Chow, Parasuraman, Lee & See) is exactly
the honest framing I want to see. No overclaim found in §1 beyond the abstract's
"per-fault literature baselines" phrasing (SF-1).

**§4 formalization.** The three-state decision function and evidence-level min rule are real
protocol features; the ceilings, weights, and elimination inequality are not accurately
formalized (MF-1, MF-4, MF-5). The "ND = no binding ceiling" row is also odd on its face — the
state that declares evidence insufficient is the one state with no cap — and is undocumented in
the released skill.

**§5–§7 benchmark design.** Truth isolation is genuinely engineered: `check-leakage.mjs`
implements four check classes (truth strings in briefs, fault vocabulary in own process
description, forbidden grader keys at any depth, glossary contamination) and gates with exit 1;
the anti-fabrication rule ("scripts may verify and score, never author",
run-benchmark-pipeline.mjs:15-19) is real. Scenario selection is non-random and disclosed as
documentation-driven. Wilson intervals are used correctly and the refusal to test across
mismatched scenario sets is correct. **Same-model baseline fairness:** the 9/9-vs-6/9 comparison
uses asymmetric success semantics (forced-answer keyword hit vs DETERMINED-gated resolution) —
the paper says so (§8.1 caveats, §8.3 "unverifiable guess"), and the bias runs against the
pipeline, so it bounds the accuracy claim rather than inflating it; the FE-protocol replication
scoring 6/6 on the same model (i.e., beating IDD's 5/6) is reported plainly. This is honest
ablation practice. **FE comparison fairness:** handled with three explicit caveats and a
same-model replication; the per-fault table is labelled descriptive. Remaining quibble: baseline
proportions in Table 8 carry no CIs while IDD's do — harmless here, but add them for symmetry.

**§7.4 stability.** The targeted 3-scenario check and its IDV14 signature-divergence caveat are
well done; the seeded retest gap (INSUFFICIENT-RUNS) is disclosed rather than papered over —
good. The 15/15 headline is the problem (MF-3), and the within-era framing quietly benefits from
counting the disowned era's runs. The mechanism-level 2-consistent/1-weak/0-divergent split
matches `consistency_audit.json` (statuses CONSISTENT/WEAK for the three v2 pairs; the only
DIVERGENT entries are cross-era pairs correctly excluded from the within-era claim).

**Repair-loop governance.** Fully verified against the orchestrator skill (spot-check 4),
including the best-round snapshot and scoped-repair mechanics that §4.6 describes.

---

## 6. Verdict

The paper's honest core (the ablation that undercuts its own accuracy story, the capped verdicts,
the execution proofs) survives scrutiny, and most numbers check out exactly. But a reader cannot
currently trust the paper's descriptions of (a) its calibration arithmetic, (b) when its scoring
instrument was frozen, (c) what its stability number counts, and (d) its ceiling function — four
failures of claim-vs-reality in the methodology chapters of a paper whose thesis is
machine-checkable claims. All are fixable by rewording, re-basing the stability statistic on
current-era pairs, one sensitivity analysis, and aligning Eq. (1)/weights with the released
artifacts — no new pipeline execution strictly required (though the outstanding tep_d11 retest
and the pre-registered repeat study would materially strengthen §7.4). Major revision.
