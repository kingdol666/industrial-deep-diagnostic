# R8 — Skeptical General Review (blind, fresh; Round 7)

Paper: `paper/main.tex` (70 pp, elsarticle preprint/review). I read the full source, the rendered
pages (`paper/review/round7/pages/p-*.png`), and verified claims against repository ground truth.
Per the blindness rule I read nothing under `paper/review/` except the briefing and the page renders.

## 1. Whole-paper read (abstract / intro promise vs delivery)

The abstract makes six load-bearing promises: (1) nine-stage gated agentic pipeline; (2) evidence
grading L1–L7 with gate propagation; (3) four-condition anti-spurious filter; (4) 12-scenario
benchmark on SKAB/TEP/IndPenSim; (5) 6/9 Top-1 with Wilson CI, 3 honest capped COMPETING_SET, 3/3
controls; (6) a bare-LLM ablation that *bounds the accuracy claim* (9/9 > 6/9). Every one of these is
delivered in the body (Secs. 3–9) and backed by released artifacts I checked. Notably, the abstract
does **not** claim an accuracy advantage over baselines — it explicitly repositions the contribution
as "auditability, calibrated uncertainty, and gated process guarantees rather than accuracy". The
title's "evaluated against per-fault literature baselines" is delivered in Sec. 8.1 + Appendix
Table A1. The word "guarantee" appears only in scoped phrases ("gated process guarantees",
"process guarantees ... remains to be demonstrated" in the conclusion) — acceptable, not overclaimed.
"Prove" is not used for empirical claims. The bare-LLM 9/9 result is stated in the abstract,
Sec. 8.3, Sec. 9 (twice), and the conclusion — always with the same honest framing. My criticism is
redundancy, not spin (see §5).

## 2. Novelty positioning

Sec. 2 is careful and, in my judgment, honest. FaultExplainer is treated as the closest published
system with three explicitly stated protocol differences (top-3+alias vs strict single verdict;
candidates-in-prompt vs none; 11-fault PCA-detectable scope vs IDD also scoring IDV3/IDV4). The
transcription caveat for FE's internally inconsistent 8/11 is disclosed ("we transcribe the reported
figure"). AgentRCA (2026), Gong et al. (MCQA-only), CausalKGPT/FD-LLM/Wen/Xu (this journal) are
positioned; Table 1 claims only (iii) calibrated three-state conclusions and (iv) a machine-verifiable
evaluation contract as absent from all cited systems — a defensible claim given the cited works.
Sec. 2.4 (trust/automation ancestry: Parasuraman, Lee & See, Heuer ACH, Hill, Chow, OntoCAPE)
explicitly disclaims invention ("a domain-specific, machine-checked instantiation ... not their
invention"). This is the right posture and I found no overclaim in the positioning.

## 5. Structure, writing, figures

- **Flow** is logical (architecture → method → framework → setup → results → comparison/ablation/
  stability → discussion), and the four contribution bullets each map to identifiable sections.
  Sec. 8 packs four topics (comparison, Register B, ablation, stability, case study) into one
  section; with Sec. 8.4 alone running to ~2 pages of dense disclosure, splitting would help.
- **Four numbering schemes** (Pipeline Step, Agent Phase, Reasoning Segment R1–R8, protocol states)
  are mostly kept distinct, but the VLM stage is "Step 3.5" at pipeline level and "data-processor's
  Phase 5.5" at agent level — correct, yet guaranteed to trip at least some readers; one clause of
  mapping would fix it.
- **Redundancy**: the bare-LLM-better-than-pipeline result and the single-miss disclosure are each
  repeated 4–5 times across abstract/8.3/9/conclusion. Honest, but ~1 page could be recovered.
- **Length**: 70 pp in 12pt preprint/review format is roughly 35–38 typeset journal pages; the
  appendix tables and the volume of disclosed method detail justify most of it, but the discussion
  (Sec. 9) could shed the repeated ablation restatements.
- **Figures** (viewed as rendered pages): Fig. 1 (architecture, p.12) and Fig. 2 (pipeline, p.14)
  are clean TikZ, readable at print size; Fig. 2's right-to-left bottom row is explained in the
  caption. Fig. 3 (p.34) matches metrics exactly (SKAB 0/2, TEP 5/6 with amber CS segment,
  IndPenSim 1/1). Fig. 4 (p.37) confidence chart matches all twelve values and marks the 0.70
  ceiling. Fig. 5 (p.38) case study annotations match the released artifacts digit-for-digit.
  Fig. 6 (p.42) and Fig. 7 (p.43) match Table A1/baselines.json. Okabe–Ito palette throughout.
  Print quality: good.
- **Terminology** is defined before use (verdict states, evidence levels, ceiling, Registers A/B);
  the "protocol terms, not everyday senses" disclaimer for DETERMINED/COMPETING_SET is a nice touch.

## 6. Verdict rationale

Every number I checked (25+ values across metrics, rubric, baselines, determinism, stability,
retest, ceilings, case study, CIs) matches the released artifacts, and the honesty infrastructure
promised in the abstract is real and machine-checkable. The single substantive description-vs-reality
problem is the RAG-channel paragraph (repeated in the discussion), which mischaracterises *which*
runs degraded vs retrieved and overstates the "no L6 item" claim by one corroboration-only evidence
item. That is a localised correction, not a validity problem. I sign a favorable report contingent on
that fix.
