## 9. §7–§8 honesty assessment

- §7.1/§7.3: ceiling compliance is explicitly labelled circular ("a property of the protocol's
  arithmetic, not an empirical discovery"); the SKAB valve verdict-type miss is reported as the
  benchmark's single compliance miss and the one-directional typing of `expect_type_set` is
  disclosed (compliance can flag over-capping, never under-capping) — unusually candid.
- §7.5: the IDV14 period discrepancy (≈12 min vs ≈30 min across sessions) is disclosed with the
  explicit bound that the signature column "should not be read as a reproducible period
  measurement"; cross-era flips are attributed to a documented discipline tightening, and the
  seeded-draw gap (insufficient-runs, execution contract outstanding) is reported rather than
  papered over.
- §8.1: the Register-A comparison is explicitly descriptive ("we refrain from a formal significance
  test"), with the three protocol caveats (candidates, alias scoring, different base models)
  stated at each use.
- §8.3: the bare-LLM 9/9 result is stated *before* the pipeline row in the narrative and correctly
  reframes the pipeline's contribution; the missing TEP control in the bare arm is disclosed as an
  execution-coverage gap (Fig 8 caption) and verified in `baselines.json`.
- §9: statistical power (wide CI), single-execution selection effect from the repair loop,
  prompt-knowledge contamination with the residual scenario-id channel, author-designed rubric/
  keywords/allowed-sets, missing expert anchoring, and the single-model/one-deployment scope are
  all stated plainly. I found no overclaim in §7–§8 that the released data contradicts.

## 10. Residual concerns (non-blocking)

1. **Power**: n=9 fault scenarios give a CI half-width of ±26 points; the paper says so, but the
   per-dataset bars (SKAB 0/2 Top-1 in Fig 3a) will still be over-read; the pre-registered
   corpus-extension plan is the right remedy and should remain a condition of record.
2. **Rubric at ceiling**: 10/12 at 100 with author-designed weights; disclosed as conformance-only
   with "limited discriminative power". Fine as a process check; it must never be cited as
   accuracy — the paper consistently does so.
3. **Chronology wording**: as in §5 of this report, one clarifying sentence on the
   executions-vs-switch overlap would make Table 4 airtight.
4. **Multiplicity**: many intervals are printed without multiplicity adjustment; acceptable here
   because no significance test is claimed and the convention (z=1.96) is declared and uniform,
   but a one-line reminder in §6.2 would help.
5. The journal's `calibrated` field is a genuine trap for readers re-deriving provenance from the
   journal alone; the paper's footnote resolves it, and I suggest the released journal schema add a
   separate keyword-provenance field in the next revision.

## 11. Verdict

Every quantitative claim I checked (12 spot checks, all match) traces to a released artifact, the
uncertainty quantification uses one consistent, correctly-implemented Wilson convention everywhere
including both required locations (Table 7 and Fig 7), the chronology table withstands a
row-by-row audit against the journal and git, the sensitivity result reproduces exactly under the
grader's own logic, and the paper's honesty apparatus (explicit IDV3 miss, pre-revision qualifiers
at every occurrence, ablation that bounds its own accuracy claim, disclosed coverage gaps and
disclosure of author-designed instruments) is consistent with what the repository actually
contains. Remaining issues are presentational, not empirical.

**Recommendation: MINOR_REVISION** (the statistics and disclosure are publication-ready; the few
clarifying sentences above should be added).
