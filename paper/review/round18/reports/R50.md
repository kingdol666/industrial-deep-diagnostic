## 3. Issues

### Must-fix (blocking, wording-level; no new experiments required)

- **A. §6.4 Implementation, first sentence (p.29 tex line 437)** — "The reasoning stages execute the
  full nine-stage agent pipeline through the engine layer (Section 3.1)" is not true of the canonical
  benchmark runs: they were driven by `scripts/benchmark/zcode_direct_pipeline.mjs` directly on the
  ZCode CLI, which is not one of the fourteen engines registered in `engines.mjs`/
  `diagnosis.service.mjs` (verified SC-4/SC-8). The same subsection's disclosure paragraph corrects
  this ("the CLI-driver class of the engine layer"), but the opening sentence read alone
  overstates. Reword to "under the protocol discipline of Section 3.1's engine layer; the recorded
  runs were driven by the released benchmark driver on the ZCode CLI (disclosed below)".
- **B. §3.1 (p.9-10) vs §5 execution form** — "sub-agents communicate only through workspace
  artifacts, never through conversation context" is a property of the dispatched architecture;
  under the disclosed inline execution, all stages run in one session, so conversation-context
  isolation cannot hold for the canonical runs, and §5's "the dispatch mechanism is the only
  difference" does not note this corollary. One clarifying clause in §5 (artifact handoffs enforced
  by protocol; session-context sharing is part of the disclosed difference) closes the gap.

### Should-fix

- C. §3.3 "Why nine stages" (p.14): "each of which the model-controlled ablation independently
  supports" overreaches for rules 2–3; §8.3 itself concedes attribution needs the filter-disabled and
  engine-substitution arms. Suggest "bounds the accuracy cost of" instead of "independently supports".
- D. The paper's VLM step is numbered 3.5 (pipeline), 3+3.3 (orchestrator dispatch table), and Phase
  5.5 (data-processor). The four-numbering-schemes disclosure covers this, but one explicit
  equivalence parenthesis at first mention would prevent a needless stumble.
- E. Table 2's caption ends with a dangling line "Checkpoint predicates: Table 3." (p.17) — cosmetic
  caption break; and §3.5's page (p.16) is more than half empty from float repacking (disclosed as
  best-effort in the preamble; acceptable but noticeable).

### Positive findings worth recording
- The §2–§3 architecture description is unusually faithful: every count, filename, predicate, cap,
  and error code I checked matches the codebase exactly (SC-1..SC-7).
- The §5 execution-form disclosure is verbatim-accurate against the event logs, including the exact
  reason string and the "varying keys and formats" characterization (SC-8). Nothing in the logs
  contradicts it; no attempt is made to pass the benchmark runs off as dispatched sub-agent runs.
- The RAG narrative resists cherry-picking: it reports the one successful retrieval, the silent
  bypasses, and the failed probes with equal specificity, and the single L6-adjacent item is
  correctly characterized as corroboration-only.

## 5. Verdict rationale

Every architecture claim I checked in my assigned scope is true of the released codebase and of the
canonical graded runs — including several claims a skeptical reviewer would expect to be softened
(package census including the six checklist-less packages; per-run RAG engagement variance; the
inline execution form and its heterogeneous log records; the bare-LLM 9/9 result being repeated in
abstract and conclusion). The two must-fix items are single-sentence accuracy fixes in §6.4 and §5;
they concern precision of language about the execution path, not the validity of any result.

VERDICT: MINOR_REVISION (confidence: high for the §2–§3/§5 scope; other sections reviewed only as
context).
