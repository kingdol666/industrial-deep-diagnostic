# R3 — Cross-Disciplinary Review (human–automation trust, deployment, practical impact)

Manuscript: `paper/main.tex` (build of 18 Sep 2026; the version reporting Top-1 4/9, 13 recorded
executions, bare-LLM ablation 9/9). Review scope assigned: practical impact/adoption, the
three-state verdict as an escalation interface, cost economics, reusable engineering takeaways,
and broader blind spots (energy cost, over-reliance, vendor lock-in). I performed six
claim-vs-repository spot checks (§6). Note for the editor: the pre-rendered page images under
`paper/review/round18/pages/` correspond to an earlier build (their per-scenario confidences and
Top-1 figures do not match the current source); my figure-level comments are therefore based on the
current `main.tex` source and the released artifacts, not on those page images.

---

## 1. Summary and overall impression

The paper presents IDD, a nine-stage agentic RCA pipeline with evidence grading (L1–L7), a
three-state verdict protocol, bounded repair, and a machine-checked benchmark, and — unusually and
commendably — bounds its own accuracy contribution with a same-model bare-call ablation (9/9 bare
vs 4/9 resolved) that concedes accuracy outright and repositions the contribution as auditability,
calibrated uncertainty, and gated process.

From my angle, this is one of the more honest system papers I have reviewed: the ceiling-compliance
metric is labelled circular by construction (main.tex:456), one compliance miss is counted as a miss
rather than narrated as a success (main.tex:456, 570), single-execution evidence and repair selection
effects are disclosed (main.tex:566), and the deployment section explicitly separates "observed in
one deployment" from "projected" (main.tex:577–579). The auditability-and-gates repositioning is the
right response to the ablation and is the paper's most defensible claim.

The weaknesses from a practical-adoption standpoint are of omission rather than misrepresentation:
the cost story has no numbers on either side (no token, dollar, or energy accounting anywhere in the
released artifacts — verified, §6.5); the "escalation interface" between machine verdict and human
decision is designed but was never exercised with a human, and the software contains no sign-off
mechanism at all (§4.2); the only human-touching checkpoint in the pipeline defaults to
machine-resolved (`AUTO_RESOLVED`); and the multi-vendor/data-sovereignty story rests on an
architecture for which exactly one engine–model pair was ever executed.

## 2. Practical impact: would a plant engineer or informatics practitioner adopt this?

**What lands.** The most transferable practical idea in the paper is the reframing of capped
verdicts as instrumentation work orders: a `COMPETING_SET` that names the single discriminating
measurement (cooling-water inlet thermocouple for IDV4/IDV11; steam-header and feed temperatures for
IDV3) converts a non-answer into an actionable engineering instruction (main.tex:570, 585). The five
capped benchmark verdicts each doing this is genuinely useful evidence, and the film-line anecdote
(180 parameter–defect associations failing FDR correction → `NEEDS_DATA` with a discriminating-data
list, main.tex:579) shows the abstention behaviour surviving contact with real (messy, episodically
labelled) plant data — arguably the paper's most persuasive deployment datum precisely because the
outcome was "go collect the right data" rather than a fabricated cause.

**What does not land yet.**

1. *"An export job plus one API call rather than an instrumentation project"* (main.tex:581). Any
   practitioner who has integrated a historian knows this sentence is wrong: tag discovery, unit
   reconciliation, sampling alignment, resampling of uneven historian compression, bad-value
   handling, and window selection are the project. The ontology stage absorbs some of this (the
   film-line run had 180 candidate parameters to align), but the paper's own benchmark ingests
   clean, pre-cut public CSVs. This sentence will cost the paper credibility with exactly the AEI
   audience it courts. Fix (issue 4): replace with an honest integration-cost statement, ideally
   quantified from the film-line engagement (how many engineer-hours from "we have a historian" to
   first pipeline run?).

2. The film-line deployment is n=1, unverifiable by design (artifacts anonymised and not released,
   main.tex:579), and reports no duration, data volume, or number of engineers involved. I accept
   the confidentiality constraint, but as evidence it currently supports only "it ran and refused
   to fabricate". Even a sanitised appendix (time span, parameter count, which channels existed,
   what the plant did after the `NEEDS_DATA`) would raise it from anecdote to case report.

3. The operational footprint is undisclosed. Running IDD means: an agent-CLI harness with sub-agent
   spawn capability, 18 skill packages, a Python venv, an optional RAG microservice (which failed
   its health probe in all 13 recorded runs, main.tex:136), a Node backend, SQLite, and a frontend.
   A plant IT department will ask who owns this stack, how it is patched, and what happens when the
   provider CLI changes out from under it. One paragraph on the minimal deployment footprint and the
   failure modes observed during the benchmark (the gates bounced artifacts in 8 of 13 runs — what
   does an operator do when a gate fails at 2 a.m.?) would help.

4. Latency, oddly, is a strength the paper fails to claim: ≈2 h per scenario (main.tex:574) against
   human RCA episodes that routinely take days to weeks is an excellent trade if the audit trail is
   trusted — but the paper never makes this comparison concrete (no expert-hours figure, no episode
   baseline), and its own expert-vs-pipeline blind test is still only "planned" (main.tex:572). See
   issue 1.

## 3. The three-state verdict as an escalation interface: is the trust story rigorous and honestly bounded?

**Rigour of the framing.** Section 2.4 (main.tex:122–123) is the right intellectual ancestry —
Parasuraman's types/levels of automation, Lee & See's appropriate reliance, Chow's reject option,
Heuer's ACH, Hill's criteria — and the modesty clause ("a domain-specific, machine-checked
instantiation... not their invention") is exactly right. Mapping capped `COMPETING_SET` to
calibrated transfer-of-control is sound. The protocol-level mechanics are genuinely
trust-supporting: confidence bounded by the weakest evidence level; ceiling compliance as a checkable
arithmetic constraint; overconfidence (`DETERMINED` and wrong) as a first-class metric; eliminations
citing contradicting evidence; falsification conditions in the reasoning chain (R5).

**Honest bounds.** The paper is candid where it matters: ceiling compliance is circular by
construction (main.tex:456); confidence values are not validated as calibrated probabilities (the
psychometric study is "future work"); the cap-set gap (agent side documents no `DETERMINED` cap;
machine gate enforces 0.90) is measured in the corpus rather than hidden, with the two offending
verdicts (0.91, 0.93) recorded as rubric deductions (main.tex:456); the SKAB valve scenario's capped
verdict is scored a compliance miss; the in-pipeline judge is downgraded to execution-quality
evidence because "self-declared quality scores are not verifiable" (main.tex:572).

**Where the trust story is not yet rigorous:**

1. **No human was ever in the loop, and the software has no place for one.** The escalation
   interface claim culminates in "a `DETERMINED` verdict does not authorise maintenance action — a
   human engineer signs off" (main.tex:589). But: (a) the only checkpoint that can involve a human,
   CP-3, accepts `AUTO_RESOLVED`, and the ontology fast-reuse path explicitly bypasses any hub wait
   and jumps to Step 3 (`.claude/skills/industrial-analysis-auto/SKILL.md:288,309`); (b) a search of
   the web backend for any approval/sign-off mechanism returns nothing (no `signoff`,
   `approved_by`, `human_approval` in `app/backend/src`); (c) no engineer endorsement artifact
   exists in the 14-artifact contract (Table 5). The human in "human-in-the-loop" exists only as a
   governance assertion outside the released system. This must be disclosed in the paper, not
   discovered by a reader in the repository. See issues 5 and 6.

2. **Gate sensitivity is unmeasured.** The audit plane's independence is procedural (separate
   sessions, separate skill protocols), not statistical — every reviewer agent shares one model
   family and one prompt author. The corpus shows the gates can catch defects (8/13 runs bounced;
   the integrity audit caught a fabricated-artifact shortcut), but the false-negative rate — a
   wrong-but-passing diagnosis sailing through judge, auditor, and rubric — has never been probed.
   For an escalation interface, the operator needs to know not just "the gates usually pass good
   work" but "the gates catch X% of injected defects". A mutation test (inject subtly corrupted
   diagnoses — swapped evidence citations, an unfounded elimination, a confidence above ceiling —
   and measure gate detection) would be cheap, is fully in the spirit of the paper, and would itself
   be a reusable contribution. See issue 7.

3. **Automation bias is never discussed.** The paper cites Lee & See but does not carry the
   implication into deployment: a polished `DETERMINED` report at confidence 0.91 with an ENDORSED
   stamp, a 97/100 judge score, and a professional HTML deliverable is precisely the artifact
   profile that induces rubber-stamping. The raw material for the mitigation already exists —
   falsification conditions and revival conditions are machine-checked artifacts — but the paper
   should state how the report/HTML template surfaces "what would change this verdict" to the
   signing engineer, and how the interface avoids single-number confidence anchoring. See issue 8.

4. **Cap-set ambiguity has deployment consequences.** The paper measures the gap between the
   agent-side cap set (no `DETERMINED` cap) and the machine gate (flat 0.90;
   `scripts/benchmark/judge-rubric.mjs:133` — verified) but never says which governs in production.
   Leaving both in force means a deployment could ship 0.93-confidence verdicts that the vendor's
   own grading gate would flag. The fix is one line in the agent-side protocol plus a sentence here.
   See issue 3.

## 4. Cost analysis: is the ≈2 h/scenario economics credible?

**What is solid.** The 2.0 h figure is well-derived and honestly explained: the sequential drawn
re-execution (117 min event span) is the clean per-scenario number, and the ≈6 h canonical spans are
correctly attributed to orchestration batching rather than hidden (main.tex:359, 574). The repair
accounting (12/13 runs at zero diagnosis-layer repairs; one targeted round on IDV3) is disclosed.
The paper correctly says the comparison that matters is against the engineering cost of obtaining
root causes, not against a fitted classifier.

**What is missing — and it matters more here than in most papers, because the paper's central
claim is a cost–benefit trade.** The ablation says the pipeline gives up resolution (9/9 → 4/9) to
gain auditability. The price of that trade is never stated in any unit a practitioner or a
sustainability-minded reader can use:

- **No token accounting.** `results/benchmark/metrics.json` and the released benchmark artifacts
  contain no token counts (verified). A nine-stage, eight-sub-agent pipeline with judge, two
  auditors, reporter, HTML builder and reviewer plausibly spends one to two orders of magnitude
  more tokens than the bare call it is measured against. The event logs exist; the multiplier is
  computable from released data. Without it, the sentence "orders of magnitude more expensive than
  a fitted classifier" (main.tex:574) is an unquantified gesture in a paper whose entire value
  proposition is quantification.
- **No energy estimate.** For AEI in 2026, the energy/environmental footprint of multi-agent LLM
  pipelines is a legitimate reviewer ask, and this pipeline — with gated redundancy (judge, two
  physical audits, HTML build + review) — is the energy-expensive end of the design space. One
  paragraph with a per-scenario energy bound (from token counts × published per-token energy
  factors) is sufficient; it does not need to be exhaustive, it needs to exist. See issue 1.
- **The amortisation claim is untested in-corpus.** "The marginal cost of a diagnosis therefore
  falls as the fleet grows" (main.tex:589) rests on ontology fast-reuse — which the benchmark
  deliberately pinned to full rebuilds (main.tex:138), so the claim has zero measurements behind it
  in the released corpus. Either exercise reuse on one sibling pair and report the delta (the
  production harness reportedly does this in seconds) or mark the economics as projected like the
  other projections in that section. See issue 2.
- **Repair-loop cost is not broken out.** A run that spends its repair budget is not 2 h; the paper
  should state the worst observed run cost (IDV3 with one repair round is the natural datum).
- **The expert-hour baseline is asserted, never measured.** Fine — but then the planned
  expert-vs-pipeline blind test (main.tex:572) should be pre-registered with cost metrics
  (expert hours, minutes-to-first-usable-conclusion) so the economics claim becomes falsifiable.

Net: the 2 h number is credible; the economics argument built on it is not yet, because it has no
denominator. This is fixable from existing logs and should be a condition of acceptance.

## 5. What an industrial-informatics reader can reuse

This is the paper's strongest axis, and I want to say so plainly, because my other sections are
critical.

- **The five evaluation contracts (C1–C5, main.tex:275–291)** — truth isolation with a
  bidirectional leakage sentinel, SHA-256 fingerprinting, stage-authored provenance-labelled
  artifacts, gate-graded scoring where a missing artifact means *not executed* rather than a score,
  and an aggregate-consistency gate — are a directly reusable checklist for evaluating *any*
  agentic pipeline, well beyond fault diagnosis.
- **The completeness gate as anti-fabrication device** is exemplary: the paper's own history (a
  scripted shortcut once synthesised artifacts and agent events; the gate now makes it
  "permanently unreplayable", main.tex:283) is the most convincing demonstration that the gate does
  work, and the honesty of reporting it is a model for the field.
- **Draw-then-re-execute consistency testing** (seeded uniform random draw, append-only record,
  replayable; `results/benchmark/retest_selection.json` — verified: seed 1003818694, pool of 9,
  index 7 → tep_d11) is the right way to prevent post-hoc cherry-picking of stability checks, and
  the paper's refusal to over-read one consistent pair ("the beginning of a stability study, not
  its end", main.tex:551) is the correct posture.
- **The honest-ablation template** (bare same-model call as a lower bound on what the pipeline adds)
  should become standard in agentic-system papers; the 9/9-vs-4/9 disclosure is the single most
  valuable number in the paper.
- **Manifest-first HTML generation with independent review** (render_manifest.json written before
  the page, self-check, then a separate reviewer agent; byte-level size floor at CP-9) is a neat,
  reusable pattern for auditable generated documents.
- **Mechanical checkpoint predicates** (Table 4): every gate is a file-existence/schema/numeric
  bound a reader can re-run — the difference between an audit claim and an audit check.

What the reader cannot yet take away is the *minimal* reusable subset: with the filter-disabled and
engine-substitution ablation arms still unexecuted (main.tex:361, 532), the paper cannot answer the
practitioner's real question — "which 20% of this machinery gives me 80% of the audit value?" A
short discussion paragraph naming the plausible minimal set (truth isolation + completeness gate +
evidence-level ceiling + one auditor) and what evidence would be needed to justify dropping the
rest would materially increase the paper's reuse value.

## 6. Claim-vs-reality spot checks (my angle)

1. **Machine 0.90 non-CS ceiling** (main.tex:226, 367, 456) → `scripts/benchmark/judge-rubric.mjs:133`
   `const ceiling = D.diagnosis_type === 'COMPETING_SET' ? 0.70 : 0.90;` → **MATCH** (the R4
   deduction of the 0.91/0.93 verdicts is real, and the agent-side/machine-side cap-gap story is
   genuine).
2. **Seeded random retest draw** (main.tex:535: seed 1003818694, tep_d11) →
   `results/benchmark/retest_selection.json`: rng mulberry32, seed 1003818694, uniform 0.8468,
   pool_size 9, index 7, `tep_d11_reactor_cooling_random` → **MATCH**.
3. **"Human engineer signs off" / escalation interface** (main.tex:589) →
   `.claude/skills/industrial-analysis-auto/SKILL.md:288,309` (CP-3 accepts `AUTO_RESOLVED`; fast
   path "jump directly to Step 3... do not enter hub wait") and grep of `app/backend/src` for
   sign-off/approval mechanisms → **MISMATCH in spirit**: the paper's positioning language
   ("is positioned as decision support") is accurate, but the escalation interface has no software
   embodiment and no human ever participated in the 13 recorded executions. The paper never states
   this.
4. **Thirteen recorded executions** (main.tex:136, 361) → `workspace/diagnostic-runs/` contains
   exactly 13 run directories; `results/benchmark/consistency_audit.json` shows most cases at
   `INSUFFICIENT-RUNS` with one v2-era pair → **MATCH** (the 1/1 stability honesty is real).
5. **Cost/token accounting** (implicit in main.tex:574 "orders of magnitude more expensive") → no
   token, cost, or energy fields in `results/benchmark/metrics.json`, the grading records, or any
   benchmark artifact I searched → **MATCH with the critique**: the claim is unquantified because
   the quantity was never computed.
6. **Single-vendor execution of a 14-engine architecture** (main.tex:361) →
   `results/benchmark/report.md:98` records the same GLM deployment / ZCode CLI harness for
   baselines and pipeline → **MATCH** with the paper's own disclosure; the sovereignty/portability
   section (main.tex:583) is accordingly design-level only, as the paper partially concedes
   ("Projected", main.tex:577).

## 7. Recommendation

**Minor Revision** — conditioned on the additive analyses in issues 1–3 and the tempering/ disclosure
fixes in issues 4–8. Nothing I found invalidates a headline claim; every must-fix is either a text
change or an analysis computable from the released logs. If the authors decline the cost/token/energy
accounting (issue 1) and the cap-set alignment (issue 3), this should be treated as Major Revision,
because the paper's central trade-off (resolution for auditability) and its deployment-governance
story would then rest on unquantified assertions.

### Numbered issues

1. **[Must-fix — Cost/Energy] Add a per-scenario resource-accounting table (tokens, wall-clock,
   estimated energy, and — if available — API cost) for: full pipeline, bare single-call LLM, and
   PCA baseline.**
   Location: Section 6.5 "Cost" (main.tex:574) and Section 6.3/Table 8 (ablation). The data exist
   in the released event logs; the bare-call multiplier is the number that makes the paper's central
   trade-off ("we give up resolution to gain an auditable process") decidable by a practitioner. Add
   one energy paragraph (token totals × published per-token energy factors) for the multi-agent
   footprint; AEI readers in 2026 should not have to guess the environmental cost of gated
   redundancy (judge + two audits + HTML build/review). Also state the worst-case run cost including
   repair rounds (IDV3's one repair round is the natural datum).

2. **[Must-fix — Economics] Mark the ontology-amortisation economics as projected, or measure it.**
   Location: main.tex:589 ("The marginal cost of a diagnosis therefore falls as the fleet grows").
   The benchmark pinned full rebuilds (main.tex:138), so this claim has no measurement behind it in
   the corpus. Cheapest fix: run the fast-reuse path on one sibling pair (e.g., the two SKAB
   scenarios) and report the time delta in a sentence; otherwise move the claim into the
   explicitly-projected register used elsewhere in Section 6.1.

3. **[Must-fix — Governance] Align the cap sets and declare which governs deployment.**
   Location: main.tex:226, 456–457, Table 4 caption context; enforcing code
   `scripts/benchmark/judge-rubric.mjs:133`. Add the flat 0.90 non-CS ceiling to the agent-side
   protocol (a one-line protocol edit) or state explicitly that the machine gate's 0.90 is
   authoritative in production and that the two corpus violations (0.91, 0.93) would be hard-blocked
   post-fix. As written, a deployment could legally emit 0.93-confidence verdicts that the system's
   own grading gate rejects.

4. **[Must-fix — Practical impact] Correct the integration-cost overclaim.**
   Location: main.tex:581 ("a deployment is an export job plus one API call rather than an
   instrumentation project"). Replace with an honest statement of what integration actually
   involves (tag mapping, unit/sampling alignment, historian export hygiene) — ideally quantified
   with the film-line engagement's engineering effort. This single sentence is the most likely one
   to destroy credibility with practising readers.

5. **[Must-fix — Trust/HITL] Disclose that no human was in the loop in any recorded execution, and
   that the sign-off step has no software embodiment.**
   Location: main.tex:589 ("a human engineer signs off") vs `SKILL.md:288` (CP-3
   `AUTO_RESOLVED` fast path bypasses the hub wait) and the absence of any approval artifact in the
   14-item contract (Table 5) or sign-off code in the backend. Add two or three sentences: the human
   role in this corpus was entirely outside the system; CP-3's default path is machine-resolved; a
   deployment-grade sign-off step (e.g., an `engineer_endorsement.json` artifact and a pending
   state) is specified but not yet exercised. The claim can stand — but only with this disclosure.

6. **[Should-fix — Trust] Report the confidence semantics to the reader as protocol confidence, not
   probability.**
   Location: main.tex:456 (calibration admits circularity) and the report/HTML deliverable
   description. Until the pre-registered repeat study (k≥3) exists, a "0.91" read by an engineer as
   P(correct) is unsupported. Recommend the paper commit to labelling confidence in the report
   template (e.g., "protocol confidence: bounded by the weakest evidence level; not an outcome
   frequency") and to surfacing falsification conditions beside the number.

7. **[Should-fix — Gate sensitivity] Add (or schedule as pre-registered) a mutation test of the
   audit plane.**
   Location: Section 3.4/5 (audit infrastructure and rubric). Inject N subtly defective diagnosis
   sets (unfounded elimination, swapped evidence citation, confidence above ceiling, correlation
   cited despite failing the anti-spurious filter) and report the gates' detection rate. This is the
   missing measurement behind "the gates are a check rather than a claim" (main.tex:270) and is
   itself a reusable contribution; it can run on the deterministic graders alone at near-zero cost
   for the schema/ceiling/grounding subset.

8. **[Should-fix — Over-reliance] Carry Lee & See into the deployment section.**
   Location: Section 6.1/6.2 (main.tex:576–591). Add a short paragraph on automation-bias risk: the
   deliverable profile (polished DET report, ENDORSED stamp, PASS gates, high confidence) is
   optimised for credibility and could induce rubber-stamping; state how the report surfaces
   "what would change this verdict" (falsification/revival conditions already exist as
   machine-checked artifacts) and how the escalation UI should present `COMPETING_SET` as a
   first-class outcome rather than a failure.

9. **[Should-fix — Security/data governance] Add a data-governance paragraph for the
   sovereignty-sensitive deployments the paper targets.**
   Location: main.tex:583 (data-sovereign deployment). An agentic CLI with code execution, plus a
   RAG engine that includes web search, raises egress and sandboxing questions that
   defence/pharma/semiconductor readers will ask first. State the egress policy of a sovereign
   deployment (web search disabled, model local, agent sandbox), and note that in the benchmark the
   RAG channel never succeeded anyway (main.tex:136), so the corpus is already effectively
   offline-consistent.

10. **[Should-fix — Lock-in] Temper portability/sovereignty to the evidence, or run a smoke test.**
    Location: main.tex:583 and Section 3.2 (main.tex:210, "engine substitution remains a designed
    but not-yet-exercised ablation axis"). All 13 runs are one engine + one model family +
    one snapshot (verified, `results/benchmark/report.md:98`); cross-engine verdict stability is
    unknown, and a silent provider model update is itself a system change the paper acknowledges but
    does not measure. Minimum viable fix: one scenario re-run on a second engine/model as a
    smoke test (the mock-engine CI path shows the harness supports this), or explicit "design-level
    only" labelling of the sovereignty paragraph (the "Projected" register at main.tex:577 should
    extend to it).

11. **[Optional — Impact] Add a "minimal viable gate set" discussion.**
    Location: end of Section 7. With the filter-disabled and engine-substitution arms unexecuted,
    the practitioner cannot tell which of the nine stages carry the audit value. Name the plausible
    minimal subset (truth isolation, completeness gate, evidence-level ceiling, one independent
    auditor) and what evidence would justify the rest — this converts the paper from a monolithic
    system description into a adoptable methodology ladder.

### Minor/editorial

- The abstract (main.tex:56) is 400+ words and carries the entire ablation; a practitioner will not
  find the 9/9-vs-4/9 disclosure in a scan. Consider a 150-word abstract with the trade-off as the
  closing sentence.
- The film-line case would gain from one sanitised table (episode span, parameter count, channels
  present, post-verdict action), clearly marked as non-releasable-in-full.
