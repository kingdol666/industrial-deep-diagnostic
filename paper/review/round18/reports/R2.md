## Summary

The paper presents IDD, a nine-stage agentic root-cause-analysis pipeline (ontology → deterministic statistics → competing-hypotheses reasoning → dual audit → gated report), built around (i) a three-state verdict protocol (DETERMINED / COMPETING_SET / NEEDS_DATA) with arithmetic confidence ceilings, (ii) L1–L7 evidence grading with weakest-link propagation, (iii) a four-condition anti-spurious-correlation filter, and (iv) a machine-checked evaluation framework (truth isolation, fingerprinting, stage-authored provenance-labelled artifacts, execution proofs). It is evaluated on a 12-scenario corpus (SKAB ×3, TEP ×7, IndPenSim ×2) and compared per-fault against FaultExplainer under an explicitly declared two-register comparison protocol, with a same-model ablation showing the bare LLM (9/9 strict keyword hits) beats the pipeline (4/9 resolved) — a commendably honest negative.

I verified the literature-facing claims against primary sources (arXiv full texts), the scenario ground truths against the released case definitions, and the headline methodological claims against released run artifacts. The related-work positioning is accurate and current — this is rare. The main problems are: a transparency gap on the IndPenSim batch-93 scenario (one of the four Top-1 wins rests partly on a generic keyword), a self-description inconsistency around the word "calibrated", missing positioning against the active-fault-diagnosis literature (which anticipates the paper's "named discriminating measurement" contribution), and a handful of precision fixes.

## 1. Related-work positioning and the A/B comparison registers

**FaultExplainer.** I checked the manuscript's transcription against the arXiv full text (arXiv:2412.14492). All load-bearing facts verify: FE's PCA implementation marks IDV3/4/9/15 as undetectable and excludes them from scoring (11 scoreable faults); in the root-causes-included regime GPT-4o scores 7/11 and o1-preview 9/11 with top-3 alias-accepted answers; alias classes are IDV1/2/8, IDV3↔IDV9, IDV4↔IDV11↔IDV14, IDV5↔IDV12↔IDV15; the General-Reasoning (no-candidate) regime reports 8/11 for both models under lenient "correct-or-related" grading. The manuscript's caveat that FE's own text names faults 10 and 13 as failing while reporting 8/11 is a real tension in the source and is handled with appropriate hedging ("we transcribe the reported figure"). The three declared protocol differences (top-3-with-alias vs strict single verdict; candidates in prompt vs none; PCA-excluded faults scored by IDD) are stated fairly and repeated in Register A, Appendix Table A1, and Fig. 8.

**AgentRCA.** Verified against arXiv:2607.22385 (Wei & Fink). The quoted numbers (40.0% Top-1, 61.5% Top-2, zero-shot, TEP) are exact; the output is indeed a ranked hypothesis table without calibrated confidence; uncertainty communication is indeed named as a future direction; the evaluation indeed stops at ground-truth-label comparison with no external harness. One imprecision: the manuscript says "ten TEP faults---excluding IDV3/9/15", but AgentRCA also excludes IDV16–20 because their physical causes are unspecified (and its scenario pool differs). A TEP-literate reader computing 20−3=17 will stumble; state both exclusions.

**Causal RCA lineage.** Iri et al. (1979) as the origin of signed-directed-graph mechanism-chain elimination, Yuan & Qin (2014) Granger-causal plant-wide oscillation localisation, and Bauer et al. (2007) transfer-entropy propagation direction are all correctly characterised. The AEI-adjacent coverage (CausalKGPT, FD-LLM, Wen et al., Xu et al., Liu et al. spatial-temporal causality graph, Yue et al., label-free evaluation) is appropriate and current.

**Automation/trust framing (§2.3).** Parasuraman et al. (2000) types/levels of automation and Lee & See (2004) appropriate reliance are the right anchors for reading the three-state verdict as calibrated transfer; Heuer's ACH, Hill's causation criteria, and Chow's reject trade-off are correctly assigned as ancestors. I note two gaps: (a) the claim that the L1–L7 grounding check "doubles as a faithfulness proxy in the sense of XAI evaluation" carries **no citation**; (b) anchoring abstention solely in Chow (1970) ignores the modern selective-prediction/conformal-prediction literature that an AEI audience will expect for "calibrated uncertainty" claims.

**Benchmark-methodology critique.** The Vieira et al. figure verifies exactly: "elevating the Macro AUROC by 7.3% and 15.3%" under condition- and segmentation-level leakage (arXiv:2509.22267), and the entity-level-split recommendation matches. Hendriks et al. (2022) is the correct lineage citation.

**Internal consistency of Section 2 and Table 1.** With one exception the table is defensible cell by cell. The exception: Table 1 marks IDD "Calib. = **yes**" while §5.3 states that ceiling compliance is "circular by construction" and reports two verdicts (0.91, 0.93) exceeding the machine gate's flat 0.90 ceiling, and the Discussion confirms no psychometric calibration study exists. The paper's own terminology ("ceiling compliance, not calibration") is the honest one; the positioning table and parts of the abstract ("calibrated shortlist", "calibrated uncertainty") drift back to the stronger word. This must be unified.

**Register fairness.** Register A/B separation is respected in both directions: Register B numbers (TEP classification accuracies, BeatGAN F1, SKAB ensemble, leakage-controlled AUROC) appear only as context and are excluded from the ranking figures; the forest plot carries FE/IDD/controls only, with the differing-set caveat printed. The comparison's asymmetries (base models uncontrolled; FE protocol cheaper; FE-official arm on the same GLM scoring 5/6 vs IDD's 3/6 Top-1) are disclosed prominently rather than buried. I judge the registers fairly used.

## 2. Technical soundness of the TEP / SKAB / IndPenSim usage

**TEP fault mechanisms (IDV1/3/4/7/11/14).** All six descriptions match the canonical Downs–Vogel / Chiang et al. cause table: IDV1 A/C feed-ratio step with B composition constant (Stream 4); IDV3 D-feed temperature step (Stream 2); IDV4 reactor cooling-water inlet temperature step; IDV7 C-header pressure loss / reduced availability (Stream 4); IDV11 reactor cooling-water inlet temperature random variation; IDV14 reactor cooling-water valve stiction. The detectability framing (IDV3/9/15 at the PCA detection limit; IDV4 weakly detected by T² and reliably by SPE) matches the classical studies and is independently reproduced by the paper's own PCA arm (IDV3 T² 2.9%/SPE 4.8%; IDV4 SPE 100% vs T² 48.8%). The released case definitions (`scripts/benchmark/cases/benchmark_cases.json`) agree with the paper's descriptions on all six.

Two quantitative chains deserve explicit engineering endorsement because they are the paper's best evidence of statistics-driven (rather than label-recalled) reasoning:

- *IDV7 (DETERMINED, 0.78).* XMV(4) opening +25.1% (z=12.4) at constant C-feed flow implies a valve-gain loss of −20.1%, and constant flow through a reduced gain requires ΔP_new/ΔP_old ≈ (0.799)² ≈ 0.64 — exactly the recorded "ΔP ratio 0.64". This is consistent with flow ∝ Cv·√ΔP and with the documented header-supply-loss mechanism.
- *IDV4 case study.* The energy-balance necessity argument is algebraically sound (constant reactor duty with a rising coolant flow requires reduced (T_out − T_in), i.e. an inlet-temperature step or an UA loss), and the quantitative elimination of the UA sub-case (a pre-existing approach < 1.5 K would force a coolant inlet ≈ 93 °C against the observed ≈ 26 K approach) is a legitimate quantitative falsification, not narrative.

**SKAB.** The valve1 scenario (inlet/suction-side valve throttling) and the cavitation case drawn from the "other" group are consistent with the dataset's documentation (valve 1 sits at the flow inlet to the pump; cavitation anomalies belong to the "other" experiments). The blind design (label withheld from all agent-visible brief content) is sound, and the residual disclosure — the case id itself embeds the English fault word — is candid. The pipeline's blind top-ranked *family* ("cavitation-type") is a genuinely blind hit.

**IndPenSim batch 93 — the weakest scenario description in the paper.** Batch 93 is within the documented faulted/deviation range (batches 91–100 carry simulated faults resulting in process deviations, per Goldrick et al.), so the scenario is legitimate. But unlike the TEP and SKAB scenarios, the paper **never states what the documented deviation of batch 93 is** (§5.1 says only "one documented process-deviation batch (93)"). Meanwhile: (a) the released case truth (`benchmark_cases.json`, case `indpensim_batch093`) describes the deviation as actuator-side pH/temperature-loop limit violations (acid-flow and heating-water actuators) driving pH and substrate concentration out of band; (b) the paper's decisive signature for the DET verdict (Table 5) is a *cooling-water* loop pinned at 2.0 vs 200–700 L/h → 301.4 K. These may be two views of one causal chain (coolant pinch → temperature excursion → pH/heating actuators saturate), but the reader cannot tell whether the verdict names the documented fault or one of its consequences. Compounding this, the released grading shows the keyword hits that certify Top-1 for this scenario are `['加热','执行器','偏差']` — the last of which is the generic token "deviation" the paper itself twice flags as non-mechanism-bearing. One of the four Top-1 wins is therefore not certified by a mechanism-family keyword. The paper discloses the generic token but still counts the scenario in the 4/9 headline. This is my strongest domain objection, and it is fixable in text (see Issues 1–2).

## 3. Is the methodology a genuine contribution or rebranding?

Judged strictly, as asked. The epistemic core — competing hypotheses with elimination (Heuer's ACH), causation criteria (Hill), a reject/abstain option (Chow), level-of-automation escalation (Parasuraman; Lee & See) — is known practice, and §2.3 says so explicitly ("a domain-specific, machine-checked instantiation of these frameworks inside an agentic pipeline---not their invention"). I accept that framing. What is genuinely new is *enforcement*: schema-validated stage artifacts, arithmetic confidence ceilings that a grading gate recomputes, an elimination contract with per-elimination evidence citation, provenance labels, execution proofs, and a completeness gate that makes fabricated runs ungradable. As an engineering-informatics contribution — converting a diagnostic epistemology into machine-checkable contracts — this clears the bar. Four refinements are required for the claim structure to be airtight:

1. **"Elimination is quantitative" overstates.** The rule is |X_i| > |E_i| "under the evaluator's weighting" (§3.1, Eq. context; §3.2) — the weighting is never defined in the paper. Either formalise it (weights, scale, threshold) or reword to "structured, evidence-cited elimination with machine-checked lower bounds (≥2 eliminations, exclusion-confidence floor 0.90)".
2. **The "named discriminating measurement" is the paper's "most transferable finding" (§6.5), but it has a name in the literature**: it is the passive counterpart of *active fault diagnosis* (test-input/experiment design to separate fault hypotheses). The contribution survives — IDD recommends measurements *post hoc* from passively recorded data, which no active-FD work does in this agentic setting — but the positioning must be made explicitly (see Issue 3).
3. **L1–L7 needs anchoring or argument.** The seven-level scheme with weakest-link propagation is sensible but asserted; it resembles intelligence-community source-reliability/credibility grading and evidence-hierarchy instruments (e.g. GRADE/CEBM). At minimum, cite an analogue and justify the two contestable orderings (L4 visual below L3 statistics — defensible for process data, but currently unargued; L7 as a level rather than the absence of evidence).
4. **Calibration language discipline** (as in §1 above): "ceiling compliance" where the paper means cap enforcement; "calibration" only where an outcome-based study exists (it does not; §5.3 concedes this).

With those amendments I would describe the contribution as: a machine-checked, gate-propagated instantiation of known diagnostic epistemology, plus an evaluation contract, plus an unusually honest cost statement (the ablation). That is publishable; overselling it would not be.

## 4. The five capped COMPETING_SET verdicts

I read the five released diagnoses (workspace run directories listed in Spot-check 3) and the grading records. Findings:

- **Artifact match.** All five carry an explicit discriminability matrix and a `discriminating_data_needed` field; the paper's named measurements are accurate transcriptions, not embellishments. Verdict types and confidences match Table 5 exactly (0.60/0.60/0.55/0.63/0.60; top-k true in all five; the valve1 verdict-type miss is exactly the released rubric's R4 detail: type COMPETING_SET not in allowed {DETERMINED}).
- **Engineering sense.** The named measurements are the right ones in each case: (i) SKAB valve1 — dual ≤0.01-bar suction/discharge pressure for head direction (the expected ~0.025-bar shift is genuinely buried under the recorded 0.327-bar quantisation) plus valve-position/command feedback: this is precisely what separates downstream throttling from upstream supply restriction; (ii) SKAB cavitation — suction pressure/NPSHa plus ≥100 Hz pressure-pulsation or acoustic-emission spectra to separate broadband bubble-collapse from low-frequency valve–pipeline self-oscillation, with tank level/temperature for vapour-pressure inputs: textbook cavitation diagnostics; (iii) IDV3 — steam-header pressure/dryness plus D/E feed-preheater outlet (or stream-10 enthalpy) measurements, which is exactly the pair that would decide the steam-side-vs-feed-side fork in which the documented D-feed-temperature cause lives; the added active test (manual steam-valve step, comparing the temperature-loop gain against calibration) is a nice touch; (iv) IDV4/IDV11 — the reactor cooling-water inlet thermocouple and UA/LMTD monitoring, which are literally the two unmeasured quantities of the fork, and IDV4's documented cause *is* the inlet-temperature step, so the named measurement is exactly right.
- **Nuance the paper should disclose.** In two of the five cases the *top-ranked* survivor is not the documented truth: skab_valve1 ranks downstream throttling (0.65) above the true suction-side restriction (0.60), and tep_d03 ranks the steam-side branch above the feed-side family that contains the D-feed-temperature cause. The paper's claims are technically accurate ("ranked among the survivors", "true mechanism ranked in all five"), but the abstract's "carried in a calibrated shortlist" would be materially stronger — and more honest — with per-verdict disclosure of the truth's rank within the shortlist. A deployment triaging on the head of the shortlist would act wrongly in 2 of 5 capped cases. This is a one-column addition, not a change of conclusions.

## 5. Missing literature an AEI reviewer would demand

1. **Active fault diagnosis** (e.g. Scott, Raimondo, Braatz, *J. Process Control* 2013; Punčochář et al. 2011, or a survey) — mandatory for Issue 3; the discriminating-measurement recommendation must be positioned against it.
2. **Venkatasubramanian, Rengaswamy, Kavuri & Yin (2003), three-part review in *J. Process Control*** — the canonical data-driven / analytical / knowledge-based FDD taxonomy. The Introduction's "three obstacles" narrative should be situated in it; its absence from a process-monitoring paper in 2026 is conspicuous.
3. **One model-based FDD anchor** (Isermann 2005, or Frank 1990, or Gertler) — §2.1 currently jumps from PCA to deep detectors as if statistical data-driven methods were the whole classical field.
4. **Modern selective prediction / conformal prediction** (Vovk et al.; Angelopoulos & Bates 2023 survey; or Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017) beside Chow (1970) — the abstention and "calibrated uncertainty" framing needs the current literature, especially since the paper itself disclaims outcome-based calibration.
5. **An XAI-faithfulness citation** for the §2.3 faithfulness-proxy claim (currently uncited).
6. *(Optional but valuable)* **One LLM-RCA/AIOps agent reference** beyond AgentRCA (the hypothesis-table agent line in software-system RCA) to harden the "none combines (i)–(iv)" claim, and/or an evidence-grading analogue (NATO admiralty-style source-reliability grading, or GRADE) to anchor L1–L7.

## Recommendation

**Minor Revision.** All load-bearing literature claims verify against primary sources; the scenario mechanics and the five capped verdicts are technically sound and faithfully transcribed from the released artifacts; the registers are used fairly. The required changes are textual and analytic-framing changes, not new experiments — with the single caveat that Issue 1 (IndPenSim transparency) must be resolved in a way that may slightly restate the headline (Top-1 with and without the generic-token scenario).
