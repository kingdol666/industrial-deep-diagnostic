## 1. Overall impression

The paper presents IDD, a nine-stage multi-agent diagnosis pipeline with evidence grading (L1-L7),
a three-state verdict protocol, and a machine-checked 12-scenario benchmark over SKAB/TEP/IndPenSim,
compared against FaultExplainer per-fault outcomes, a same-model bare-LLM ablation, and a classical
PCA baseline. The defining feature of the manuscript — and the reason it survives skeptical scrutiny —
is that its most damaging result is stated in the abstract itself: a bare single call to the same
model resolves 9/9 documented faults versus the pipeline's 6/9, so the pipeline's claimed margins
are auditability, calibrated uncertainty, and gated process guarantees, not accuracy. Every
headline number I checked is qualified in place (5/9 pre-revision, 4/6 TEP pre-revision appear in
the abstract, contributions, framework, results, conclusion, table captions and figure captions).

I attempted to break the paper's factual claims against the released tree and failed to find a
single mismatch. Thirteen spot checks (below) all matched, including two against code and several
against raw event journals and git history. This is, as far as I can verify, an honest paper.

## 4. Novelty

Positioning against FaultExplainer is fair and specific (protocol properties (i)-(iii), alias
policy, PCA-excluded faults), and the FE transcription is flagged where FE's own text is
internally inconsistent (8/11 vs named failures). Contemporaneous AgentRCA is cited and
differentiated (ranked list, no calibrated confidence, no truth-comparison harness). Section 2.4
honestly assigns intellectual ancestry (ACH, Hill, Chow, Parasuraman, Lee & See, OntoCAPE) and
claims only a machine-checked domain instantiation. Table 1's "IDD only" row is hedged to the
cited systems. For an applied venue this is an acceptable novelty posture; the real contribution
is the evaluation contract plus the demonstration that protocol discipline converts accuracy into
calibrated abstention — the paper says exactly this.

## 8. Weaknesses (none factual; ordered by importance)

1. Evidence base is one model family on one harness (ZCode CLI driver, GLM, Sept 2026); the
   14-engine substitution axis — a headline architectural property — is designed but never
   exercised. Disclosed twice; still the largest gap between architectural promise and evidence.
2. Author-designed keywords/rubric/allowed-sets measure conformance to the authors' own protocol;
   the 5/9↔6/9 swing rests on one synonym. The sensitivity addendum mitigates; independent expert
   anchoring remains outstanding (acknowledged).
3. "15/15 within-version agreement" mixes 12 pre-discipline-era pairs with 3 current-era pairs;
   the sentence immediately decomposes it, but a skimming reader could over-read the headline.
4. C1's chronology prose carries dual timezones in every item; correct but hard to hold in mind.
5. n=9 with single executions gives a wide CI ([35.4,87.9]) — unavoidable and plainly stated;
   the repeat study is pre-registered but largely unexecuted (current era k=2 for 3 pairs, one
   drawn scenario INSUFFICIENT-RUNS).
6. Length/density: 74 pp; abstract at the 250-word ceiling; Section 5(C1) and Section 8.4 are the
   densest passages and would benefit from tables.

## 9. Verdict

No factual mismatch found in 13 checks spanning numbers, artifact paths, event journals, git
history, and backend code. The paper's promises are delivered with unusual candour about its own
weaknesses. Remaining issues are presentational (chronology table, stability-headline phrasing,
length) plus the inherent single-model/single-execution limits that are already disclosed and
pre-registered for follow-up. I sign a favourable report: minor revision.
