## 10. Findings

### Must-fix (blocking)
None. Every quantitative claim I checked (7 Wilson CIs, 5 chronology timestamps, 2
recomputed keyword scenarios, rubric/judge/stability/baseline/determinism aggregates,
repair counts, caps) reproduced from the released artifacts.

### Should-fix (non-blocking)
1. §4.1 (p.~13): one clause to reconcile "nd carries no protocol cap" with the R4 gate's
   `type === 'COMPETING_SET' ? 0.70 : 0.90`, which also caps NEEDS_DATA at 0.90
   (judge-rubric.mjs ~L133). No benchmark impact (no NEEDS_DATA scenario).
2. §6.2 Metrics (p.~16): state z=1.96 once in the metrics text itself, not only in
   Table 6's caption and the figure code.
3. Fig 7 (p.46): x-axis runs to ~137% to fit interval labels while ticks stop at 100;
   either clip the axis at ~105 and place labels inside, or note the extension.
4. §8.5 stability paragraph (p.~26) packs 15/15, era semantics, seeded draw, and the
   insufficient-runs gap into one paragraph; a small table (or splitting) would help
   readability.
5. Table 6 row "Honest capped COMPETING_SET verdicts 3/9" mixes an interpreted label
   into a numeric table; consider a neutral label ("capped verdicts ranking truth first:
   2/3") or move to text.

### Comments (not actions)
- The keyword-sensitivity addendum + journaled chronology is a model for how grading-key
  revisions should be documented; the "one uniform application, not per scenario after
  seeing results" claim is genuinely supported by the journal pattern (superseded
  old-set grading minutes before the switch completes, calibrated batch three minutes
  later, commit formalizing the same day).
- The pipeline-internal judge mean (93.1) and rubric (97.9) are properly demoted to
  non-metrics; the paper never rests a correctness claim on them.
- Statistically, the paper knows exactly how weak its evidence is (n=9, n=3 controls,
  single executions) and says so in the right places; the defensible core is the audit
  machinery, and the paper now argues exactly that.
