# Review R2 — LLM-based Industrial Fault Diagnosis (Blind Review)

Manuscript: "Trustworthy agentic diagnosis for industrial processes: evidence-graded competing-hypothesis reasoning evaluated against per-fault literature baselines" (main.tex, 46 pp. render).
Reviewer focus: related-work coverage, citation accuracy, novelty, TEP/SKAB/IndPenSim soundness, deployment realism.

## Paper Summary

The paper presents IDD, a nine-stage multi-agent LLM diagnosis pipeline (ontology, deterministic statistics, competing-hypothesis reasoning with L1–L7 evidence grading and confidence caps, physical verification, bounded repair gates) evaluated on a 12-scenario benchmark from SKAB, Tennessee Eastman Process, and IndPenSim. On single recorded executions it resolves 4/9 documented faults to a single root cause (Top-1 44.4%), returns capped COMPETING_SET verdicts naming the discriminating measurement in the other five, and raises zero false alarms on three controls; a model-controlled ablation shows a bare single call to the same model family scores 9/9, so the claimed margins are auditability, capped uncertainty, and gated process rather than accuracy. The evaluation contribution is a machine-checked contract (truth isolation, data fingerprinting, stage-authored artifacts, gate-graded scoring, artifact-consistency verification), validated internally on the released corpus plus one seeded random-draw re-execution.

## Major Issues

1. **[§ Discussion, "Prompt-knowledge contamination"; Abstract] The identifier-recall channel is left unresolved, and the joint worst case is never stated — this caps the credibility of the headline accuracy numbers.** The authors disclose that run identifiers literally name the answer (`tep_d14_reactor_valve_sticking` names mechanism and dynamic form), and state that "all four TEP-derived outcomes—including the resolved IDV1/IDV7/IDV14—are recall-possible until the pre-registered neutralised-identifier re-run is executed." I verified the disclosure chain in the text; the mitigation offered (form-discrimination across IDV4/11/14, § Results "Per-Mechanism Breakdown") is genuinely good evidence for the *capped* verdicts, but it cannot rescue the three resolved TEP verdicts, because under identifier recall the model gets mechanism *and* form without reading the data — and the authors' own ablation (Table, ablation) shows the bare model "plausibly by literature recall" resolving IDV3. The joint worst case is never written down: combining the identifier-recall channel (all four TEP outcomes recall-possible) with the identity-strict discount of batch 93 gives a floor of 0–1/9 resolved, versus the headline 4/9. **Fix:** (i) execute the neutralised-identifier re-run *during this revision* — it is the single cheapest experiment that de-risks the paper's central claim, and "pre-registered" is not a substitute for performed when the infrastructure demonstrably exists (one full pipeline run ≈ 2 h, § Cost); (ii) state the joint worst case explicitly in § Results "Overall Performance" alongside the 3/9 identity-strict count.

2. **[§ Comparison "Verdict Stability", § Discussion "Statistical power"] The stability machinery — a headline contribution of the evaluation framework — is validated on exactly one pair (1/1), and all repeat studies the paper itself pre-registers remain unexecuted.** Section "Verdict Stability" honestly says "One consistent pair is the beginning of a stability study, not its end," yet the abstract reports "A seeded random draw selected one fault scenario for independent re-execution, reproducing the verdict state, actuator, and mechanism class within two confidence points" as a load-bearing result. For a paper whose thesis is evaluation rigor, n=1 consistency evidence is the weakest link, and the cost is modest and quantified by the authors themselves (≈2.0 h/scenario; ≈1.9 h median agent span). **Fix:** execute at least k=3 repeats of the diagnosis stage for all nine fault scenarios (full pipeline for a subset) and report verdict-state agreement rates and confidence spreads; if not feasible, demote the stability sentence in the abstract to the same status as the pre-registration note.

3. **[§ Framework, contract C1; § Setup "Metrics"] The grading-keyword instrument was calibrated with knowledge of baseline outcomes and of a "v1-era corpus" that the paper never defines — a provenance circularity that touches every scored number.** C1 states the keyword sets were "calibrated against the v1-era corpus and the published per-fault baselines, then frozen; ... commit 221d061." Two problems: (a) "the v1-era corpus" appears nowhere else in the manuscript — a reader cannot know what was calibrated, against what distribution of verdicts, or why that is not a leakage of expected outcomes into the instrument; (b) calibrating mechanism synonyms against the *published per-fault baselines'* correctness sets injects baseline knowledge into the very keys used to score the IDD-vs-FaultExplainer comparison of § Comparison "Register A". The paper repeatedly and honestly labels keys "author-designed" and discloses the generic token ("deviation"), but never analyzes the *direction* of the calibration bias (e.g., whether the added synonyms coincide with FE's alias families). **Fix:** disclose the calibration procedure concretely (which baseline outcomes, which tokens removed/added); add a sensitivity analysis — re-score Top-1/top-k under leave-one-synonym-out, and ideally under an independently (blind-)authored keyword set for the TEP subset; define "v1-era corpus" or delete the reference to it.

4. **[§ Related Work "LLM-Based Industrial Diagnosis"; § Architecture §3.2; Table 1] Verified related-work omissions that a Q1 reviewer will flag.** I checked the LLM-for-FDD landscape externally and found: (i) **Alsaif et al. (2024), "Multimodal Large Language Model-Based Fault Detection and Diagnosis in the Context of Industry 4.0," *Sensors* (MDPI)** — a TEP-validated multimodal-LLM FDD framework with on the order of 125 citations, entirely absent; for an AEI paper claiming to survey "LLM-Based Industrial Diagnosis" this is a must-cite. (ii) **Merkelbach et al. (2024), "Using Multi-Modal LLMs to Create Models for Fault Diagnosis"** — same line, relevant to the ontology layer. (iii) **RAG is never cited**: the architecture's "semantic-knowledge layer" is a FastAPI+ChromaDB retrieval-augmented-generation service (§3.2 and Fig. 1 use the acronym "RAG"), yet the retrieval-augmentation literature (Lewis et al., "Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks," NeurIPS 2020) is absent from the 47-entry bibliography. (iv) The claim "among published and contemporaneous systems, none combines ..." is scoped to "systems cited above," but the adjacent agentic-RCA-for-software-systems line (e.g., RCACopilot; mABC; LLM-based microservice RCA, 2023–2025) is not mentioned at all; without a scoping sentence the claim is attackable. **Fix:** add Alsaif et al. 2024 and Merkelbach et al. 2024 to §2.2 (a TEP-relevant row or footnote in Table 1), cite Lewis et al. 2020 at first RAG use in §3.2, and add one sentence scoping the "none combines" claim to industrial *process* diagnosis, acknowledging the microservice-RCA literature.

5. **[§ Related Work, Table 1 (tab:related)] AgentRCA — the paper's own "closest to our setting" system — is missing from the positioning table.** The prose (§2.2) says "Closest to our setting, the contemporaneous AgentRCA (July 2026)..." and I verified the system exists and matches the description (Wei & Fink, arXiv:2607.22385, "Agentic Root Cause Analysis through Evidence-Grounded Reasoning": zero-shot agent that iteratively gathers statistical evidence and evaluates competing hypotheses). Yet Table 1's five rows include FaultExplainer, Gong et al., PCA/Deep FDD, and Causal RCA — but not AgentRCA. The five-property combination claim ("IDD is the only system that combines all five") is doing its heaviest lifting precisely against the row that was omitted. **Fix:** add an AgentRCA row (Input: series; Scenario-level RCA: yes; Evidence trail: yes (reasoning traces); Cap-enforced: no; Repro. gate: no). The claim survives — but it must be made *with* the closest competitor in the table, not only in prose.

6. **[§ Related Work §2.1; § Comparison "Model-Controlled Baselines"; Abstract] No classical RCA baseline on the same recordings, and the "auditability margin" remains construct-unmeasured.** §2.1 credits Granger-causality/transfer-entropy and signed-directed-graph RCA (Yuan & Qin 2014; Bauer et al. 2007; Iri et al. 1979) as the classical scenario-level lineage "that IDD must be read against" — and then never runs any of them on the 12 scenarios. The ablation suite contains PCA detection (T²/SPE), FE-protocol LLM arms, and a bare LLM, but the cheapest falsification test of the paper's framing — does a deterministic Granger/TE or SDG method rank the true mechanism in the surviving set on the same keyword protocol? — is missing. Separately, the abstract asserts the pipeline's margins are "auditability, calibrated uncertainty, and gated process," yet no instrument measures auditability (the rubric R1–R7 measures conformance, as § Setup Metrics admits: "a conformance instrument with limited discriminative power"; the blinded expert adjudication is "outstanding"). **Fix:** add a Granger/TE-based RCA arm (the pipeline's own lag-compensated CCF machinery is most of the implementation) reporting top-k under the same keywords; and either run a small human audit-replay study (can an engineer re-derive each verdict from the 14 released artifacts?) or reword the abstract to say the margins are *architectural properties* rather than measured outcomes.

## Minor Issues

1. **[§ Setup "Benchmark configuration disclosure"; § Comparison "Register A"] Bare `\ref{app:artifacts}` renders as a dangling "A".** main.tex line 367: "...are released with the repository. \ref{app:artifacts} indexes the released per-scenario artifacts." and line 490: "Table~\ref{tab:perfault} (\ref{app:artifacts})" both render as "A indexes..." / "(A)". Write "Appendix~\ref{app:artifacts}" in both places.
2. **[§ Related Work §2.1] The Venkatasubramanian taxonomy is paraphrased incorrectly and only Part I is cited.** The text says "the tripartite quantitative/features/knowledge partition ... ~\cite{venkatasubramanian2003review}", but the cited entry is only Part I (quantitative model-based methods, CCE 27(3):293–311); the tripartite partition (quantitative / qualitative / process-history) spans Parts I–III (2003, same journal). Cite all three parts and use the original labels, or rephrase as "the classical model-based/data-driven partition".
3. **[§ Introduction, second obstacle] "deep classifiers~\cite{tuli2022tranad,pozdnyakov2024adversarial}" is loose.** TranAD (PVLDB 2022) is a deep anomaly *detector*, not a classifier, and the Pozdnyakov et al. entry is an adversarial-robustness benchmark; § Related Work itself correctly says "deep anomaly detectors". Align the introduction's wording.
4. **[§ Related Work §2.1] `tao2019digital` (a general digital-twin survey) is cited for "digital-twin approaches mirror model-based diagnosis"** — a survey-level support for a specific claim. Either cite a DT-for-diagnosis-specific work or soften the clause.
5. **[§ Discussion "Documentation for regulated operations"] GxP, ALCOA+, and GAMP 5 are name-dropped without any reference.** Cite ISPE GAMP 5 (2nd ed., 2022) and an ALCOA+ data-integrity source (e.g., MHRA 'GXP' Data Integrity Guidance, 2018), or remove the proper names.
6. **[Abstract] Length and density.** The abstract runs to ~370 words with parenthetical caveats that duplicate § Results; AEI conventions sit closer to 250. Move the keyword-set and identifier-recall caveats to the body (they are already there, more precisely stated) and keep the abstract's hedges to one clause each.
7. **[§ Related Work §2.2] "the paper's own PCA implementation leaves all four of these faults unscored" — ambiguous antecedent.** On first read "the paper" appears to be this manuscript. Write "FaultExplainer's own PCA implementation" (the Introduction's phrasing, "the published LLM baseline's own PCA implementation", is already unambiguous).
8. **[§ Introduction; § Related Work §2.1] The classical TEP detectability citation set omits Lyman & Georgakis (1995), AIChE J.** — the original plant-wide-control/TEP detection-rate study that the Chiang/Russell/Braatz books compile. Add it next to `russell2000delay,chiang2001fault` for the IDV3/9/15-at-the-limit and IDV4-weak-T² claims.
9. **[Bibliography] Reference count (47) is low for AEI**, and several 2026-dated keys carry year-mismatched names (`agentic2025review` → year 2026; `khan2024faultexplainer` → published 2025, CCE 199:109152 — the entry itself is correct, the key is not visible to readers). Adding the missing lines from Major 4 largely resolves the count; confirm the rendered reference list years match each source.
10. **[§ Setup §6.2; § Comparison §8.1] "by common consent the hardest TEP fault" (IDV3) is an unsupported superlative.** Cite a TEP benchmark study noting IDV3's weak signature, or soften to "commonly regarded".
11. **[§ Results, Table tab:overall] Statistical proofreading.** I recomputed the Wilson intervals at z=1.96: 4/9→[18.9,73.3] ✓, 9/9→[70.1,100.0] ✓, 3/6→[18.8,81.2] ✓, 4/4→[51.0,100] ✓, 8/9→[56.5,98.0] ✓ — but 3/3 computes to [43.85, 100.0], which the table prints as [43.8, 100.0] (rounds to 43.9 under round-half-up; make the rounding convention consistent). Also verify the "15.3 percentage points of Macro-AUROC" figure attributed to Vieira et al. against the source page, as it is quoted to one decimal.
12. **[Figures] Fig. 1 (fig_architecture) and Fig. 2 (fig_pipeline) are HTML-rendered raster exports** (the captions disclose this). They render crisply at screen size in the checked PNGs, but Elsevier print reproduction prefers vector (PDF/SVG) or ≥300 dpi at column width — confirm before acceptance.

## Verdict

**MAJOR REVISION.** The domain execution is sound — I verified the TEP fault facts, PCA detectability framing, FaultExplainer transcription (including its disclosed 8/11 inconsistency), Wilson intervals, rubric arithmetic, and figure/table cross-consistency, and found no errors — and the paper's self-disclosure is well above the field norm; but the headline accuracy claims remain recall-uncertain (Major 1), the stability evidence is n=1 while the machinery to fix it demonstrably exists (Major 2), the grading instrument's provenance is circular as described (Major 3), and the related-work coverage has verified omissions including the closest competing system absent from the positioning table (Majors 4–5). All required work is bounded and mostly executable by the authors' own tooling within a revision cycle, so I do not recommend rejection.
