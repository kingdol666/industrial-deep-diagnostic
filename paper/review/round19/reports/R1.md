The internal consistency of the released corpus is genuinely good. The issues below concern instrument validity and inference weight, not arithmetic.

## Paper Summary

The paper presents IDD, a nine-stage agentic root-cause-diagnosis pipeline (ontology → deterministic statistics → competing-hypotheses reasoning → judge/physical-audit gates → bounded repair → HTML deliverable) with evidence levels L1–L7, confidence ceilings tied to the weakest evidence level, a four-condition anti-spurious-correlation filter, and a three-state verdict protocol (DETERMINED / capped COMPETING_SET / NEEDS_DATA). It is evaluated in single recorded executions on a 12-scenario corpus from SKAB, TEP and IndPenSim under a truth-isolation contract with leakage sentinels and gate-graded scoring, resolving 4/9 documented faults Top-1 (44.4%), ranking the true mechanism in all nine surviving sets (top-k 9/9), and raising zero false alarms on three controls. A model-controlled ablation shows a bare single call to the same GLM-family model over the same blind digest keyword-scores 9/9 — above the pipeline's 4/9 — which the authors use to bound their accuracy claims to "process quality, not accuracy"; a seeded random draw selected TEP IDV11 for full re-execution (verdict state, actuator XMV_10 and mechanism class reproduced, Δconfidence 0.02), and the deterministic baseline suite reproduced all 45 output files byte-for-byte.

## Major Issues

**M1. Truth isolation is broken by the identifier channel, and the one control that would repair the headline claims was deferred instead of executed. (Sections 6.1, 9 "Prompt-knowledge contamination", abstract p.1; released run-dir names in metrics.json)**
The paper discloses that every TEP case id and run-directory name embeds the documented mechanism and dynamic form (e.g., `tep_d14_reactor_valve_sticking`), and Section 3.1 states the orchestrator dispatches every stage by passing absolute paths (`SKILL_PATH`, `RUN_DIR`) — so the answer is visible to every sub-agent in every session. The leakage sentinel (C1) checks briefs and user-context files but not the directory name the agents are given. The consequence is stated in Section 9: all four TEP-derived outcomes (Top-1 hits IDV1, IDV7, IDV14; 3 of the 4 resolved verdicts) are "recall-possible until the pre-registered neutralised-identifier re-run is executed". The same channel applies to the baseline arms (the bare LLM answers literally print "IDV(14): …"), which the paper notes cuts both ways. Under the worst case the headline Top-1 could be 1/9 (batch-93 already discounted identity-strict) rather than 4/9. This control costs ≈2 h per scenario (the paper's own figure) — roughly 12 h of agent time for the six TEP scenarios — and was known before submission, yet it is left as a pre-registration. A Q1 reviewer cannot be expected to accept "trustworthy diagnosis" headline numbers whose decisive control is deferred.
*Fix:* Execute the neutralised-identifier re-run (opaque hash run-dir ids, identifier-stripped brief headers) for the six TEP scenarios before publication and report both identifier-exposed and identifier-neutralised Top-1/top-k in Table 6; failing that, move all identifier-affected outcomes into a visibly separated "recall-possible" tier in the abstract, Table 6, and Fig. 4, and retitle the headline accordingly.

**M2. The grading instrument was calibrated with ground-truth knowledge on (prior executions of) the same scenarios it now grades; the released keyword sets confirm family-level granularity coarse enough to score a wrong-actuator verdict as a Top-1 hit. (Sections 5 C1, 6.2 Metrics; benchmark_report_en.md Appendix A; journal.jsonl kw_hits)**
Section 5 C1 states the frozen keyword set (commit 221d061) was "calibrated against the v1-era corpus and the published per-fault baselines" — i.e., tuned with truth knowledge on these very scenarios before re-grading the v2 corpus. Appendix A shows what that calibration produced: batch-93's set contains `执行器` (actuator), `加热` (heating) and `偏差` (deviation); the journal records that the pipeline's DETERMINED verdict — which pins the *cooling*-water actuator while the documented deviation names the acid-dosing and heating-water flows — scored Top-1 via exactly those three broad tokens. The paper discloses the generic token and discounts Top-1 to 3/9 identity-strict, but it does not confront the systemic implication: an instrument tuned on the test corpus, with tokens at this granularity, makes Top-1 = 4/9 an instrument-dependent estimate and makes top-k = 9/9 (mandatory ≥3 broad hypotheses + these keyword sets + the identifier channel of M1) close to unfalsifiable. One sentence ("one scenario's set includes a generic token") understates a structural property.
*Fix:* Report Table 6's Top-1/top-k under three instruments side by side: (i) the frozen calibrated set, (ii) the pre-calibration set (releasable from the edit history the paper says exists), and (iii) an independently authored set written from the documented truth only (e.g., FE's alias classes). Alternatively, report only identity-strict scoring as primary and family-level scoring as sensitivity — and state explicitly in 6.2 that the calibrated instrument was tuned on prior runs of the same cases.

**M3. The identity-strict reading is applied asymmetrically: Top-1 is discounted to 3/9, but "determined-verdict precision 4/4" and "overconfidence 0" are not. (Sections 7.2, 7.3–7.4; Table 6; Fig. 4 and Fig. 5 captions)**
The paper's own identity-strict analysis (Section 7.2) establishes that the batch-93 verdict is DETERMINED and does not score against the documented deviation — i.e., determined-and-wrong at the identity level, or at minimum determined-and-not-scoring. Yet Table 6 reports "Determined-verdict precision 4/4" unconditionally, the abstract-adjacent claims and Fig. 5 caption repeat "overconfidence 0 / no conclusion is simultaneously DETERMINED and wrong", and §7.3 calls overconfidence "a first-class error metric" that "never fires". Precision 4/4 is therefore an instrument-lens number presented next to a Top-1 number that the same paper argues must be discounted under the strict lens. The authors' implicit defence — the pipeline's cooling-actuator finding may be a true concurrent anomaly — is plausible but is never stated for the precision metric, only for the Top-1 metric; symmetric treatment is required. Since "no determined-and-wrong verdict" is one of the paper's two core selling points for the cap mechanism, this gap matters.
*Fix:* Add an identity-strict row to Table 6 (precision 3/4 with the concurrent-anomaly caveat spelled out; overconfidence ≥1 under that lens) and qualify the Fig. 4/Fig. 5 caption claims with "under the calibrated keyword instrument".

**M4. The ablation's central contrast equates two different quantities, and the one arm that would explain the paper's central cost statement is missing though partially computable from released artifacts. (Abstract p.1; Section 8.3; Table 5)**
(i) The abstract says the bare call "resolves 9/9 faults — more than the pipeline". "Resolved Top-1" is defined in Section 6.2 as a DETERMINED verdict naming the true mechanism, with verdict typing, evidence chain and ceiling arithmetic; the bare arm's 9/9 is "strict mechanism-keyword hit on the model's top-1 line" with no verdict state at all. The paper never argues these are commensurable; the equation is doing rhetorical work in both directions (it inflates the baseline and self-deprecates the pipeline). (ii) The deeper confound: the bare arm consumes the pipeline's own engineered statistical digest, so 9/9 demonstrates the deterministic stage already carries the discriminating signal and that the pipeline's *diagnostician/protocol* (elimination thresholds, cap arithmetic) is where resolution is lost. That reading supports a cheaper competitor (one call + digest + a decision rule) that dominates the pipeline on accuracy *and* cost (minutes vs ≈2 h), leaving auditability as the sole margin — a framing the paper approaches but does not state. The cap-disabled counterfactual is computable today without new runs: forcing a commit to each capped verdict's top-ranked survivor would yield Top-1 = 6/9 (IDV4 and IDV11 correct at family/branch level; IDV3, both SKAB cases wrong), which quantifies exactly how much resolution the cap policy costs — a number the paper's "the capped verdicts are where that cost is visible" claim needs.
*Fix:* Rename the bare-arm metric in abstract and Table 5 (e.g., "keyword-hit rate under strict scoring; no verdict typing"); add the forced-commit counterfactual computed from the released gradings; and either run the pre-registered filter-disabled arm or explicitly label the mechanism of the resolution loss (caps + elimination thresholds, not orchestration) as inferred rather than tested.

**M5. Single-execution evidence with an n = 1 consistency pair carries more inferential weight than the section framing supports, and the consistency criterion is decided at a granularity where agreement is near-guaranteed. (Section 8.4; Fig. 7; Section 9)**
The corpus holds exactly one repeat pair (TEP IDV11). The paper is candid ("One consistent pair is the beginning of a stability study, not its end"), but the section title "Verdict Stability: a Random-Draw Independent Re-execution", the abstract clause, and Fig. 7's "within-era agreement 1/1" collectively suggest a stability result where there is one anecdote. Worse, the agreement rule (same diagnosis_type AND (same primary tag OR same mechanism class)) evaluates the *coarsest* structured fields, and the drawn scenario is arguably the one fault whose signature is hardest to miss (variance ratio 40.1 with frozen means — the same deterministic statistics stage runs in both sessions, so structural agreement is close to mechanical). A draw landing on IDV3 or a SKAB case would have been a more informative test; with one draw, the protocol's evidential yield is unknown. The seed's provenance from the evaluation harness (disclosed) and the supersession rule ("latest proven execution is canonical") are both fine for this single pre-registered draw but would produce outcome-dependent selection if reused for multiple rounds — worth stating as a guard.
*Fix:* State "n = 1 repeat pair" in the abstract clause and the Section 8.4 heading area; add a power-style remark on what DIVERGENT would have looked like under the rule; pre-commit that future draws will not use the supersession rule to replace disfavourable re-runs; and prioritise the already pre-registered k ≥ 3 repeats for at least the hardest-signature scenarios (IDV3, SKAB cavitation) before claiming protocol-level reproducibility of the reasoning layer.

## Minor Issues

1. **(Section 6.1, promise not kept in Section 9.)** Section 6.1 says "the consequences of non-random selection are discussed in Section 9", but the Discussion's power paragraph covers only sample width, not selection consequences (mechanism-family diversity, difficulty distribution, documentation-availability bias). Add the promised paragraph, and stop attaching superpopulation Wilson intervals to a purposive sample without that caveat in the same sentence (Table 6, Fig. 6).
2. **(Abstract p.1 vs suite_determinism.json.)** The abstract claims the deterministic suite "reproduce[s] all 45 output files byte-for-byte"; the released verdict excludes `executed_at` ("byte-for-byte with timestamps excluded" appears only in Section 8.3). Add the qualifier to the abstract.
3. **(Section 6.2 / metrics.json taxonomy drift.)** In the released metrics.json the `calibrated` flag folds verdict-type compliance into calibration (skab_valve1_1 is `calibrated: false` because of the type mismatch although 0.60 ≤ 0.70), whereas the paper defines ceiling compliance and verdict-type compliance as separate metrics and reports tep_d01 (0.91 > machine 0.90) as `calibrated: true`. Reconcile the released field semantics with the paper's taxonomy or add a README note, otherwise readers auditing the artifact will reconstruct different numbers than Table 6.
4. **(Section 6.2, rubric R3.)** The IndPenSim control loses 20 points (R3) because its correct "normal operation" hypothesis is not grounded in "the fault-brief numbers the check targets" — a structural mis-specification of R3 for control scenarios (there are no fault-relevant numbers to cite). The paper reports the deduction without noting the check, not the run, is at fault. Scope R3 to fault scenarios or document the exception.
5. **(Table 6 row "Capped COMPETING_SET verdicts … 5/9".)** This row mixes a count (5 capped verdicts among 9 faults) with a property claim ("true mechanism ranked in all") and sits among rate rows with CIs; label it descriptively or move it to the narrative.
6. **(Section 6.1.)** Clarify whether the TEP fault-free control brief carries the anomaly-onset-window declaration that fault briefs carry. If it does, the brief is mildly primed toward a fault (which makes the zero-false-alarm result conservative, in the authors' favour — say so); if not, the TEP control is a different brief regime than claimed ("same process description and question").
7. **(Section 2.2 / Table A1 provenance.)** The FaultExplainer transcription (7/11 GPT-4o, 9/11 o1-preview, PCA-excluded IDV3/4/9/15) is flagged as transcribed, and the paper even notes an internal inconsistency in FE's own text — good. The contemporaneous AgentRCA citation (40.0% Top-1 / 61.5% Top-2, July 2026) I could not verify; ensure the bibliographic record resolves at revision, since Table 1's "none combines all five" positioning leans on it.
8. **(Section 6.4 / report §9.3.)** The bare-LLM arm is a "recorded replay of live runs"; the paper does not state *when* the live replies were recorded relative to keyword calibration and pipeline execution. A single sentence with timestamps (the report labels each reply `recorded`) would close the last experimenter-degrees-of-freedom question on the ablation arms. Also fill the trivially cheap coverage gap: run the bare arm on the TEP fault-free control (currently "--" in Fig. 8/Table 5).
9. **(Fig. 4 / Figs. 1–3.)** Figs. 1, 3 and 4 are HTML-rendered panels with very small annotation text (e.g., the per-scenario rubric/judge panel and Wilson-CI labels are barely legible at print size). AEI production will likely object; re-export at vector resolution or increase base font size.
10. **(Data availability.)** The Zenodo DOI is promised "on acceptance"; for review-time verifiability the supplementary evaluation repository (or an anonymised mirror) should be referenced with an access path in the manuscript itself, since the paper's whole argument is "the artifacts are released".

## Verdict

**MAJOR REVISION.** The evaluation machinery, released artifacts, and disclosure discipline are unusually strong — every number I checked reproduces exactly, and the self-deprecating ablation is a model of honest bounding — but the headline accuracy claims (Top-1 4/9, top-k 9/9, precision 4/4, overconfidence 0) rest on a truth-adjacent keyword instrument calibrated on the same corpus, an open identifier channel whose dedicated control was deferred despite being cheap, and an asymmetric application of the paper's own identity-strict lens. All required fixes are executable within one revision cycle (the neutralised-identifier re-run, multi-instrument scoring, symmetric precision accounting, and the forced-commit counterfactual need no new methodology), which is why this is a revision rather than a rejection.
