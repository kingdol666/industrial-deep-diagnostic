# Reviewer R3 — Presentation, Figures & Layout (Advanced Engineering Informatics, blind review)

Reviewed from `main.tex` plus all 46 rendered pages (`pagepng/p-01..46.png`, 110 dpi) and the 10 figure sources (`figures/*.png`, ~4400 px wide). All figure pages, all table pages, and ~14 text-only pages were viewed. Figure-source glyph heights were measured programmatically (PIL band analysis) to estimate effective printed point sizes.

## Paper Summary

The paper presents IDD, a nine-stage agentic pipeline for industrial root-cause analysis that couples LLM competing-hypothesis reasoning with deterministic statistics, evidence grading (L1–L7), anti-spurious-correlation filtering, and machine-checked gates, evaluated on a 12-scenario benchmark (SKAB, TEP, IndPenSim) with per-fault FaultExplainer baselines and model-controlled ablations. Headline: 4/9 faults resolved Top-1, top-k 9/9, zero false alarms, with the pipeline's margins claimed as auditability rather than accuracy. The manuscript is unusually disciplined about disclosure, and its figures/tables are accurate; the presentation problems are systematic legibility of in-figure text, one identifier contradiction, and an em-dash-heavy prose style.

## Major Issues

1. **In-figure text prints below Elsevier's ~6 pt floor across most figures (systematic).** Measured from the sources at their placed widths: Fig. 5 (p. 26) y-axis secondary labels and the x-axis annotation band at ≈4.5–5.5 pt; Fig. 2 (p. 10) stage-box description text ≈4–5 pt; Fig. 3 (p. 21) quoted artifact strings ≈5 pt; Fig. 6 (p. 27) panel-(a) annotations and panel-(b) chain chips ≈4.5–5 pt; Fig. 7 (p. 30) "IDD decisive evidence" cell text ≈4.5–5 pt; Fig. 9 (p. 33) the six-line footnote block below the matrix; Fig. 10 (p. 35) the consistency-audit status table and rotated tick labels; Fig. 1 (p. 8) grey sub-lines under plane titles. Root cause is concrete and checkable: `figures/make_figures.py` sets `PRINT_W = 5.4` "so that the nominal point sizes are the printed sizes", but the actual elsarticle-preprint text block measures ≈4.6 in (523 px at 113 px/in on the rendered pages), so every PNG is downscaled a further ~15%, and many elements additionally set explicit 7.2–7.6 pt sizes. **Fix:** set the figure width to the real `\textwidth` (print `\the\textwidth`, or ≈4.6 in) and regenerate all figures with the smallest explicit font ≥8.5 pt nominal; move Fig. 9's footnote block and Fig. 10's status table into the caption or text rather than shrinking them.

2. **Run-identifier contradiction between Fig. 3 (p. 21) and Section 8.4 (p. 34).** The Fig. 3 header displays "run 202609171639527" as the TEP IDV4 anatomy trace, while §8.4 states "the original batch execution `202609171639527` (run 1)" of the drawn scenario TEP IDV11. The same directory id cannot be both scenarios' execution, and in a paper whose evidentiary claims lean on replayable run identifiers this is a load-bearing error. **Fix:** check both against the released run directories and correct either the Fig. 3 image header or the ids quoted in §8.4 (and re-verify §8.4's 0.62-vs-0.60 pairing still attaches to the right scenario).

3. **Pervasive em-dash and nested-parenthetical style degrades sentence-level readability.** 153 `---` constructs in a 46-page paper; many sentences carry two to three nested parentheticals (e.g., abstract: "(Top-1 44.4%; a hit certifies …, never identity)", "(top-k 9/9, a set-membership bound; identifier recall cannot be excluded---…)", all within three sentences; §6.1 first paragraph, p. 17, is a single ~250-word semicolon-and-dash chain covering three datasets). This is the paper's most AI-sounding surface feature and no copyeditor will fix it structurally. **Fix:** a dedicated language pass targeting ≤1 em-dash per ~150 words, converting stacked parentheticals into separate sentences, and splitting the §6.1 dataset paragraph into one or two sentences per dataset.

## Minor Issues

1. **Spelling inconsistency:** "artefact" once (§6.1, p. 17, "not an artefact of the brief") vs "artifact" (70 occurrences). Unify on "artifact".
2. **Repeated rhetorical template "Three X"** (8×): "Three long-standing obstacles", "Three terms recur", "Three rules govern", "Three observations matter", "Three results follow", "Three design decisions", "three disclosures", "Three further layers". Vary the scaffolding.
3. **Near-verbatim repeated sentence:** "the main evidence-backed mitigation, among the disclosed residual channels, against prompt-knowledge contamination" appears in §7.3 (p. 24) and again in §9 (p. 37). Keep one instance.
4. **Table 1 (p. 5) rows labelled by bare citation numbers** ("[4, 5] PCA/Deep FDD", "[23, 24] Causal RCA"): fragile under renumbering and uninformative to readers; name the representative systems (e.g., "TranAD; PCA/Deep FDD").
5. **Over-long captions:** Table 6 caption ≈230 words (p. 23) and Fig. 7 caption ≈180 words (p. 30) duplicate body text (rubric-deduction arithmetic, colour semantics). Move the detail to a table note or the text; keep captions to what the figure shows.
6. **Table 7 (p. 25) "Decisive signature" column is justified in a narrow measure**, producing large inter-word gaps in rows 1 and 4 (e.g., "flow plateau −3.13%"). Use `\raggedright` for that p-column.
7. **Table 2 (p. 9) cell wrapping:** the agent cell "judge ‖ report-reviewer" breaks as "judge ‖ / report- / reviewer", and the `→` glyphs in the HTML-build row land alone on their own lines. `\raggedright` plus non-breaking ties will fix both.
8. **Fig. 7 in-figure title cites "(Chiang et al., 2001)" in author–year form** while the paper uses numeric citations. Change to "[9]" or drop the in-figure citation.
9. **Fig. 2 (p. 10) serpentine flow is unexplained:** row 2 reads right-to-left (Step 6 rightmost, Step 9 leftmost), so a left-to-right scan meets Step 9 before Step 6; the caption never mentions the boustrophedon arrangement. Add a half-sentence to the caption or mirror row 2.
10. **Float drift:** several floats land 2–3 pages after first citation (Fig. 2 cited p. 7 → placed p. 10; Fig. 8 cited p. 28 → p. 31; Table 7 cited p. 22 → p. 25; Table 8 cited p. 29 → p. 32). `placeins [section]` keeps them in-section, but a strategic `\clearpage` before §7/§8 or earlier float declarations would tighten this. Also, Fig. 6 (p. 27) appears one page *before* its first citation (p. 28).
11. **Fig. 9 (p. 33) largely duplicates Table 8 (p. 32)** (same arms, per-scenario vs aggregate). Keep both, but state the division of labour explicitly in the Table 8 caption to preempt redundancy criticism.
12. **Reference-list hygiene:** ref. [6] prints both "doi:10.7910/DWN/6CJ3R1" and "URL https://doi.org/10.7910/DWN/6CJ3R1" (duplicate, p. 42); ref. [21] contains "et al." inside the author list; ref. [1] appends a non-standard "preprint: arXiv:2412.14492" note. Clean the .bib entries.
13. **Byline name "ZhangHaoZheng"** has no given/family split in the byline or CRediT statement (p. 41); Elsevier metadata normally expects "Given Family" — please verify how this should index.
14. **Sentence-initial "Fig."** (§7.1 "Fig. 3 makes…", §7.4 "Fig. 5 plots…"): Elsevier style spells out "Figure" at sentence start.
15. **Table A1 (p. 46) is the tightest table in the paper:** a ~20 cm-wide tabular resized to the 12.2 cm measure puts body text at ≈6 pt effective. Consider splitting the last column or rotating the table.
16. **§8 title is overloaded** — "Comparison With Published Baselines, Ablation, Stability, and a Case Study" bundles four topics; the case study (§8.5) in particular is a Results-style narrative and could live in §7 or the section could be renamed.

## Verdict

**MINOR REVISION.** The manuscript's captions, tables, statistics and figure–text cross-references are accurate and internally consistent, the layout is clean (no overfull floats, no orphaned pages), and declarations/appendix follow Elsevier conventions; what stands between it and acceptance is mechanical: regeneration of essentially all figures at the true `\textwidth` with ≥6 pt effective text, correction of the Fig. 3/§8.4 run-identifier contradiction, and a focused language pass on the em-dash/parenthetical style. None of these requires new science or re-review of the results.
