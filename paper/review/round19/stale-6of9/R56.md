# R56 — Sceptical General Review (Round 19, blind)

Reviewer role: whole-paper read — abstract promise vs delivery, coherence, novelty, writing,
reproducibility/ethics, and above all whether the paper's description matches what the released
system actually does. I read the full LaTeX source, the rendered page images for the abstract,
architecture, framework, results, comparison, stability, case study, appendix and back matter,
and performed 14 claim-vs-reality checks against the repository (numbers, artifact paths,
event logs, code). I read nothing under `paper/review/` except this briefing's target pages.

---

## 1. Summary of the paper

The paper presents IDD, a nine-stage agentic diagnosis pipeline for industrial root-cause
analysis: ontology construction, deterministic statistics, competing-hypothesis reasoning with
L1–L7 evidence grading, anti-spurious-correlation filtering, automatic physical verification,
ten-criterion judging with bounded repair, gated reporting and HTML review. It contributes a
machine-checked evaluation framework (truth isolation, SHA-256 fingerprinting, stage-authored
artifacts with provenance, gate-graded scoring, artifact-consistency verification) and reports a
12-scenario benchmark (SKAB 2+1, TEP 6+1, IndPenSim 1+1): Top-1 6/9 (CI [35.4, 87.9]), top-k 8/9,
TEP subset 5/6, controls 3/3 with zero false alarms, rubric 97.9/100, judge gate 93.1. A
model-controlled ablation shows a bare call to the same model family resolves 9/9 — more than the
pipeline — so the claimed margin is auditability, calibrated uncertainty and gated process
guarantees, not accuracy.

## 2. Abstract vs delivery

The abstract (~250 words, exactly at the Elsevier cap by my count) makes only claims the body
delivers: 6/9 with the IDV3 miss stated as a miss; 5/9 pre-revision and 4/6 TEP pre-revision
stated without spin and repeated in Table 7, Fig. 6/7 captions, §8.1, §9 and the Conclusion; the
bare-LLM 9/9 > 6/9 ablation result is in the abstract itself, which is unusually honest. The
three capped COMPETING_SET verdicts, the named discriminating measurements, and the zero
false-alarm result all check out against released artifacts (see spot checks S3–S8). One
qualification: the abstract's closing sentence "Internal consistency is machine-verified" is
stronger than what C5 claims in §5 ("deliberately not an independent re-execution of the
reasoning layer"); and the abstract does not carry the two execution-form qualifications that the
results rest on (inline single-session stage execution; VLM stage never ran with vision).

## 3. Contributions vs delivery

Contribution 1 (quality-gated pipeline) and 2 (evidence grading with gate propagation) are
delivered as described — I verified the checkpoint table (Table 3) against the artifact set on
disk, the R4 calibration gate in code, the reason-coded DIRECT_MEASUREMENT ceiling in the
released `confidence.json` of the IDV4 run, and the ceiling documentation in the diagnostician
skill (INDIST ≤65, CS ≤70, PARAM_AMBIGUITY ≤50; no DET cap agent-side, 0.90 at the machine gate).
Contribution 3 (reproducible evaluation framework) is delivered to an unusual standard: the
released artifact index (Table A2) matches disk file-for-file, the 14-item completeness gate in
Table 5 is byte-identical to the grader's `PIPELINE_ARTIFACTS` list, and the journal/git
chronology of the keyword calibration is exactly as tabulated. Contribution 4 (12-scenario
benchmark with per-fault FE comparison) is delivered with the stated caveats; the Register A/B
separation is respected throughout and no cross-system significance test is claimed.

The novelty is moderate and the paper says so: relative to FaultExplainer and the contemporaneous
AgentRCA, the differentiators are the three-state calibrated verdicts, evidence-level bounding,
and the machine-checkable evaluation contract (Table 1 positioning is fair; elements (iii)–(iv)
are genuinely absent from the cited systems). The accuracy contribution is admittedly negative
(bare LLM 9/9 vs pipeline 6/9); the paper's reframing of the contribution as audit/process
guarantees is the correct and honest move, and §4.4's intellectual-ancestry section correctly
declines to claim the underlying frameworks as inventions.

## 6. Reproducibility, ethics, back matter

Reproducibility posture is exemplary for this manuscript class: per-scenario run directories
with stage-authored artifacts and event logs, a run journal, git-pinned keyword sets with an
edit-history disclosure, a released sensitivity addendum that shifts the headline by exactly one
scenario in each direction, seeded re-test draws with replayable seeds, a determinism verdict
for the deterministic arms (45/45), and an artifact-consistency audit. The two-layer
reproducibility semantics (byte-level deterministic stage vs procedure-level reasoning layer)
are stated correctly and never conflated. The C1 chronology table discloses the superseded
pre-switch grading batch rather than hiding it, and the sensitivity analysis converts what could
have been a hidden conflict of interest into a bounded, released result. Back matter is
complete: single-author CRediT, no-COI, no-funding, data availability (supplementary + Zenodo
DOI on acceptance; third-party datasets cited), and a GenAI-in-writing declaration that usefully
distinguishes the writing tools from the system under study. Ethics: public datasets, no human
subjects; the one operational deployment is anonymised and outside the released corpus, as
stated.

## 7. Weaknesses (why not "accept as-is")

1. **Execution form vs architecture narrative.** The benchmarked runs executed each stage
   *inline* in one session (no sub-agent spawn tool; disclosed in §5 "Execution form" and §6.4),
   consecutive stages may share a model session, and the VLM stage ran its non-visual degradation
   in every run (no vision-capable model in the deployment). The paper discloses all of this
   wherever it matters, but the abstract still sells "nine-stage agentic orchestration" without
   the qualifier, and the p.10 assertion "sub-agents communicate only through workspace artifacts,
   never through conversation context" describes the production dispatch form, not the evaluated
   one. The evaluated artifact is best described as protocol-faithful inline execution of the
   nine-stage contract; a reader should not take the benchmark numbers as evidence that the
   multi-agent dispatch machinery improves anything (the paper does not claim this, but could
   say it more bluntly once, early).
2. **Modest discriminative power of the evaluation, by the paper's own showing.** n=9 with CI
   [35.4, 87.9]; the rubric is at ceiling for 10/12 and is author-designed; keywords are
   author-designed; no human-expert anchor. All disclosed, but the scientific yield is the
   protocol-and-audit demonstration, not a performance advance. Acceptable for AEI if framed as
   engineering-informatics methodology, which it is.
3. **Copyediting.** §6.4 (p.34) has a broken sentence: an unbalanced opening parenthesis
   "(streaming CSV inspection, … environment)" and a lowercase continuation "spans). the entire
   chain (prepare, …)". The abstract sits exactly at the 250-word cap. p.55 is nearly empty.
4. **Clock conventions.** Run-directory names and `.pipeline_events.jsonl` stamps share one clock
   (local, UTC+8 — a Z-suffixed event timestamp equals the local dir-name time), while journal
   grading stamps are UTC; Table 4's caption covers the journal but a reader re-deriving
   execution spans from event logs needs one clarifying sentence.
5. **Commit 221d061** is a 347-file mixed snapshot (baselines, gradings, briefs, case
   definitions); "the calibrated set is pinned at commit 221d061" is accurate but slightly
   dignifies a snapshot commit; one clause would fix it.

## 8. Verdict

Every factual claim I checked against the repository — including several the authors might
reasonably have hoped no one would check (the superseded pre-switch grading batch, the bare-LLM
control coverage gap, the one-directional verdict typing, the IDV14 period-estimate instability
across sessions) — is accurate and, where adverse, disclosed more prominently than required.
The paper's promise–delivery alignment is the best I can recall reviewing. The remaining issues
are presentational (abstract qualifier, one broken sentence, float packing, clock-convention
footnote) and do not touch the results. I sign a favourable report at minor revision.
