## 1. Summary of the paper

The paper presents IDD, a nine-stage multi-agent diagnosis pipeline (ontology, deterministic
statistics, competing-hypothesis reasoning, physical verification, gated reporting) with L1-L7
evidence grading, a three-state verdict protocol (DETERMINED / COMPETING_SET / NEEDS_DATA), a
four-condition anti-spurious filter, and a machine-checked evaluation framework (truth isolation,
SHA-256 fingerprinting, stage-authored artifacts with provenance, a 14-artifact completeness gate,
and an artifact-consistency gate). It reports a 12-scenario benchmark on SKAB/TEP/IndPenSim
(9 faults + 3 controls), per-fault comparison against FaultExplainer, three model-controlled
baselines, stability/determinism audits, and a case study.

  five-axis positioning (Table 1) and the claim that calibrated three-state conclusions and a
  machine-verifiable evaluation contract are absent from all cited systems are defensible; the

  references (AgentRCA/Wei & Fink, Gong et al. JIII, Vieira et al. MSSSP) are bibliographically
  complete and plausible; editors should re-verify them at proof stage.
- Intellectual ancestry (Heuer ACH, Hill's criteria, Chow's reject option, Parasuraman, Lee &

## 7. Weaknesses (skeptical view)

1. **Evidence base is thin for the applied claim**: n=9 faults, one recorded execution each, one
   model family, one deployment; the Wilson CI [35.4, 87.9] spans nearly the whole unit interval.
   The paper discloses all of this and leans on process guarantees, but AEI readers should
   understand that the accuracy table is anecdotal; the transferable finding is the capped-verdict
   behaviour and the audit machinery.
2. **Self-graded protocol**: rubric, keywords, and allowed-verdict sets are author-designed;
   independent expert anchoring is outstanding (disclosed). Until that exists, "conformance"
   remains internal.
3. **Headline uses the calibrated keywords**, which the authors themselves revised inside the
   execution window (executions span 18:43→04:54; the switch is journaled 04:06-04:09, i.e., in
   the middle, with three canonical executions after it). The released sensitivity analysis
   (exactly one scenario flips either way, verified in check 1) and the ever-present 5/9 and 4/6
   qualifiers neutralise most of the concern, but the residual asymmetry is real.
4. **Length and repetition**: 72 pp with the keyword-calibration timeline narrated three times
   (C1, stability, discussion-adjacent) and the bare-LLM bound restated in six places. A third
   of the prose could be cut without losing content.
5. **Minor precision errors** (see checks 7 and 8): the "(all in the optional layers)" clause is
   factually wrong for the orchestrator package; a sentence acknowledging that the engine
   capability table and the dispatch table jointly realise the 14-engine surface would be more
   accurate than the current phrasing.

## 8. Verdict

The paper's description matches what the system actually does to a degree I have rarely seen:
of ten targeted claim-vs-reality checks mixing statistics, artifact paths, and code-level
architecture claims, nine match exactly and one fails only on a subordinate clause. The
prominent negatives are genuinely prominent. The remaining issues are textual precision, length,
and the inherent thinness of a 9-fault single-run corpus — all addressable without new
experiments. I would sign a favorable report after minor revision.
