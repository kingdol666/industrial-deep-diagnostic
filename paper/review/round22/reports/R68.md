## 1. What the paper promises and whether it delivers

The abstract promises: (i) a nine-stage agentic diagnosis pipeline with evidence grading
L1–L7, an anti-spurious filter, and a machine-checked evaluation framework; (ii) 6/9 Top-1
on a 12-scenario benchmark with the pre-revision 5/9 stated inline; (iii) IDV3 reported as
a mechanism-level miss; (iv) three capped COMPETING_SET verdicts naming missing
measurements; zero false alarms; (v) TEP subset 5/6 with 4/6 pre-revision stated inline;
(vi) the model-controlled ablation showing a bare same-model call resolves 9/9 — *more than
the pipeline* — so the pipeline's margins are auditability, not accuracy; (vii) "internal
consistency is machine-verified against the released execution proofs."

**Every one of these promises is delivered and, so far as I could trace, accurate.**
Item (vi) in particular — printing in the abstract that a bare LLM call beats the pipeline
on scored accuracy — is the single strongest credibility signal in the paper, and it checks
out against the released baseline record (see SC-8 below). Item (vii) is carefully scoped
in §5(C5) as internal-consistency verification ("deliberately not an independent
re-execution of the reasoning layer"), which is the honest phrasing.

## 3. Novelty assessment

Positioning against FaultExplainer is well done and fair: the paper states the three
protocol differences (candidates-in-prompt vs none, alias-tolerant top-3 vs strict single
verdict, PCA-scoreable subset) *every time* Register A is invoked, refuses a significance
test across different sets, and reads the per-fault table "descriptively, not as a
superiority claim" — the right posture given the bare-LLM 9/9 result. The contemporaneous
AgentRCA comparison (40.0% Top-1 / 61.5% Top-2 on ten TEP faults, no calibrated
confidence, no external truth harness) is cited accurately as far as I can judge and is
not caricatured. The five-column positioning table (Table 1) is assertive but each cell
for IDD is backed by a released artifact I verified (evidence trails, calibrated
three-state verdicts, reproducibility gate). Section 2.4 grounding the design in
Parasuraman/Lee-&-See/Heuer/Hill/Chow, while explicitly disclaiming invention of these
frameworks, is above-average scholarship for a systems paper. The genuinely novel
contributions are the machine-checkable evaluation contract (truth isolation,
fingerprinting 153/153, stage-authored provenance, gate-graded scoring, keyword
calibration released with its full journal timeline and a sensitivity driver) and the
honest three-state verdict behaviour demonstrated on real recordings. That is enough
novelty for AEI.

  evidence supports: applied before any *canonical grading that counted* (and before the

- **MF-2 — Abstract (p.1–2).** Machine count ≈252 words (counting math groups and
  "6/9"-style tokens as single words), at/over AEI's 250-word limit. Trim ~5 words.

## 6. Verdict

The paper's central promise — that its description of the system can be checked against
what the system actually does — is not merely met but demonstrably met: I traced 16 claim
clusters to released artifacts, graders, journals, git history, and skill/engine source
code, and found every printed number, artifact path, and architecture claim accurate,
with two wording-level exceptions (MF-1, MF-2) and no overclaim anywhere — the opposite:
the abstract, results, and discussion systematically print the results that hurt the
system (bare-LLM 9/9 > pipeline 6/9; 5/9 and 4/6 pre-revision; IDV3 miss; single
verdict-type compliance miss; degraded VLM; inline execution form; author-designed
instruments; single-execution evidence; n=9). The remaining fixes are precision edits, not
research gaps. I would sign a favorable report conditional on MF-1 and MF-2.
