Trustworthy agentic diagnosis for industrial processes: evidence-graded competing-hypothesis reasoning evaluated against per-fault literature baselines
ZhangHaoZhenga
aInstitute of Advanced Technology, University of Science and Technology of China, Hefei 230088, China
Abstract
Large language model (LLM) based diagnosis promises root-cause analysis of industrial processes without task-specific training, yet two obstacles keep it out of engineering practice: conclusions that cannot be audited, and evaluation protocols that score sample-level classification while engineers need scenariolevel, mechanism-grounded root causes. We present the Industrial Deep Diagnostic (IDD) pipeline: nine-stage agentic orchestration (ontology, deterministic statistics, competing-hypothesis reasoning, physical verification, bounded-repair gates) with evidence grading (L1­L7), an anti-spurious-correlation filter, and a machine-checked evaluation framework of stage-authored, provenance-labelled artifacts. In single recorded executions on a 12-scenario benchmark from three public datasets (SKAB, Tennessee Eastman Process, IndPenSim), IDD resolves 4/9 documented faults to a single root cause (Top-1 44.4%; a hit certifies mechanism-family agreement, never identity, and the three TEP-derived hits remain identifier-recall-possible); the true mechanism is named within the primary finding or surviving-hypothesis set in all nine (top-k 9/9, a set-membership bound); the five unseparable cases return capped competing_set verdicts naming the discriminating measurement; no control raises a false alarm. A model-controlled ablation bounds every accuracy claim: a bare call to the same model family over the same blind digest keyword-scores 9/9 faults (no verdict typing)--more than the pipeline resolves--so the pipeline's margins are process qualities, not accuracy. One seeded random draw selected a fault scenario for an independent re-execution (one repeat pair), reproducing the verdict state, actuator, and mechanism class within two confidence points; the deterministic suite arms reproduce all 45 output files byte-identically (timestamps excluded); internal consistency is machine-verified.
Keywords: Fault root-cause analysis, Large language models, Multi-agent systems, Competing-hypothesis reasoning, Evidence grading, Reproducible benchmark

1. Introduction
Root-cause analysis (RCA) of industrial process anomalies is a knowledgeintensive activity: the engineer must decide which of many correlated sensor deviations represents the true underlying cause, justify that judgement with evidence, and communicate it with a calibrated degree of confidence. Three long-standing obstacles keep that automation unsatisfactory in practice.
First, single-LLM diagnosis is unstructured and unauditable. Recent evaluations of LLM-based fault explanation on the Tennessee Eastman Process (TEP) [1] show that large models can articulate plausible mechanisms, but their answers arrive as free-form text with no machine-checkable structure, no separation of facts from inference, and no mechanism that prevents a confidentsounding conclusion from resting on a spurious correlation. A recent review of agentic approaches to process monitoring and fault diagnosis concludes that evidence for LLM/multi-agent fault diagnosis remains scarce [2]: the direction is promising but lacks engineering-grade pipelines.
Second, classical data-driven fault detection and diagnosis (FDD) optimises the wrong target. Principal-component methods [3], deep anomaly detectors [4, 5], and their benchmarks [6] score per-sample detection or classification [7]. An engineer, however, asks a per-episode question: given this anomalous run, what is the root cause, how confident are we, and what evidence supports it? A 99%-accurate classifier that returns a fault label without mechanism, evidence, or calibration does not answer that question. The detection­diagnosis gap is not merely theoretical: classical detectability studies place the documented disturbances IDV3, IDV9 and IDV15 of the fifteen-fault TEP catalogue at the detection limit, with IDV4 weakly detected by the T 2 statistic [8, 9], and the published LLM baseline's own PCA implementation leaves all four of these faults unscored [1].
Third, correlation is not causation. In recirculating process loops, almost every pair of variables is correlated through control action or material balance. An RCA method that treats strong correlation as causation will confidently report control-loop artifacts as root causes. Despite decades of causalinference literature [10, 11], contemporary LLM-diagnosis studies rarely include explicit anti-spurious safeguards.
This paper addresses the three obstacles with the Industrial Deep Diagnostic (IDD) pipeline and a benchmark built to evaluate it honestly. Our contributions are:
1. A quality-gated agentic diagnosis pipeline that couples deterministic statistical tooling with LLM reasoning under a competing-hypotheses protocol (Section 4), bounded repair loops, and automatic physical verification, so that every conclusion is decomposable into hypothesis, evidence, exclusion, and confidence.
2

2. An evidence-grading and calibration scheme (levels L1­L7 with gate propagation) that bounds reported confidence by the weakest supporting evidence level. In the corpus none of the determined verdicts is wrong, and in every scenario whose data cannot separate the surviving mechanisms the pipeline returns a capped competing_set verdict that keeps the true mechanism ranked and names the discriminating measurement instead of forcing a conclusion (Section 7).
3. A reproducible evaluation framework--truth isolation, data fingerprinting, artifact-provenance labels, recorded execution traces, and a machine-checked artifact-consistency gate--in which a scenario is graded only if its complete artifact set was authored by gated pipeline stages and the execution proof passes; an internal integrity audit removed a scripted artifact-fabrication shortcut, and the benchmark was re-executed end to end under the tightened contract (Section 5).
4. A 12-scenario benchmark on three public datasets (SKAB, TEP, IndPenSim) whose TEP subset carries scored per-fault baselines from FaultExplainer [1] on four faults (the remaining two lie where its PCA screen excludes them). IDD resolves 4/9 faults to a single root cause while ranking the true mechanism in every fault scenario's surviving set (top-k 9/9) and raising zero false alarms on the controls; a seeded random-draw reexecution reproduced one scenario's verdict state and mechanism (Section 8.4); and the graded runs executed as true per-stage sub-agent dispatches communicating only through workspace artifacts (Sections 5, 7­8).
Section 2 positions the work against data-driven FDD, LLM-based diagnosis, and multi-agent orchestration research. Sections 3 and 4 detail the architecture and methodology. Section 5 defines the evaluation framework. Sections 6­8 present the benchmark and comparisons, and Section 9 discusses limitations and deployment prospects.
2. Related Work
2.1. Data-Driven Fault Detection and the Benchmark Gap
Statistical process monitoring on the Tennessee Eastman Process established the dominant evaluation template [12, 7]: principal-component analysis (PCA) and projection methods with T 2/Q statistics [3], later extended by deep anomaly detectors [13, 4] and adversarial robustness studies [5]. Within the tripartite partition of fault detection and diagnosis into quantitative-model-based, qualitative-model-based, and process-history methods [14, 15, 16], these families occupy the process-history branch, and the quantitative branch is anchored by model-based analytical redundancy and residual-based fault isolation [17]. All of these branches score sample-level detection or fault-class accuracy. Critically, they do not evaluate root-cause mechanisms: a classifier that labels a run "IDV(4)" provides the class identity but neither a causal mechanism, an evidence trail, nor a confidence statement. The classical detection ceiling (IDV3/9/15 at the limit, IDV4 weak) is documented [8, 9]. Benchmark methodology has drawn
3

scrutiny: random segment splits inflate reported AUROC--by up to 15.3 percentage points of Macro-AUROC under segmentation-level leakage in Vieira et al. [18], with a lineage formalised by Hendriks et al. [19]--motivating our entity-level scenario splits (Section 5). Scenario-level root-cause localisation itself has a classical lineage that IDD must be read against: Granger-causal and transfer-entropy methods localise propagation directions in plant-wide oscillations [10, 11], signed-directed-graph reasoning performs mechanism-chain elimination [20], and digital-twin practice (surveyed in [21]) mirrors model-based diagnosis.
2.2. LLM-Based Industrial Diagnosis
FaultExplainer [1] is the closest published system: it couples PCA-based detection with LLM-generated fault explanations on TEP. Its evaluation uses two prompt regimes. In the root-causes-included regime the prompt carries the documented root-cause list, the model outputs a top-3 candidate list, and alias matches are accepted (alias classes: IDV1/2/8, IDV3IDV9, IDV4IDV11IDV14, IDV5IDV12IDV15); here GPT-4o resolves 7/11 and o1-preview 9/11 of the 11 PCA-detectable faults (FaultExplainer's own PCA implementation excludes IDV3, IDV4, IDV9 and IDV15 from scoring). In its General-Reasoning regime--no candidate list, designed to simulate unseen faults--both models place the correct or a related cause in the top-3 for 8/11 faults under lenient "related" grading (FE's own text names faults 10 and 13 as failing yet reports 8/11; we transcribe the reported figure). Protocol differences therefore condition the comparison: (i) FE's outputs are top-3 lists with alias/related acceptance, whereas IDD commits to a single mechanism verdict scored by strict mechanism keywords; (ii) the two PCA-excluded faults in our subset (IDV3, IDV4) are scored by IDD; (iii) FE's alias policy grades a cooling-water-family answer as correct for IDV11 and IDV14 alike, whereas IDD separates the three forms of the same physical channel. Gong et al. [22] orchestrate multiple LLM agents with difficulty-aware routing, but evaluate on a multiple-choice QA benchmark (FailureSensorIQ [23]) rather than on real time-series data.
Liu et al. [24] combine scene graphs, fine-tuned Llama-3 and augmented reality for machine-tool maintenance, and causal-graph RCA has been studied for time-varying processes [25] and knowledge-map-based group decision settings [26]; a label-free evaluation scheme for diagnosis models has been proposed in the same venue [27]. Multimodal LLM frameworks extend the modality range: Alsaif et al. [28] couple textual, tabular, and image modalities for Industry-4.0 fault detection and diagnosis over industrial fault records, and Merkelbach and Hasenauer [29] show that multimodal LLMs can draft physical models for automated diagnosis, within disclosed limits. Recent work in this journal shows the same momentum and the same gaps. CausalKGPT couples an industrial causal knowledge graph with an LLM for quality-problem cause analysis [30]; FD-LLM fine-tunes a large language model for equipment fault diagnosis [31]; Wen et al. orchestrate LLMs for human­machine collaborative troubleshooting [32]; and Xu et al. pair a deep digital twin with a vision-language model

2.3. Multi-Agent Orchestration Frameworks
Generic agent frameworks (AutoGen [35] and commercial derivatives; ReActstyle reasoning­acting loops [36]) provide message-passing and role prompting but leave audit, evidence policy, and evaluation contracts to the application. Our position, following the practice of the reviewed agentic-FDD literature [2], is that industrial diagnosis requires these as first-class, machine-checked pipeline elements rather than prompt conventions; Section 3 describes how IDD enforces them through checkpoints, bounded repair loops, and an event-log execution proof.
2.4. Trust, Automation Levels, and Intellectual Ancestry
IDD's design decisions sit on well-mapped ground, and we credit the map. The three-state verdict is a level-of-automation and trust-calibration decision--what the system concludes versus what it escalates to humans--in the sense of Parasuraman et al.'s types and levels of automation [37] and Lee

5

& See's appropriate-reliance account of trust [38]: a capped competing_set implements calibrated transfer in their sense. The competing-hypotheses discipline descends from Heuer's Analysis of Competing Hypotheses [39], the four-condition anti-spurious filter from Hill's causation criteria [40], and the abstention option from Chow's recognition-with-reject tradeoff [41], whose statistical counterpart is modern selective prediction [42]; the L1­L7 grounding check doubles as a faithfulness proxy in the sense of rigorous XAI evaluation [43]. The named-discriminating-measurement exit is the passive counterpart of active fault diagnosis, which designs inputs to separate faults before they occur [44]: IDD inherits the passive setting of plant recording and converts its residual ambiguity into an instrumentation request. The ontology-asset layer continues the process-engineering ontology lineage of OntoCAPE [45]. IDD's contribution with respect to this ancestry is a domain-specific, machine-checked instantiation of these frameworks inside an agentic pipeline--not their invention.
3. System Architecture
3.1. Execution Architecture
The following terms recur throughout. A skill package is a versioned folder holding an agent's protocol checklist, JSON Schemas, and validation scripts; a specialist agent is an independently spawned session that executes one skill package; an engine is the CLI/RPC harness that runs such a session. Fig. 1 shows the skill execution architecture as five planes, of which the audit plane is machine-checked. Client surfaces (the web application, CLI drivers, and a CI harness) submit jobs through the engine layer: fourteen interchangeable CLI/RPC engines behind one uniform event contract, with pre-flight probing and typed error codes. The orchestrating main agent holds the domain knowledge as skill packages and dispatches each pipeline stage by passing absolute paths (SKILL_PATH, RUN_DIR); sub-agents communicate only through workspace artifacts, never through conversation context.
Read against the released software, the layer map needs three qualifications. First, the package contains 18 skill packages executed by 14 specialised agents, but the diagnostic path reported here (Steps 2­8.5) dispatches eight specialist sub-agents--context-builder, data-processor, diagnostician, judge, reportreviewer, reporter, html-visualizer, html-reviewer--with the VLM visual analyser dispatched by the data-processor inside its Phase 5.5; the remaining packages form optional layers outside the benchmarked path (Section 3.2). Second, a skill package is not a prompt alone: every dispatched package carries the protocol checklist (references/agent-protocol.md) and ships the JSON Schemas and validation scripts that machine-check its artifacts before the stage may complete--the structural difference from prompting a model directly. Third, the engine layer gives the pipeline harness portability: all fourteen engines implement one start/stream/close contract, so an engine substitution is a configuration change rather than a re-implementation--pre-registered as an ablation axis, not yet exercised. 3.2. Knowledge, Web, and Enhancement Layers
Beyond the diagnostic path sit three further layers; how each was exercised, degraded, or left idle in the benchmark is recorded in the run events.
Semantic-knowledge layer. A retrieval-augmented generation microservice [46] (FastAPI + ChromaDB, rag-retrieval-engine/) supplies semantic understanding for ontology construction and is the only sanctioned source of level-L6 external evidence; the orchestrator probes it with a three-second health check at Step 0. When it is unavailable, the ontology builder falls back to the shipped parameter_to_physics.json mechanism map plus the data's own self-description, and the degradation is recorded in the run events. In the benchmark corpus the channel was never available: all thirteen runs record a failed probe and build every ontology through the documented fallback (recorded per run as a dedicated probe-failure file or a rag_validated:false ontology flag); the TEP ontologies ground their variable semantics in the published process description, cross-checked against the data. No L6-tagged item exists in the released corpus, so no verdict rests on external evidence.
Ontology asset store. Ontologies are published to a portable asset store (ontology_store.mjs publish) keyed by a schema fingerprint; a later scenario whose variable schema matches is resolved by deterministic fast-reuse, which copies the validated ontology verbatim into the run directory in seconds instead of re-deriving it. All thirteen benchmark ontologies were published to the store on completion; none was consumed by fast-reuse in this corpus, because the benchmark configuration pins the ontology step to full rebuilds (the reuse path is exercised by the production harness outside this benchmark). The store is deterministic and therefore does not affect the benchmark's truth-isolation contract (it reuses variable semantics, never scenario verdicts).
Integration surface. The web application couples an Express backend with a Vue 3/Vite frontend and an SSE/WebSocket live stream. Its diagnosis endpoint accepts an engine selection and returns typed pre-flight errors for unknown or unusable engines, with probing and the real spawn sharing one code path. The optional enhancement layer (E0­E8, deterministic scripts orchestrated by a dedicated agent) post-processes completed runs for reporting depth and lies outside the benchmarked path.

3.4. Execution Layer: Interchangeable Engines With a Uniform Contract
Reasoning agents are executed by any of fourteen interchangeable engines-- the Claude Agent SDK, several RPC and ACP dialects, and a set of one-shot CLIs--behind a single start/stream/close contract realised as a capability table (engines.mjs) plus first-party wiring entries in the service dispatch table. Engine availability is probed and validated before execution with typed error codes (400 unknown engine, 409 HARNESS_UNAVAILABLE unusable engine); a scripted in-process mock engine exercises the full pipeline in CI without external dependencies. Because every engine emits the same standardised event stream, the benchmark harness consumes any compliant engine's stream identically; diagnostic quality across engines is an empirical ablation question, and engine substitution remains a designed but not-yet-exercised ablation axis (Section 9).
3.5. Audit Infrastructure
Every stage transition appends a structured event to .pipeline_events. jsonl; a log checker validates the start and completion records of every agent against the required contract, and a finalisation gate aggregates inventory, schema, closure, judge-cross-audit, and event-archive checks into a single PASS/FAIL verdict. 4. Diagnostic Methodology

4.1. Problem Formulation
A scenario supplies a recorded window DS of p process variables and, optionally, user context; the task is to produce a verdict v  {det, cs, nd} with a mechanism-level root cause, a confidence c  [0, 1], and an evidence chain. The diagnostician forms a hypothesis set H = {h1, . . . , hk}, k  3, where each hi carries a mechanism class mi, a set Ei of evidence items, and a contradiction set Xi. Two definitions govern the protocol. The evidence level of a hypothesis is the weakest level in its chain, (hi) = mineEi level(e) with level(e)  {L1, . . . , L7} (Section 4.3)--a conclusion cannot be stronger than its weakest citation. The confidence ceiling is the minimum of the five-factor assessment and every protocol cap applicable to the verdict state,

The agent-side protocol documents no det cap, but the machine grading gate flags--rather than blocks--confidence above 0.90 for every non-cs state and above 0.70 for cs (calibration check R4 of the released rubric; the two 0.91/0.93 verdicts of Section 7.2 are exactly this flag firing), so the effective cap set C includes 0.90 for det at the gate (the gate's flat non-CS 0.90 ceiling also covers nd). Reason-coded per-run ceilings may still bind; the released schema enumerates the reasons: indistinguishable competing set, unsorted-data lag claims, product-specific-only evidence, all-criteria-verified, direct measurement. For example, the canonical IDV7 run binds its determined verdict at 0.78 under a reason-coded ceiling of 0.80 (recorded reason ALL_CRITERIA_VERIFIED, noting that the root variable, header supply pressure, is unobservable, so the verdict is capped at the justified-inference level). Algorithm 1 states the whole procedure as executed, with deterministic (script) and semantic (agent) steps marked; the ablation of Section 8.3 is the empirical test of this split.
4.2. Competing-Hypotheses Protocol
The diagnostician agent must generate at least three candidate hypotheses per fault scenario (the protocol's intellectual ancestry is credited in Section 2.4), assign each a mechanism class (e.g., fluid restriction, cavitation, rotor dynamics, feed-composition step, cooling disturbance, sensor drift), collect for each an evidence list with explicit levels, identify contradictions, and eliminate hypotheses whose contradictions are stronger than their support. The protocol admits exactly three conclusion states: determined (a single surviving hypothesis, bounded by the five-factor assessment; reason-coded per-run ceilings may further bind it), competing_set (2 survivors that the data cannot discriminate, with confidence capped at 0.70; observed caps in this corpus are 0.55­0.63 and the discriminating measurement is named), and needs_data (evidence insufficient; missing data itemised). A fourth, implicit state--normal operation with

13

no confirmed fault--applies to control scenarios. The elimination contract is quantitative: every elimination must cite the contradicting evidence, and every surviving chain must cite evidence numbers present in the statistical digests.
4.3. Evidence Grading and Gate Propagation
Every piece of evidence carries a level L1­L7 (L1 direct measurement, L2 operating documents, L3 statistical analysis, L4 visual evidence, L5 domain ontology/mechanism, L6 external reference, L7 unsupported assumption). The ordering is a policy choice: direct measurement outranks statistical processing because a sensor reading cannot inherit a processing error, while visual evidence is graded below statistics because chart reading is itself an inference. The scenario confidence is bounded by the weakest level contributing to the surviving chain, and the quality gate flags, for deduction, conclusions whose confidence exceeds that bound. This converts "hallucination control" from a prompt instruction into a checkable arithmetic constraint.
4.4. Anti-Spurious-Correlation Filter
Four conditions must hold before a correlation may be cited as causal support: (i) temporal precedence of cause over effect, verified by lag-compensated cross-correlation; (ii) statistical significance surviving detrending, Simpsonstratification checks, and multiple-testing correction; (iii) a physical mechanism annotated in the ontology connecting the variables; and (iv) absence of contradictions under leave-one-out outlier sensitivity analysis, with production-state (steady/batch-phase) filtering applied before correlation. Correlations failing the filter are recorded but explicitly barred from hypothesis support; definitional pairs (e.g., valve position and the level it controls, r = 1.0) are classified as control-loop artifacts. In the benchmark this filter is the reason control-loop correlations are not reported as root causes, and the reason normal batches with impulsive recipe-dosing spikes (|z| up to 16.2) are graded as healthy rather than faulty.
4.5. Automatic Physical Verification
After the conclusion is drafted, a physics checker recomputes quantitative feasibility (mass/energy balances, thermal and vibration magnitude envelopes) from the cleaned data, ontology, and anomaly report, producing a machinereadable physical_checks verdict. When no automated check applies to a scenario's column semantics, the pipeline falls back to an explicit L1­L5 manualverification memo; silent omission is not permitted.
4.6. Quality Gates, Repair Loops, and Calibration
A ten-criterion judge scores the diagnosis (threshold 90); a parallel physical pre-audit reviews mechanism chains; failures trigger bounded repair (per-fault rerun 3, global cap 5, with anti-oscillation confidence capping at 0.50). The final physical audit must return ENDORSED. If the review-repair budget exhausts without an endorsement, the run proceeds best-effort and the finalisation
14

gate records the missing endorsement as a critical failure, so the run is a pipeline failure rather than a gradeable result. Ceiling compliance is then auditable endto-end: every scenario grading records whether the reported confidence respects the protocol ceiling, and overconfidence (determined and wrong) is a first-class error metric.
The released governance keeps a stochastic reasoner bounded by design. Each repair round snapshots the best-scoring attempt (best_round copy of the diagnosis set); the second and third rounds run in a declared repair scope that recomputes only the implicated artifacts and marks the rest as carried over, so a fix cannot silently rewrite evidence that was not found defective (auditable from the declared repair scopes and carried-over markers in the released logs). The loop never halts the run on score alone: if the threshold is still unmet after the budget, the pipeline delivers a best-effort report with a gate-enforced capped verdict (and the cap recorded), because failing to deliver is worse than delivering with a disclosed confidence. The protocol adds two machine-checkable disciplines. The data-truth mandate requires every number in a diagnosis to be reproducible from the raw data (chain items carry derived markers and a NOTAPPLICABLE fallback), and the red-light list makes the principal failure modes auditable: no forced single verdict from a competing set, no global correlation cited as causal evidence, no conclusion without an evidence-level tag, no HTML outside the manifest. Violations are what the judge is instructed to flag, which is how the audit plane of Section 3 remains a check rather than a claim.
5. A Reproducible Evaluation Framework
IDD's benchmark framework (released as the project's experience/ evidence repository and docs/benchmark/ protocol) is designed around five contracts, each machine-enforced:
(C1) Truth isolation. Scenario definitions carry ground truth and grading keywords, but the pipeline's visible inputs contain only the process description and the data. Ground truth never enters the inference context; the grader owns it exclusively. A leakage sentinel machine-checks the contract from both directions: no ground-truth string may appear in any agent-visible brief, and no fault-vocabulary may appear in the run directories' user-context files. Scenario definitions--including the grading keywords and the allowed verdict set of every scenario--are versioned in the released repository with their edit history. The keyword sets used to grade the current corpus are the calibrated set pinned at commit 221d061--over-generic tokens removed, mechanism synonyms added-- then frozen before any current-era execution. The calibration is truth-adjacent in two ways that Section 7.2 quantifies: the "v1-era corpus" it was tuned against denotes the pre-discipline execution batch of the same twelve scenarios (run directories dated before the 2026-09-14 anti-oscillation and confidence-cap revision, retained with the released edit history), and the published per-fault baselines contributed the mechanism synonyms. The sentinel machine-checks briefs and user-context files, not run-directory names; the identifier channel is the residual disclosure channel and is treated in Section 9. These keys are
15

author-designed (Section 9): a hit certifies mechanism-family agreement with the documented truth, not identity, and one scenario's set includes a generic token.
(C2) Data fingerprinting. Every input file is hashed (SHA-256) into a dataset manifest; the reproducibility gate re-verifies all 153 input fingerprints and the leakage sentinel returns a PASS verdict before any metric is accepted.
(C3) Stage-authored artifacts with provenance. Each pipeline stage writes its own machine-checkable artifacts: the ontology, the statistical analysis conclusion, the four-piece diagnosis, the judge's ten-criterion feedback, the auditors' ENDORSED verdicts, the report, the page model (render_manifest.json) behind the HTML deliverable, and the reviewer's independent verdict. Every scored verdict carries an explicit provenance label (judge-agent-10-criteria, auditor-agent, html-reviewer-agent), so downstream consumers can distinguish independently authored judgements from rendered ones.
(C4) Gate-graded scoring. The grader accepts a scenario only if its run directory contains the complete artifact set of an executed pipeline (the fourteen items in Table 4), the event log passes the log checker, and the finalisation gate returns PASS. Otherwise the scenario is reported as not executed --a pipeline failure, not a benchmark result. The gate is exercised in practice: an earlier protocol revision allowed a scripted shortcut that expanded a single reasoning note into a full artifact set (complete with synthesised agent events); the internal integrity audit flagged the fabrication, the shortcut was deleted from the codebase, and the entire benchmark was re-executed through the real pipeline. The grader's completeness gate makes the shortcut permanently unreplayable.
(C5) Artifact-consistency gate. A verifier recomputes all aggregate metrics from the per-scenario gradings, re-checks dataset hashes, verifies case coverage, and requires an execution proof per run; any drift fails the evaluation. The gate verifies the internal consistency of the recorded execution--recomputed aggregates, hashes, coverage, proofs--and is deliberately not an independent re-execution of the reasoning layer; the released report carries the resulting internally-consistent verdict.
Execution form. The canonical benchmark runs reported here were executed in the production dispatch form: an orchestrating agent dispatched each pipeline stage to an independent sub-agent session through the harness's subagent spawn tool, passing absolute paths (SKILL_PATH, RUN_DIR); sub-agents communicated only through workspace artifacts, never through conversation context, and each authored its own artifacts under its own skill protocol. The pipeline's one mandatory parallel step (judge and pre-auditor, Steps 5a/5b) executed as two concurrent sessions. The orchestrator's role was limited to dispatch, gate verification, and event bookkeeping--it authored no pipeline artifact and no agent event. The benchmark driver itself retains the anti-fabrication division of labour: its scripts verify and score; where artifacts were missing it printed an execution contract and refused to grade. The event logs record every agent start/completion with declared outputs, and the log checker validates ordering and declared files per run. This form is the property that makes the

Reproducibility semantics. The framework distinguishes two layers explicitly. The deterministic statistical stage is byte-reproducible: identical inputs and code regenerate identical digests, and the released dataset fingerprints pin the inputs. The reasoning layer is reproducible at the procedure level: reexecuting the protocol re-launches the same gated pipeline on the same blind inputs and re-derives conclusions under the same gates and truth comparison; wording may vary between runs, but every verdict remains bound to the same evidence discipline. Reproduction claims in this paper therefore distinguish the two layers, and bit-level reproducibility is claimed only for the deterministic stage.
Scenario splits follow the entity-level discipline recommended by recent benchmark studies [18, 19]: each experimental batch/file appears in exactly one scenario, controls are paired with faults per dataset, and no segment-level splitting is used anywhere.

6. Experimental Setup
6.1. Datasets and Scenarios We assemble 12 scenarios from three public benchmarks, listed in Table 5.
SKAB [47] is a closed water-circulation testbed with vibration, current, pressure, temperature, and flow channels at 1 Hz. Its two fault scenarios are inletvalve throttling (the official label file names suction-line valve restriction) and

17

two-phase-flow cavitation drawn from the "other" group, whose official label names cavitation but is withheld from the scenario description; the anomaly-free control completes the set. TEP [12, 48, 6] contributes six documented fault scenarios: IDV1 feed-composition step, IDV3 D-feed-temperature step (classically hard to detect), IDV4 reactor-cooling-water step, IDV7 C-header pressure loss, IDV11 random cooling-water variation, and IDV14 cooling-valve stiction, plus the fault-free baseline as control. The TEP scenario briefs declare the anomalyonset window (sample 161, idx 160 zero-based), so detection is out of scope for these scenarios and the task is RCA within a localised window; the IndPenSim briefs declare no onset. IndPenSim [49, 50] is a 100,000 L penicillin fermentation simulator; its documented process-deviation batch is 93, whose logged deviation is actuator-side (out-of-band pH and temperature excursions driven by the acid-dosing and heating-water flows--a description taken from the benchmark case definition, not from the paper's diagnosis), with one normal batch as control; the pipeline's cooling-loop signature for batch 93 is evaluated against this documented deviation in Section 7. Ground truth derives from verifiable sources only: SKAB's official label file, the established TEP cause table [9], and IndPenSim's faulted-batch markers. Scenario selection followed a documentation rule rather than random sampling: every scenario corresponds to a publicly documented fault episode, chosen so that each dataset contributes mechanisms from distinct families plus a paired control; the consequences of non-random selection are discussed in Section 9. The control briefs mirror the fault briefs' structure but differ in what they disclose: the SKAB control's process description is identical to its fault sibling's except that the fault brief announces an anomaly (onset withheld), while the TEP and IndPenSim control briefs state that the segment is the fault-free baseline--just as the corresponding fault briefs state that an anomaly exists. Only the SKAB control is therefore blind: the zero-false-alarm outcome shows that the pipeline does not fabricate faults under an honest brief, and the SKAB control additionally exercises blind discrimination (its strong raw correlations are refuted, Section 7.4).
Three design decisions shaped the subset. First, the TEP subset is chosen so that every fault maps onto a published per-fault outcome from FaultExplainer [1], enabling scenario-granular comparison rather than aggregate-only comparison: four faults (IDV1/7/11/14) carry scored baselines, and IDD additionally scores the two faults that screen excludes, IDV3 and IDV4. Second, IDV3 sits at the classical PCA detection limit and IDV4 is weakly detected [8, 9]--both lie where classical detection and the published baseline stop--so they probe rootcause reasoning at and beyond that limit. Third, IDV4, IDV11 and IDV14 perturb the same physical channel (reactor cooling water) with three different dynamic forms (step, random variation, valve stiction); separating them requires reading the statistics of each recording, not recalling a fault label, which partially mitigates prompt-knowledge contamination.

6.3. Comparison Protocol
We distinguish two comparison registers. Register A (same-task direct comparison): methods that output one root-cause conclusion per scenario on the same dataset family; the published FaultExplainer per-fault numbers fall here, at scenario granularity. Register B (reference only): sample-level detection/classification results; they are reported for context and must not be ranked against our scenario-level metrics. Register A comparisons inherit two protocol differences: FaultExplainer supplies candidate root causes in its prompt and accepts alias matches, whereas IDD uses no candidates and scores mechanism keywords strictly; and FaultExplainer scores only the 11 PCA-detectable faults, whereas IDD additionally scores the two faults that screen excludes, IDV3/IDV4. Because the scenario sets differ, no significance test is claimed across systems.
6.4. Implementation
The deterministic stages run as versioned scripts (streaming CSV inspection, anomaly profiling, correlation with lag-compensated cross-correlation functions (CCF), validation with Simpson/detrending/outlier-sensitivity/multiple-testing checks, physics checking via the shared scientific Python environment). The reasoning stages execute the full nine-stage agent pipeline under their skill protocols (Section 3); every stage transition is recorded in the event log. The twelve canonical scenarios were executed as concurrent batches under one orchestrating agent (per-run event spans reflect orchestration batching, not isolated agent time); the clean per-scenario figure is the fully sequential random-draw re-execution (Section 8.4; 2.0 h end to end). The deterministic stage takes seconds per scenario. The entire chain (prepare, briefs, pipeline-execution verification, gategraded scoring, rubric, aggregation, artifact-consistency gate, report) is driven by a single entry point with fail-fast stage gates.

8.4. Verdict Stability: a Random-Draw Independent Re-execution
The evaluation framework formalises consistency testing as a draw-then-reexecute protocol: a scenario is selected by a seeded uniform random draw over the nine-fault pool (so the selection is reproducible and fixed before comparison, though the seed originates from the evaluation harness rather than an external randomness beacon--a limitation we state alongside the draw record) and then re-executed through the full gated pipeline in a fresh run directory with fresh
35

agent sessions (new ontology build, new statistical stage, new diagnosis, new review cycle, new HTML build and review); the draw record is append-only and replayable from the released seed. In the current corpus the draw selected TEP IDV11 (seed 1003818694; the record carries the pool, the raw uniform value, and the resulting index), and the drawn re-execution completed all nine stages end to end in 2.0 h, passing every gate (finalisation pass). The framework's rule is that the latest proven execution of a scenario is its canonical graded run, so the re-execution (0.60) superseded the original batch execution (0.62) in every aggregate; both are capped competing_set verdicts with the same signature, so the supersession changes no headline number. Fig. 10 maps the full audit.
The two executions of the drawn scenario--the corpus's single repeat pair (n=1): the original batch execution 202609171639527 (run 1) and the drawn re-execution 202609172247007 (run 2, now canonical)--agree on everything the protocol measures at the mechanism level: the verdict state (capped competing_set in both), the implicated actuator (XMV_10, the reactor cooling-water valve), the mechanism class (environment-side cooling disturbance), and the top-ranked hypothesis (a cooling-water-side disturbance rather than a valve-internal fault), with the two sessions' confidence estimates 0.02 apart (0.62 vs 0.60, both under the 0.65 INDISTINGUISHABLE cap). Both sessions also agree on the discriminating evidence: the variance-burst topology with frozen means and the actuator-leads-temperature lead structure; the retest additionally tightened the causal-direction argument (lag-5 r=-0.636 with the closed-loop lag-0 sign flip formally barred from causal citation) and bounded the excluded feed and condenser families more sharply. The consistency audit marks the pair consistent--the current corpus holds exactly one case-version pair, and within-era agreement is therefore 1/1 with zero divergences. The agreement is decided at the structured level (verdict type, equipment tag, mechanism class); the two sessions' free-text mechanism strings share only 0.14 of cause tokens, and the re-run's specific form ranks within the same cooling branch--wording drifts, the structure does not. One consistent pair is the beginning of a stability study, not its end. The agreement rule evaluates the coarsest structured fields (verdict type, equipment tag, mechanism class), and the drawn scenario is one whose deterministic signature--a variance burst of ratio 40 with frozen means--makes structural agreement close to mechanical, since both sessions run the same statistics stage; a draw landing on IDV3 or a SKAB case would have stressed the rule harder. With one draw, the protocol's evidential yield is itself unmeasured. The supersession rule (latest proven execution is canonical) was fixed before comparison and will not be applied selectively to disfavour re-runs; the remaining eleven fault-or-control scenarios each hold a single current-era execution, the audit lists them as outstanding re-run contracts rather than assuming their stability, and the released consistency report enumerates them. For TEP IDV4--a fault excluded from FaultExplainer's scoring by its PCA implementation, and only weakly detected classically (T 2 48.8% vs SPE 100% in our deterministic replication) [8]--the recorded chain reads: (i) L3 statistical evidence: at fault onset the reactor cooling valve XMV_10 takes a sustained +6.8 step (41.1%44.9% with no ramp) while reactor temperature spikes to z=+10.5 with a transient on its sister thermocouple (+2.0/ - 2.3) and ev-

ery loop-external column stays flat (the separator-outlet column at -0.08)--a loop-localised cooling triad; (ii) L5 mechanism, with a necessity argument: an energy balance on the reactor heat-removal loop shows the observed coolantflow rise with conserved reactor temperature algebraically requires an upward shift in the cooling-water inlet temperature (or, indistinguishably, a sudden drop in exchanger UA)--a controller compensating exactly as the physics demands, not a sensor artifact; (iii) eliminations: feed-side, condenser, purge, and ramp-form alternatives are quantitatively excluded (each at 95% confidence with cited contradictions), and the feed-composition channel shows no in-window signature; (iv) conclusion: capped competing_set at 0.63 (INDISTINGUISHABLE ceiling 0.65) between "cooling-water inlet-temperature step" (top-ranked) and "sudden UA loss". The recording contains no cooling-water inlet measurement; the verdict names it, and the audit trail quantifies the fork: conserving the loop temperatures under the UA sub-case requires a pre-existing reactor-to-coolant temperature approach below 1.5 K (implying a coolant inlet above 93 °C, against the 26 K approach observed in the pre-onset data), which strongly disfavours the pure-UA form--though it stays below the quantitative elimination threshold because a UA loss can redistribute the loop's LMTD rather than move the observed temperatures, which is precisely why the verdict is a capped competing set rather than a determination. The quantification sharpens the ranking within the cap. The full chain, together with the physics-check verdict, the judge's ten-criterion scores (97/100), and the independent HTML review (pass), is persisted in the grading record and released with the evaluation. We read the cap as the finding: on a fault the published baseline cannot even score, the pipeline localises the loop, states the physically necessary mechanism family, quantifies why the two remaining sub-mechanisms cannot be separated, and names the single measurement--the cooling-water inlet thermocouple--whose installation would collapse the set to determined.
9. Discussion, Limitations, and Deployment Prospects
Statistical power and single-execution evidence. With n=9 fault scenarios, Top-1 = 44.4% supports only a wide interval ([18.9%, 73.3%] at 95% confidence); the top-k bound (9/9, [70.1%, 100%]) is the stronger statement the current sample size can support, and it is a lower bound on resolution only because the keyword instrument is strict (Section 6). Scenario selection was purposive rather than random: the suite over-represents documented, public fault episodes, which biases difficulty downward and buys mechanism diversity across families rather than within them, so the intervals in Table 6 read as measurement uncertainty on this corpus, not sampling generalisation to a scenario population. Two qualifications attach to these figures. First, every headline number is a single recorded execution per scenario--except TEP IDV11, whose drawn re-execution (Section 8.4) reproduced the verdict state and mechanism at confidence 0.02, the only repeat measurement the corpus currently holds. Second, the bounded repair loop introduces a selection effect, since a reported verdict may be the product of up to five attempts; in this corpus, however,
38

twelve of thirteen runs closed without diagnosis-layer repair, the single exception being disclosed with the configuration (Section 6). The model-controlled ablation (Section 8.3) already bounds the accuracy claim (9/9 bare versus 4/9 resolved)--so the pipeline's value on documented faults is process quality, not accuracy. Expanding the repeats (all 12 scenarios, k  3: verdict-type stability, confidence spread, repair counts) remains pre-registered, with eleven scenarios awaiting their second in-era execution--gaps we report rather than fill; extending the corpus (the full 20-class TEP catalogue with the extended simulation dataset [52]; bearing datasets; novel-disturbance suites) is the designed path to tighter intervals.
Prompt-knowledge contamination. TEP's documented fault mechanisms appear in textbooks and are therefore plausibly memorised by frontier models. Our protocol mitigates this at the level where memorisation actually operates--but the mitigation has a limit. The documented cause table records not only the disturbed channel but each fault's dynamic form (IDV4 step, IDV11 random variation, IDV14 stiction), so recall of that table alone could reproduce the observed mechanism labels; the statistics-driven reading rests on the recorded reasoning chains, each of which cites the per-recording statistic that discriminates its form. Two residual channels are disclosed. First, the scenario identifier: every TEP case id and run-directory name embeds the documented mechanism and dynamic form (tep_d14_reactor_valve_sticking names the answer outright), so identifier recall is possible in principle across the whole documented catalogue--IDV14, one of the four resolved verdicts, is the sharpest case. The leakage sentinel machine-checks brief and user-context content, not directory names, so the identifier channel sits outside it. The joint worst case is stated rather than implied: under identifier recall on all TEP-derived outcomes together with the identity-strict batch-93 discount, resolved Top-1 could be as low as 1/9; in Table 6 and Fig. 4 the three TEP-derived resolved verdicts should be read as a recall-possible tier until the neutralised-identifier re-run executes. That re-run (six TEP scenarios under opaque hash run identifiers and identifier-stripped brief headers, 12 h of agent time by the cost accounting below) is committed as the first post-revision execution; it requires a contained harness extension (hash-id run directories) tracked in the released repository. The briefs themselves carry no fault vocabulary (leakage-sentinel checked), and the form-discrimination evidence of Section 7.3 is the main evidence-backed mitigation among the disclosed residual channels: the mechanism labels the pipeline commits to are read off per-recording statistics, which identifier recall would not require. Under the identifier channel alone, recall could supply both mechanism and dynamic form, so all four TEP-derived outcomes--including the resolved IDV1/IDV7/IDV14--carry the recall-possible tier until the re-run executes. Second, the SKAB "other"-group scenario, whose fault label is withheld entirely from all natural-language brief content, still yielded the cavitation-type mechanism ranked among the survivors of a capped verdict. The RAG channel was unavailable in every run (Section 3.2), and no L6-tagged item exists anywhere in the released corpus: no verdict rests on external evidence. A noveldisturbance suite (combination perturbations absent from the literature) is the

definitive control and remains under construction. What the five capped verdicts mean. The five competing_set out-
comes are the protocol working as designed, and they are also the benchmark's most transferable finding: each verdict localises the disturbance to a subsystem, eliminates whole mechanism families with quantitative contradictions, keeps the true mechanism ranked among the survivors, and names the one measurement whose addition would collapse the set to determined (Section 7.4). In deployment terms these are not failures but work orders for targeted instrumentation, and the three-state protocol is what converts a diagnosis into that engineering workflow: determined conclusions carry mechanism chains that point to corrective action; capped conclusions carry the named channel. To keep the framing falsifiable, the released scenario definitions record the expected verdict set of every scenario, fixed before execution; SKAB inlet-valve throttling is the one scenario whose expectation was determined-only, and its capped verdict is counted as the benchmark's single compliance miss rather than narrated as a success.
Inference authorship and provenance. Every scored artifact in this benchmark is authored by a gated pipeline stage--the diagnosis by the diagnostician, the quality verdict by the judge, the ENDORSED decision by the physical auditor, the review verdict by the independent HTML reviewer--and each carries a provenance label in the grading record. The runs reported here executed in the production dispatch form (independent per-stage sub-agent sessions; Section 5), so the reviewing stages were physically separate sessions from the authoring stages--separate in session and protocol, though sharing the one model family (Section 5). Because self-declared quality scores are not verifiable, the benchmark's reported quality metric is the deterministic compliance rubric (Section 6)--and the rubric, the grading keywords, and the allowed verdict sets remain author-designed instruments measuring conformance to this paper's own protocol; their release with the repository makes them auditable, but independent anchoring (the blinded expert adjudication of the released evidence chains) is the outstanding validation step--so the "process quality" margins claimed for the pipeline are architectural properties, not yet measured outcomes. A further provenance caveat: the L1­L7 levels are asserted by the reasoning stage itself; the machine gate checks ceiling arithmetic and grounding, not the correctness of a label, so a mislabelled evidence item could inflate its own ceiling--a disclosed residual risk whose mechanical binding (L1/L3  ingestion/statistics files, L5  ontology nodes) is a toolchain next step. An internal integrity audit that found (and removed) a scripted shortcut capable of fabricating artifacts and agent events is itself part of the released history; the completeness gate now makes that shortcut unreplayable. The audit plane's trust boundary is internal: the event log proves process conformance, not external attestation--a compromised execution layer could still fabricate events--which is why the grader's division of labour (scripts verify, never author) and the artifact cross-checks carry the weight. What is still missing is a comparison against human experts on identical digests; an expert-vs-pipeline blind test is planned.

The economic comparison is only meaningful against the engineering cost of obtaining root causes (expert hours), and the ontology-asset layer (Section 3.2) amortises the reasoning cost across a plant's scenario fleet.
9.1. Industrial Deployment Prospects
We separate what the benchmark and one operational deployment demonstrate from what the architecture projects.
Observed in one deployment (artifacts anonymised and not part of the released corpus): a film-line run exercised the pipeline end to end on episodic scratch defects of a biaxially-oriented polyester film line and returned needs_data with an explicit discriminating-data list after 180 parameter­ defect associations failed false-discovery-rate correction, redirecting the investigation from parameter tuning toward surface-inspection and equipment-event data; no cause was fabricated. Projected: the integration, sovereignty, and documentation claims below are design properties of the architecture, not yet benchmarked outcomes.
Integration with existing plant infrastructure. IDD consumes what plants already produce: flat exports from historians (OPC-UA/PI), LIMS quality records, and batch logs. The same pipeline runs interactively through a web application and programmatically through a versioned REST API (/api/diagnosis with engine selection and typed error contracts), so a deployment is an export job plus API integration rather than an instrumentation project--the integration work is real but bounded by the historian export format. Because the deterministic stage is byte-reproducible, results do not drift when the surrounding plant IT systems change.
Data-sovereign deployment. The fourteen-engine execution layer (Section 3) accepts on-premise and private-model agents through uniform dialect adapters. For operators who cannot send process data to external LLM services--defence, pharmaceuticals, semiconductors, national infrastructure-- the reasoning stage can be pointed at an internally hosted model without touching the audit, evidence, or gate contracts--no non-default engine has yet executed the full gated pipeline in the released corpus, so this path is design-level, like the amortisation projection; the benchmark protocol itself executes identically against a scripted mock engine, which is how the full chain is exercised in CI.
41

Documentation for regulated operations. Because every conclusion is bound to graded evidence, physics checks, and a fingerprinted execution trace, the by-product of each run is an RCA report whose traceability serves the documentation expectations of regulated environments (e.g., GxP batch-deviation investigation, in the sense of ALCOA+-style data integrity [53] and GAMP 5 computerised-systems thinking [54]). This is a documentation property of the artifact, not a computerised-system validation claim; the latter would require a formal qualification exercise.
Deployment governance and knowledge compounding. IDD is positioned as decision support: a determined verdict does not authorise maintenance action--a human engineer signs off. In the current release the sign-off is procedural (no separate human-endorsement artifact exists in the run contract, and all thirteen benchmark runs were fully autonomous); we disclose this as an implementation gap, not a deployed control, and the three-state protocol is precisely the escalation interface between machine conclusion and human decision. Model-change management rides the engine layer's version disclosure (Section 6): a provider silently updating the reasoning model changes the system, so the benchmark configuration disclosure doubles as the deployment pinning record. Finally, the ontology layer compounds knowledge across runs (Section 3.2): all thirteen benchmark runs published their ontologies to the store on completion, and every field incident becomes a candidate graded scenario, closing the loop between deployment and evaluation. In deployment the marginal cost of a diagnosis therefore falls as the fleet grows (projected: the benchmark pinned full rebuilds, so this amortisation is design-level, not measured)--an economics that sample-level detectors, which must be retrained per asset, do not share.
Scope of applicability. The method requires multivariate time-ordered process data with engineering semantics for the channels (to build the ontology) and applies to continuous, batch, and testbed processes alike--the three benchmark families were chosen to span exactly that range. It does not apply where no time-aligned process data exists, and its root-cause reach is bounded by the sensor set, as the capped verdicts demonstrate.
10. Conclusion
We presented IDD, an agentic diagnosis pipeline that turns LLM-based rootcause analysis into an auditable engineering procedure--competing hypotheses with mechanism classes, evidence levels bounding reported confidence, a four-condition anti-spurious filter, physical verification, and quality gates with bounded repair--together with a machine-checked evaluation contract for diagnosis benchmarks: truth isolation, data fingerprinting, stage-authored artifacts with provenance, and gate-graded scoring. On the 12-scenario corpus the headline outcome is deliberately modest: 4/9 faults resolved Top-1, the true mechanism ranked in every fault scenario's surviving set (top-k 9/9; 8/9 when a survivor must carry the specific form or stream branch, not just the family), zero false alarms, five capped verdicts that each name the measurement that
42

would settle them, and every number backed by an execution proof. The modelcontrolled ablation bounds what the pipeline can claim: a bare call to the same model already exceeds its accuracy (9/9 versus 4/9), so the contribution is the auditable process rather than the accuracy. One seeded re-execution reproduced verdict state, actuator, and mechanism class within two confidence points; the deterministic suite reproduced byte-for-byte; and the per-fault TEP comparison shows IDD resolving the GPT-4o baseline's IDV1 miss while scoring the PCAexcluded pair that baselines exclude, as capped verdicts with the mechanism family ranked. These claims have limits: resolution of documented faults is the pipeline's weak axis by its own ablation (a bare same-model call scores 9/9 versus the pipeline's 4/9 resolved; accuracy is not the contribution), results are single recorded executions of a stochastic reasoning layer whose configuration is disclosed and whose run identifiers carry the documented mechanism labels (so the ranking claim is not recall-blind), the machine gate's flat 0.90 non-CS ceiling flags two verdicts (0.91 and 0.93) that the agent-side cap set permits, so the two cap sets' gap is measured rather than hidden; and the rubric and keywords are author-designed instruments with independent expert anchoring outstanding. The evidence base is one model family at one deployment on twelve public-dataset scenarios; cross-model and cross-plant generalisation of the process guarantees (in contrast to the accuracy findings, which the ablation already bounds) remains to be demonstrated. The evaluation framework, per-scenario evidence chains, and consistency artifacts are released to support scrutiny and reuse; the pre-registered repeat and ablation studies, and the novel-disturbance suite, are the immediate next steps.
