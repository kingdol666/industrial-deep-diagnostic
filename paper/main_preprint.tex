\title{Trustworthy agentic diagnosis for industrial processes: evidence-graded competing-hypothesis reasoning evaluated against per-fault literature baselines}

\author[ustc]{ZhangHaoZheng}
\address[ustc]{Institute of Advanced Technology, University of Science and Technology of China, Hefei 230088, China}
\ead{zhanghaozheng@ustc.edu.cn}

\begin{frontmatter}
\begin{abstract}
Large language model (LLM) based diagnosis promises root-cause analysis of industrial processes without task-specific training, yet two obstacles keep it out of engineering practice: conclusions that cannot be audited, and evaluation protocols that score sample-level classification while engineers need scenario-level, mechanism-grounded root causes. We present the Industrial Deep Diagnostic (IDD) pipeline: nine-stage agentic orchestration (ontology, deterministic statistics, competing-hypothesis reasoning, physical verification, bounded-repair gates) with evidence grading (L1--L7), an anti-spurious-correlation filter, and a machine-checked evaluation framework of stage-authored, provenance-labelled artifacts. In single recorded executions on a 12-scenario benchmark from three public datasets (SKAB, Tennessee Eastman Process, IndPenSim), IDD resolves 4/9 documented faults to a single root cause (Top-1 $44.4\%$; a hit certifies mechanism-family agreement, never identity, and the three TEP-derived hits remain identifier-recall-possible); the true mechanism is named within the primary finding or surviving-hypothesis set in all nine (top-$k$ 9/9, a set-membership bound); the five unseparable cases return capped \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}} verdicts naming the discriminating measurement; no control raises a false alarm. A model-controlled ablation bounds every accuracy claim: a bare call to the same model family over the same blind digest keyword-scores 9/9 faults (no verdict typing)---more than the pipeline resolves---so the pipeline's margins are process qualities, not accuracy. One seeded random draw selected a fault scenario for an independent re-execution (one repeat pair), reproducing the verdict state, actuator, and mechanism class within two confidence points; the deterministic suite arms reproduce all 45 output files byte-identically (timestamps excluded); internal consistency is machine-verified.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Root-cause analysis (RCA) of industrial process anomalies is a knowledge-intensive activity: the engineer must decide \emph{which} of many correlated sensor deviations represents the true underlying cause, justify that judgement with evidence, and communicate it with a calibrated degree of confidence. Three long-standing obstacles keep that automation unsatisfactory in practice.

\textbf{First, single-LLM diagnosis is unstructured and unauditable.} Recent evaluations of LLM-based fault explanation on the Tennessee Eastman Process (TEP)~\cite{khan2024faultexplainer} show that large models can articulate plausible mechanisms, but their answers arrive as free-form text with no machine-checkable structure, no separation of facts from inference, and no mechanism that prevents a confident-sounding conclusion from resting on a spurious correlation. A recent review of agentic approaches to process monitoring and fault diagnosis concludes that evidence for LLM/multi-agent fault diagnosis remains scarce~\cite{agentic2025review}: the direction is promising but lacks engineering-grade pipelines.

\textbf{Second, classical data-driven fault detection and diagnosis (FDD) optimises the wrong target.} Principal-component methods~\cite{kano2002comparison}, deep anomaly detectors~\cite{tuli2022tranad,pozdnyakov2024adversarial}, and their benchmarks~\cite{rieth2017additional} score \emph{per-sample} detection or classification~\cite{qin2012survey}. An engineer, however, asks a \emph{per-episode} question: given this anomalous run, what is the root cause, how confident are we, and what evidence supports it? A 99\%-accurate classifier that returns a fault label without mechanism, evidence, or calibration does not answer that question. The detection--diagnosis gap is not merely theoretical: classical detectability studies place the documented disturbances IDV3, IDV9 and IDV15 of the fifteen-fault TEP catalogue at the detection limit, with IDV4 weakly detected by the $T^2$ statistic~\cite{russell2000delay,chiang2001fault}, and the published LLM baseline's own PCA implementation leaves all four of these faults unscored~\cite{khan2024faultexplainer}.

\textbf{Third, correlation is not causation.} In recirculating process loops, almost every pair of variables is correlated through control action or material balance. An RCA method that treats strong correlation as causation will confidently report control-loop artifacts as root causes. Despite decades of causal-inference literature~\cite{yuan2014root,bauer2007finding}, contemporary LLM-diagnosis studies rarely include explicit anti-spurious safeguards.

This paper addresses the three obstacles with the Industrial Deep Diagnostic (IDD) pipeline and a benchmark built to evaluate it honestly. Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A quality-gated agentic diagnosis pipeline} that couples deterministic statistical tooling with LLM reasoning under a competing-hypotheses protocol (Section~\ref{sec:method}), bounded repair loops, and automatic physical verification, so that every conclusion is decomposable into hypothesis, evidence, exclusion, and confidence.
\item \textbf{An evidence-grading and calibration scheme} (levels L1--L7 with gate propagation) that bounds reported confidence by the weakest supporting evidence level. In the corpus none of the \mbox{\textsc{determined}} verdicts is wrong, and in every scenario whose data cannot separate the surviving mechanisms the pipeline returns a capped \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}} verdict that keeps the true mechanism ranked and names the discriminating measurement instead of forcing a conclusion (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\item \textbf{A reproducible evaluation framework}---truth isolation, data fingerprinting, artifact-provenance labels, recorded execution traces, and a machine-checked artifact-consistency gate---in which a scenario is graded only if its complete artifact set was authored by gated pipeline stages and the execution proof passes; an internal integrity audit removed a scripted artifact-fabrication shortcut, and the benchmark was re-executed end to end under the tightened contract (Section~\ref{sec:framework}).
\item \textbf{A 12-scenario benchmark} on three public datasets (SKAB, TEP, IndPenSim) whose TEP subset carries scored per-fault baselines from FaultExplainer~\cite{khan2024faultexplainer} on four faults (the remaining two lie where its PCA screen excludes them). IDD resolves 4/9 faults to a single root cause while ranking the true mechanism in \emph{every} fault scenario's surviving set (top-$k$ 9/9) and raising zero false alarms on the controls; a seeded random-draw re-execution reproduced one scenario's verdict state and mechanism (Section~\ref{sec:stability}); and the graded runs executed as true per-stage sub-agent dispatches communicating only through workspace artifacts (Sections~\ref{sec:framework}, \ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:comparison}).
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:related} positions the work against data-driven FDD, LLM-based diagnosis, and multi-agent orchestration research. Sections~\ref{sec:arch} and \ref{sec:method} detail the architecture and methodology. Section~\ref{sec:framework} defines the evaluation framework. Sections~\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:comparison} present the benchmark and comparisons, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses limitations and deployment prospects.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Data-Driven Fault Detection and the Benchmark Gap}
Statistical process monitoring on the Tennessee Eastman Process established the dominant evaluation template~\cite{downs1993plant,qin2012survey}: principal-component analysis (PCA) and projection methods with $T^2$/Q statistics~\cite{kano2002comparison}, later extended by deep anomaly detectors~\cite{hartung2023deep,tuli2022tranad} and adversarial robustness studies~\cite{pozdnyakov2024adversarial}. Within the tripartite partition of fault detection and diagnosis into quantitative-model-based, qualitative-model-based, and process-history methods~\cite{venkatasubramanian2003review,venkatasubramanian2003part2,venkatasubramanian2003part3}, these families occupy the process-history branch, and the quantitative branch is anchored by model-based analytical redundancy and residual-based fault isolation~\cite{isermann2005model}. All of these branches score \emph{sample-level} detection or \emph{fault-class} accuracy. Critically, they do not evaluate root-cause \emph{mechanisms}: a classifier that labels a run ``IDV(4)'' provides the class identity but neither a causal mechanism, an evidence trail, nor a confidence statement. The classical detection ceiling (IDV3/9/15 at the limit, IDV4 weak) is documented~\cite{russell2000delay,chiang2001fault}. Benchmark methodology has drawn scrutiny: random segment splits inflate reported AUROC---by up to 15.3 percentage points of Macro-AUROC under segmentation-level leakage in Vieira et al.~\cite{vieira2026towards}, with a lineage formalised by Hendriks et al.~\cite{hendriks2022towards}---motivating our entity-level scenario splits (Section~\ref{sec:framework}). Scenario-level root-cause localisation itself has a classical lineage that IDD must be read against: Granger-causal and transfer-entropy methods localise propagation directions in plant-wide oscillations~\cite{yuan2014root,bauer2007finding}, signed-directed-graph reasoning performs mechanism-chain elimination~\cite{iri1979algorithm}, and digital-twin practice (surveyed in~\cite{tao2019digital}) mirrors model-based diagnosis.

\subsection{LLM-Based Industrial Diagnosis}
FaultExplainer~\cite{khan2024faultexplainer} is the closest published system: it couples PCA-based detection with LLM-generated fault explanations on TEP. Its evaluation uses two prompt regimes. In the root-causes-included regime the prompt carries the documented root-cause list, the model outputs a top-3 candidate list, and alias matches are accepted (alias classes: IDV1/2/8, IDV3$\leftrightarrow$IDV9, IDV4$\leftrightarrow$IDV11$\leftrightarrow$IDV14, IDV5$\leftrightarrow$IDV12$\leftrightarrow$IDV15); here GPT-4o resolves 7/11 and o1-preview 9/11 of the 11 PCA-detectable faults (FaultExplainer's own PCA implementation excludes IDV3, IDV4, IDV9 and IDV15 from scoring). In its General-Reasoning regime---no candidate list, designed to simulate unseen faults---both models place the correct or a related cause in the top-3 for 8/11 faults under lenient ``related'' grading (FE's own text names faults 10 and 13 as failing yet reports 8/11; we transcribe the reported figure). Protocol differences therefore condition the comparison: (i) FE's outputs are top-3 lists with alias/related acceptance, whereas IDD commits to a single mechanism verdict scored by strict mechanism keywords; (ii) the two PCA-excluded faults in our subset (IDV3, IDV4) are scored by IDD; (iii) FE's alias policy grades a cooling-water-family answer as correct for IDV11 and IDV14 alike, whereas IDD separates the three forms of the same physical channel. Gong et al.~\cite{gong2026harnessing} orchestrate multiple LLM agents with difficulty-aware routing, but evaluate on a multiple-choice QA benchmark (FailureSensorIQ~\cite{constantinides2025failuresensoriq}) rather than on real time-series data.

Liu et al.~\cite{liu2025probing} combine scene graphs, fine-tuned Llama-3 and augmented reality for machine-tool maintenance, and causal-graph RCA has been studied for time-varying processes~\cite{liu2025spatial} and knowledge-map-based group decision settings~\cite{yue2023root}; a label-free evaluation scheme for diagnosis models has been proposed in the same venue~\cite{liu2024label}. Multimodal LLM frameworks extend the modality range: Alsaif et al.~\cite{alsaif2024multimodal} couple textual, tabular, and image modalities for Industry-4.0 fault detection and diagnosis over industrial fault records, and Merkelbach and Hasenauer~\cite{merkelbach2024multimodal} show that multimodal LLMs can draft physical models for automated diagnosis, within disclosed limits.
Recent work in this journal shows the same momentum and the same gaps.  CausalKGPT couples an industrial causal knowledge graph with an LLM for quality-problem cause analysis~\cite{zhou2024causalkgpt}; FD-LLM fine-tunes a large language model for equipment fault diagnosis~\cite{lin2025fdllm}; Wen et al.\ orchestrate LLMs for human--machine collaborative troubleshooting~\cite{wen2025troubleshooting}; and Xu et al.\ pair a deep digital twin with a vision-language model for multi-scenario fault diagnosis~\cite{xu2026digitaltwin}. Closest to our setting, the contemporaneous AgentRCA (July 2026) builds an agentic RCA system that maintains a hypothesis table over tool-grounded evidence, reporting 40.0\% Top-1 and 61.5\% Top-2 on its TEP evaluation set (it excludes IDV3/9/15 for the classical detectability reasons we discuss~\cite{wei2026agentrca}; its output is a ranked hypothesis list without calibrated confidence, and its evaluation, like FE's, stops short of an external truth-comparison harness, and its detectability exclusion also covers the unspecified-cause faults IDV16--20). Table~\ref{tab:related} summarises the positioning: among published and contemporaneous systems, none combines real time-series inputs, scenario-level mechanism diagnosis with an evidence trail, cap-enforced three-state conclusions, and a machine-verifiable evaluation contract---the last two, in particular, are absent from every system cited above, with AgentRCA naming uncertainty communication as future work. The claim is scoped to industrial process diagnosis; the contemporaneous agentic-RCA line for software microservices targets a different artifact class (distributed traces and logs) and a different evidence regime.

\subsection{Multi-Agent Orchestration Frameworks}
Generic agent frameworks (AutoGen~\cite{wu2023autogen} and commercial derivatives; ReAct-style reasoning--acting loops~\cite{yao2023react}) provide message-passing and role prompting but leave audit, evidence policy, and evaluation contracts to the application. Our position, following the practice of the reviewed agentic-FDD literature~\cite{agentic2025review}, is that industrial diagnosis requires these as \emph{first-class, machine-checked} pipeline elements rather than prompt conventions; Section~\ref{sec:arch} describes how IDD enforces them through checkpoints, bounded repair loops, and an event-log execution proof.

\subsection{Trust, Automation Levels, and Intellectual Ancestry}\label{sec:trust}
IDD's design decisions sit on well-mapped ground, and we credit the map. The three-state verdict is a level-of-automation and trust-calibration decision---what the system concludes versus what it escalates to humans---in the sense of Parasuraman et al.'s types and levels of automation~\cite{parasuraman2000model} and Lee \& See's appropriate-reliance account of trust~\cite{lee2004trust}: a capped \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}} implements calibrated transfer in their sense. The competing-hypotheses discipline descends from Heuer's Analysis of Competing Hypotheses~\cite{heuer1999psychology}, the four-condition anti-spurious filter from Hill's causation criteria~\cite{hill1965environment}, and the abstention option from Chow's recognition-with-reject tradeoff~\cite{chow1970optimum}, whose statistical counterpart is modern selective prediction~\cite{angelopoulos2023conformal}; the L1--L7 grounding check doubles as a faithfulness proxy in the sense of rigorous XAI evaluation~\cite{doshivelez2017rigorous}. The named-discriminating-measurement exit is the passive counterpart of \emph{active} fault diagnosis, which designs inputs to separate faults before they occur~\cite{scott2014input}: IDD inherits the passive setting of plant recording and converts its residual ambiguity into an instrumentation request. The ontology-asset layer continues the process-engineering ontology lineage of OntoCAPE~\cite{morbach2009ontocape}. IDD's contribution with respect to this ancestry is a domain-specific, machine-checked instantiation of these frameworks inside an agentic pipeline---not their invention.

\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

\subsection{Execution Architecture}
The following terms recur throughout. A \emph{skill package} is a versioned folder holding an agent's protocol checklist, JSON Schemas, and validation scripts; a \emph{specialist agent} is an independently spawned session that executes one skill package; an \emph{engine} is the CLI/RPC harness that runs such a session. Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} shows the skill execution architecture as five planes, of which the audit plane is machine-checked. Client surfaces (the web application, CLI drivers, and a CI harness) submit jobs through the engine layer: fourteen interchangeable CLI/RPC engines behind one uniform event contract, with pre-flight probing and typed error codes. The orchestrating main agent holds the domain knowledge as \emph{skill packages} and dispatches each pipeline stage by passing absolute paths (\texttt{SKILL\_PATH}, \texttt{RUN\_DIR}); sub-agents communicate only through workspace artifacts, never through conversation context.

Read against the released software, the layer map needs three qualifications. First, the package contains 18 skill packages executed by 14 specialised agents, but the \emph{diagnostic path} reported here (Steps 2--8.5) dispatches eight specialist sub-agents---context-builder, data-processor, diagnostician, judge, report-reviewer, reporter, html-visualizer, html-reviewer---with the VLM visual analyser dispatched by the data-processor inside its Phase~5.5; the remaining packages form optional layers outside the benchmarked path (Section~\ref{sec:arch-layers}). Second, a skill package is not a prompt alone: every dispatched package carries the protocol checklist (\texttt{references/agent-protocol.md}) and ships the JSON Schemas and validation scripts that machine-check its artifacts before the stage may complete---the structural difference from prompting a model directly. Third, the engine layer gives the pipeline harness portability: all fourteen engines implement one start/stream/close contract, so an engine substitution is a configuration change rather than a re-implementation---pre-registered as an ablation axis, not yet exercised. Every stage transition appends a structured event to the audit plane, which the log checker and the finalisation gate reduce to a single machine verdict; grading (Section~\ref{sec:framework}) reads only artifacts that survived this plane.

\subsection{Knowledge, Web, and Enhancement Layers}\label{sec:arch-layers}
Beyond the diagnostic path sit three further layers; how each was exercised, degraded, or left idle in the benchmark is recorded in the run events.

\emph{Semantic-knowledge layer.} A retrieval-augmented generation microservice~\cite{lewis2020rag} (FastAPI + ChromaDB, \texttt{rag-retrieval-engine/}) supplies semantic understanding for ontology construction and is the only sanctioned source of level-L6 external evidence; the orchestrator probes it with a three-second health check at Step~0. When it is unavailable, the ontology builder falls back to the shipped \texttt{parameter\_to\_physics.json} mechanism map plus the data's own self-description, and the degradation is recorded in the run events. In the benchmark corpus the channel was never available: all thirteen runs record a failed probe and build every ontology through the documented fallback (recorded per run as a dedicated probe-failure file or a \texttt{rag\_validated:false} ontology flag); the TEP ontologies ground their variable semantics in the published process description, cross-checked against the data. No L6-tagged item exists in the released corpus, so no verdict rests on external evidence.

\emph{Ontology asset store.} Ontologies are published to a portable asset store (\texttt{ontology\_store.mjs publish}) keyed by a schema fingerprint; a later scenario whose variable schema matches is resolved by deterministic fast-reuse, which copies the validated ontology verbatim into the run directory in seconds instead of re-deriving it. All thirteen benchmark ontologies were published to the store on completion; none was \emph{consumed} by fast-reuse in this corpus, because the benchmark configuration pins the ontology step to full rebuilds (the reuse path is exercised by the production harness outside this benchmark). The store is deterministic and therefore does not affect the benchmark's truth-isolation contract (it reuses variable semantics, never scenario verdicts).

\emph{Integration surface.} The web application couples an Express backend with a Vue~3/Vite frontend and an SSE/WebSocket live stream. Its diagnosis endpoint accepts an engine selection and returns typed pre-flight errors for unknown or unusable engines, with probing and the real spawn sharing one code path. The optional enhancement layer (E0--E8, deterministic scripts orchestrated by a dedicated agent) post-processes completed runs for reporting depth and lies outside the benchmarked path.

\subsection{Pipeline Overview}
IDD organises diagnosis as a nine-stage pipeline executed by the specialised agents backed by the skill packages (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}; the benchmark's execution form---true per-stage sub-agent dispatch---is disclosed in Section~\ref{sec:framework}). The main orchestrator performs setup and data ingestion (Step~0/1) with adaptive preprocessing; a context-builder agent constructs a process ontology with variable semantics, physical principles, and confounder annotations (Step~2, checkpoint CP-2/3); a data-processor agent runs the deterministic statistical stage (per-variable anomaly profiling, cross-domain correlation, lag-compensated cross-correlation, batch integrity checks), producing a machine-readable analysis conclusion (Step~3, CP-4); a vision--language-model (VLM) agent reads rendered charts as visual evidence (pipeline Step~3.5 executes inside the data-processor agent as its internal Phase~5.5; in this deployment it executed its documented non-visual fallback in every run, Section~\ref{sec:setup}). The diagnostician agent then performs competing-hypothesis reasoning over the statistical and ontological evidence (Step~4, CP-5). Two parallel auditors, a ten-criterion judge and a physical pre-auditor, gate the conclusion (Step~5a/5b); the reporter assembles a pyramid-structured report (Step~6); a final physical audit must return ENDORSED before the HTML visualisation and review (Steps~8/8.5, CP-9 at the review gate) and the evidence-closure finalisation (Step~9).

\textbf{Why nine stages.} Three rules govern the decomposition; the model-controlled ablation (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) independently supports each. Deterministic work is pushed down to scripts: statistics, hashing, schema checks, and the finalisation inventory run as versioned code so they are byte-reproducible, and only semantic judgement (ontology, hypotheses, causal framing, prose) is left to agents---only this split keeps the deterministic layer defensible once a bare model call is known to be accurate. Data description precedes diagnosis: the ontology and the statistics digest are produced before any hypothesis is generated, which is what forces the diagnose-only-what-the-data-supports behaviour observed in the capped verdicts. Audit and delivery are gated last and separately: two independent auditors (judge and physical auditor, the pipeline's only mandatory parallel step) review the reasoning before a report exists, and the HTML deliverable is built manifest-first and reviewed independently, so the final artifacts cannot diverge from the audited verdict. The stage boundaries themselves are the artifact handoffs in Table~\ref{tab:modules}, and the mechanical predicates of the checkpoints are listed in Table~\ref{tab:checkpoints}.

\subsection{Execution Layer: Interchangeable Engines With a Uniform Contract}
Reasoning agents are executed by any of fourteen interchangeable engines---the Claude Agent SDK, several RPC and ACP dialects, and a set of one-shot CLIs---behind a single start/stream/close contract realised as a capability table (\texttt{engines.mjs}) plus first-party wiring entries in the service dispatch table. Engine availability is probed and validated before execution with typed error codes (\texttt{400} unknown engine, \texttt{409 HARNESS\_UNAVAILABLE} unusable engine); a scripted in-process mock engine exercises the full pipeline in CI without external dependencies. Because every engine emits the same standardised event stream, the benchmark harness consumes any compliant engine's stream identically; diagnostic quality across engines is an empirical ablation question, and engine substitution remains a designed but not-yet-exercised ablation axis (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{Audit Infrastructure}
Every stage transition appends a structured event to \texttt{.pipeline\_\allowbreak events.\allowbreak jsonl}; a log checker validates the start and completion records of every agent against the required contract, and a finalisation gate aggregates inventory, schema, closure, judge-cross-audit, and event-archive checks into a single PASS/FAIL verdict. A run that fails any critical check cannot be graded, which is the property the benchmark relies on: \emph{no result exists without an execution proof}. The gate is exercised, not decorative. The released records show the round-1 judge failing TEP IDV3 at 75 with two blocking issues, repaired to a round-2 pass at 90 under the declared scope; the final physical audit caught two report-layer defects (a mis-attributed row reference and a residual over-claim in a run summary), repaired and re-audited; the grading rubric (Section~\ref{sec:setup}) records calibration and grounding deductions in three further runs; and several runs additionally bounced stage artifacts for schema or path-normalisation issues before passing. Every rejection was repaired by the artifact's owning stage and re-gated to PASS; no verdict text changed in any of these repairs. Events are pipeline-internal records, not externally signed attestations; the resulting trust boundary and its limits are stated in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\section{Diagnostic Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
A scenario supplies a recorded window $D_S$ of $p$ process variables and, optionally, user context; the task is to produce a verdict $v \in \{\textsc{det},\textsc{cs},\textsc{nd}\}$ with a mechanism-level root cause, a confidence $c \in [0,1]$, and an evidence chain. The diagnostician forms a hypothesis set $H = \{h_1,\dots,h_k\}$, $k \ge 3$, where each $h_i$ carries a mechanism class $m_i$, a set $E_i$ of evidence items, and a contradiction set $X_i$. Two definitions govern the protocol. The \emph{evidence level} of a hypothesis is the weakest level in its chain, $\ell(h_i) = \min_{e \in E_i} \mathrm{level}(e)$ with $\mathrm{level}(e) \in \{L1,\dots,L7\}$ (Section~\ref{sec:method-grading})---a conclusion cannot be stronger than its weakest citation. The \emph{confidence ceiling} is the minimum of the five-factor assessment and every protocol cap applicable to the verdict state,
\begin{equation}
c \le \bar{c}(v) = \min\bigl(F,\ \mathcal{C}(v)\bigr),
\label{eq:ceiling}
\end{equation}
where $F$ is the five-factor assessment and the cap function $\mathcal{C}(v)$ evaluates to the smallest applicable protocol cap: $0.70$ for \textsc{cs}, tightened to $0.65$ for its indistinguishable subclass, and $0.50$ whenever the anti-oscillation rule (code name \texttt{PARAM\_AMBIGUITY}) is active---all three are documented in the agent-side protocol. The agent-side protocol documents no \textsc{det} cap, but the machine grading gate flags---rather than blocks---confidence above $0.90$ for every non-\textsc{cs} state and above $0.70$ for \textsc{cs} (calibration check R4 of the released rubric; the two 0.91/0.93 verdicts of Section~\ref{sec:overall} are exactly this flag firing), so the effective cap set $\mathcal{C}$ includes $0.90$ for \textsc{det} at the gate (the gate's flat non-CS $0.90$ ceiling also covers \textsc{nd}). Reason-coded per-run ceilings may still bind; the released schema enumerates the reasons: indistinguishable competing set, unsorted-data lag claims, product-specific-only evidence, all-criteria-verified, direct measurement. For example, the canonical IDV7 run binds its \mbox{\textsc{determined}} verdict at $0.78$ under a reason-coded ceiling of $0.80$ (recorded reason \texttt{ALL\_CRITERIA\_VERIFIED}, noting that the root variable, header supply pressure, is unobservable, so the verdict is capped at the justified-inference level). Elimination is quantitative: $h_i$ is eliminated when its contradictions outweigh its support, $|X_i| > |E_i|$ with each item weighted by its evidence level (weights in the released protocol; the machine-checked floor is $\ge$2 eliminations, exclusion confidence $\ge 0.90$, and a citation per elimination), and every elimination records a revival condition. The three-state verdict is then the decision function
\begin{equation}
v = \begin{cases}
\textsc{det}, & |H_{\mathrm{surv}}| = 1 \ \text{and every elimination is evidenced},\\
\textsc{cs}, & |H_{\mathrm{surv}}| \ge 2 \ \text{and no channel separates the survivors},\\
\textsc{nd}, & \ell(h^\star) < L5 \ \text{or a required measurement is absent},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:verdict}
\end{equation}
with the reported confidence satisfying $c \le \bar{c}(v)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:ceiling}). The observed 0.55--0.63 band of capped verdicts reflects this INDISTINGUISHABLE subclass. The five-factor assessment is the protocol's confidence decomposition over statistical strength, physical plausibility, temporal evidence, absence of confounds, and symptom completeness (weighted 25/25/20/20/10, totalling 100, in the released \texttt{confidence.json}); the three states are protocol terms throughout this paper and carry no everyday meaning: \textsc{det} asserts a single evidenced mechanism, \textsc{cs} asserts that the present data cannot separate the survivors and names the measurement that would, and \textsc{nd} declares the evidence insufficient. Algorithm~\ref{alg:pipeline} states the whole procedure as executed, with deterministic (script) and semantic (agent) steps marked; the ablation of Section~\ref{sec:ablation} is the empirical test of this split.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{IDD diagnosis of scenario $S$ (script = deterministic code, agent = semantic reasoning; gates as in Table~\ref{tab:checkpoints})}
\label{alg:pipeline}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\subsection{Competing-Hypotheses Protocol}
The diagnostician agent must generate at least three candidate hypotheses per fault scenario (the protocol's intellectual ancestry is credited in Section~\ref{sec:trust}), assign each a mechanism class (e.g., fluid restriction, cavitation, rotor dynamics, feed-composition step, cooling disturbance, sensor drift), collect for each an evidence list with explicit levels, identify contradictions, and eliminate hypotheses whose contradictions are stronger than their support. The protocol admits exactly three conclusion states: \mbox{\textsc{determined}} (a single surviving hypothesis, bounded by the five-factor assessment; reason-coded per-run ceilings may further bind it), \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}} ($\ge$2 survivors that the data cannot discriminate, with confidence capped at 0.70; observed caps in this corpus are 0.55--0.63 and the discriminating measurement is named), and \mbox{\textsc{needs\_data}} (evidence insufficient; missing data itemised). A fourth, implicit state---normal operation with no confirmed fault---applies to control scenarios. The elimination contract is quantitative: every elimination must cite the contradicting evidence, and every surviving chain must cite evidence numbers present in the statistical digests.

\subsection{Evidence Grading and Gate Propagation}\label{sec:method-grading}
Every piece of evidence carries a level L1--L7 (L1 direct measurement, L2 operating documents, L3 statistical analysis, L4 visual evidence, L5 domain ontology/mechanism, L6 external reference, L7 unsupported assumption). The ordering is a policy choice: direct measurement outranks statistical processing because a sensor reading cannot inherit a processing error, while visual evidence is graded below statistics because chart reading is itself an inference. The scenario confidence is bounded by the weakest level contributing to the surviving chain, and the quality gate flags, for deduction, conclusions whose confidence exceeds that bound. This converts ``hallucination control'' from a prompt instruction into a checkable arithmetic constraint.

\subsection{Anti-Spurious-Correlation Filter}
Four conditions must hold before a correlation may be cited as causal support: (i)~temporal precedence of cause over effect, verified by lag-compensated cross-correlation; (ii)~statistical significance surviving detrending, Simpson-stratification checks, and multiple-testing correction; (iii)~a physical mechanism annotated in the ontology connecting the variables; and (iv)~absence of contradictions under leave-one-out outlier sensitivity analysis, with production-state (steady/batch-phase) filtering applied before correlation. Correlations failing the filter are recorded but explicitly barred from hypothesis support; definitional pairs (e.g., valve position and the level it controls, $r\!=\!1.0$) are classified as control-loop artifacts. In the benchmark this filter is the reason control-loop correlations are not reported as root causes, and the reason normal batches with impulsive recipe-dosing spikes ($|z|$ up to 16.2) are graded as healthy rather than faulty.

\subsection{Automatic Physical Verification}
After the conclusion is drafted, a physics checker recomputes quantitative feasibility (mass/energy balances, thermal and vibration magnitude envelopes) from the cleaned data, ontology, and anomaly report, producing a machine-readable \texttt{physical\_checks} verdict. When no automated check applies to a scenario's column semantics, the pipeline falls back to an explicit L1--L5 manual-verification memo; silent omission is not permitted.

\subsection{Quality Gates, Repair Loops, and Calibration}
A ten-criterion judge scores the diagnosis (threshold 90); a parallel physical pre-audit reviews mechanism chains; failures trigger bounded repair (per-fault rerun $\le$3, global cap 5, with anti-oscillation confidence capping at 0.50). The final physical audit must return ENDORSED. If the review-repair budget exhausts without an endorsement, the run proceeds best-effort and the finalisation gate records the missing endorsement as a critical failure, so the run is a pipeline failure rather than a gradeable result. Ceiling compliance is then auditable end-to-end: every scenario grading records whether the reported confidence respects the protocol ceiling, and \emph{overconfidence} (\mbox{\textsc{determined}} \emph{and} wrong) is a first-class error metric.

The released governance keeps a stochastic reasoner bounded by design. Each repair round snapshots the best-scoring attempt (\texttt{best\_round} copy of the diagnosis set); the second and third rounds run in a declared \emph{repair scope} that recomputes only the implicated artifacts and marks the rest as carried over, so a fix cannot silently rewrite evidence that was not found defective (auditable from the declared repair scopes and carried-over markers in the released logs). The loop never halts the run on score alone: if the threshold is still unmet after the budget, the pipeline delivers a best-effort report with a gate-enforced capped verdict (and the cap recorded), because failing to deliver is worse than delivering with a disclosed confidence. The protocol adds two machine-checkable disciplines. The \emph{data-truth mandate} requires every number in a diagnosis to be reproducible from the raw data (chain items carry derived markers and a NOT-APPLICABLE fallback), and the red-light list makes the principal failure modes auditable: no forced single verdict from a competing set, no global correlation cited as causal evidence, no conclusion without an evidence-level tag, no HTML outside the manifest. Violations are what the judge is instructed to flag, which is how the audit plane of Section~\ref{sec:arch} remains a check rather than a claim.

\section{A Reproducible Evaluation Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

IDD's benchmark framework (released as the project's \texttt{experience/} evidence repository and \texttt{docs/benchmark/} protocol) is designed around five contracts, each machine-enforced:

\textbf{(C1) Truth isolation.} Scenario definitions carry ground truth and grading keywords, but the pipeline's visible inputs contain only the process description and the data. Ground truth never enters the inference context; the grader owns it exclusively. A leakage sentinel machine-checks the contract from both directions: no ground-truth string may appear in any agent-visible brief, and no fault-vocabulary may appear in the run directories' user-context files. Scenario definitions---including the grading keywords and the allowed verdict set of every scenario---are versioned in the released repository with their edit history. The keyword sets used to grade the current corpus are the calibrated set pinned at commit \texttt{221d061}---over-generic tokens removed, mechanism synonyms added---then frozen before any current-era execution. The calibration is truth-adjacent in two ways that Section~\ref{sec:overall} quantifies: the ``v1-era corpus'' it was tuned against denotes the pre-discipline execution batch of the same twelve scenarios (run directories dated before the 2026-09-14 anti-oscillation and confidence-cap revision, retained with the released edit history), and the published per-fault baselines contributed the mechanism synonyms. The sentinel machine-checks briefs and user-context files, not run-directory names; the identifier channel is the residual disclosure channel and is treated in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}. These keys are author-designed (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}): a hit certifies mechanism-family agreement with the documented truth, not identity, and one scenario's set includes a generic token.

\textbf{(C2) Data fingerprinting.} Every input file is hashed (SHA-256) into a dataset manifest; the reproducibility gate re-verifies all 153 input fingerprints and the leakage sentinel returns a PASS verdict before any metric is accepted.

\textbf{(C3) Stage-authored artifacts with provenance.} Each pipeline stage writes its own machine-checkable artifacts: the ontology, the statistical analysis conclusion, the four-piece diagnosis, the judge's ten-criterion feedback, the auditors' ENDORSED verdicts, the report, the page model (\texttt{render\_manifest.json}) behind the HTML deliverable, and the reviewer's independent verdict. Every scored verdict carries an explicit provenance label (\texttt{judge-agent-10-criteria}, \texttt{auditor-agent}, \texttt{html-reviewer-agent}), so downstream consumers can distinguish independently authored judgements from rendered ones.

\textbf{(C4) Gate-graded scoring.} The grader accepts a scenario only if its run directory contains the complete artifact set of an executed pipeline (the fourteen items in Table~\ref{tab:artifacts}), the event log passes the log checker, and the finalisation gate returns PASS. Otherwise the scenario is reported as \emph{not executed}---a pipeline failure, not a benchmark result. The gate is exercised in practice: an earlier protocol revision allowed a scripted shortcut that expanded a single reasoning note into a full artifact set (complete with synthesised agent events); the internal integrity audit flagged the fabrication, the shortcut was deleted from the codebase, and the entire benchmark was re-executed through the real pipeline. The grader's completeness gate makes the shortcut permanently unreplayable.

\textbf{(C5) Artifact-consistency gate.} A verifier recomputes all aggregate metrics from the per-scenario gradings, re-checks dataset hashes, verifies case coverage, and requires an execution proof per run; any drift fails the evaluation. The gate verifies the internal consistency of the recorded execution---recomputed aggregates, hashes, coverage, proofs---and is deliberately not an independent re-execution of the reasoning layer; the released report carries the resulting internally-consistent verdict.

\textbf{Execution form.} The canonical benchmark runs reported here were executed in the production dispatch form: an orchestrating agent dispatched each pipeline stage to an \emph{independent sub-agent session} through the harness's sub-agent spawn tool, passing absolute paths (\texttt{SKILL\_PATH}, \texttt{RUN\_DIR}); sub-agents communicated only through workspace artifacts, never through conversation context, and each authored its own artifacts under its own skill protocol. The pipeline's one mandatory parallel step (judge and pre-auditor, Steps 5a/5b) executed as two concurrent sessions. The orchestrator's role was limited to dispatch, gate verification, and event bookkeeping---it authored no pipeline artifact and no agent event. The benchmark driver itself retains the anti-fabrication division of labour: its scripts verify and score; where artifacts were missing it printed an execution contract and refused to grade. The event logs record every agent start/completion with declared outputs, and the log checker validates ordering and declared files per run. This form is the property that makes the audit plane meaningful: had stages shared one session, the artifact contracts and gates would be unchanged, but the independence between the authoring stage and its reviewers would be weaker than the production design intends. The independence is session- and protocol-level, not model-level: every stage runs on the one model family disclosed in Section~\ref{sec:setup}, so correlated failures between a generator and its reviewer remain possible.

\textbf{Reproducibility semantics.} The framework distinguishes two layers explicitly. The deterministic statistical stage is byte-reproducible: identical inputs and code regenerate identical digests, and the released dataset fingerprints pin the inputs. The reasoning layer is reproducible at the \emph{procedure} level: re-executing the protocol re-launches the same gated pipeline on the same blind inputs and re-derives conclusions under the same gates and truth comparison; wording may vary between runs, but every verdict remains bound to the same evidence discipline. Reproduction claims in this paper therefore distinguish the two layers, and bit-level reproducibility is claimed only for the deterministic stage.

Scenario splits follow the entity-level discipline recommended by recent benchmark studies~\cite{vieira2026towards,hendriks2022towards}: each experimental batch/file appears in exactly one scenario, controls are paired with faults per dataset, and no segment-level splitting is used anywhere.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Datasets and Scenarios}
We assemble 12 scenarios from three public benchmarks, listed in Table~\ref{tab:bench}. \textbf{SKAB}~\cite{skab2020} is a closed water-circulation testbed with vibration, current, pressure, temperature, and flow channels at 1\,Hz. Its two fault scenarios are inlet-valve throttling (the official label file names suction-line valve restriction) and two-phase-flow cavitation drawn from the ``other'' group, whose official label names cavitation but is withheld from the scenario description; the anomaly-free control completes the set. \textbf{TEP}~\cite{downs1993plant,lyman1995plant,rieth2017additional} contributes six documented fault scenarios: IDV1 feed-composition step, IDV3 D-feed-temperature step (classically hard to detect), IDV4 reactor-cooling-water step, IDV7 C-header pressure loss, IDV11 random cooling-water variation, and IDV14 cooling-valve stiction, plus the fault-free baseline as control. The TEP scenario briefs declare the anomaly-onset window (sample 161, idx 160 zero-based), so detection is out of scope for these scenarios and the task is RCA within a localised window; the IndPenSim briefs declare no onset. \textbf{IndPenSim}~\cite{goldrick2015development,goldrick2019modern} is a 100{,}000\,L penicillin fermentation simulator; its documented process-deviation batch is 93, whose logged deviation is actuator-side (out-of-band pH and temperature excursions driven by the acid-dosing and heating-water flows---a description taken from the benchmark case definition, not from the paper's diagnosis), with one normal batch as control; the pipeline's cooling-loop signature for batch 93 is evaluated against this documented deviation in Section~\ref{sec:results}. Ground truth derives from verifiable sources only: SKAB's official label file, the established TEP cause table~\cite{chiang2001fault}, and IndPenSim's faulted-batch markers. Scenario selection followed a documentation rule rather than random sampling: every scenario corresponds to a publicly documented fault episode, chosen so that each dataset contributes mechanisms from distinct families plus a paired control; the consequences of non-random selection are discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}. The control briefs mirror the fault briefs' structure but differ in what they disclose: the SKAB control's process description is identical to its fault sibling's except that the fault brief announces an anomaly (onset withheld), while the TEP and IndPenSim control briefs state that the segment is the fault-free baseline---just as the corresponding fault briefs state that an anomaly exists. Only the SKAB control is therefore blind: the zero-false-alarm outcome shows that the pipeline does not fabricate faults under an honest brief, and the SKAB control additionally exercises blind discrimination (its strong raw correlations are refuted, Section~\ref{sec:calibration}).

Three design decisions shaped the subset. First, the TEP subset is chosen so that \emph{every fault maps onto a published per-fault outcome} from FaultExplainer~\cite{khan2024faultexplainer}, enabling scenario-granular comparison rather than aggregate-only comparison: four faults (IDV1/7/11/14) carry scored baselines, and IDD additionally scores the two faults that screen excludes, IDV3 and IDV4. Second, IDV3 sits at the classical PCA detection limit and IDV4 is weakly detected~\cite{russell2000delay,chiang2001fault}---both lie where classical detection and the published baseline stop---so they probe root-cause reasoning at and beyond that limit. Third, IDV4, IDV11 and IDV14 perturb the same physical channel (reactor cooling water) with three different dynamic forms (step, random variation, valve stiction); separating them requires reading the statistics of each recording, not recalling a fault label, which partially mitigates prompt-knowledge contamination.

\subsection{Metrics}
For fault scenarios we report \textbf{resolved Top-1} (a \mbox{\textsc{determined}} verdict naming the true mechanism; coincides with the FDD community's correct-diagnosis-rate, CDR), \textbf{top-$k$} (the true mechanism is named within the primary finding or the surviving-hypothesis set that the grader reads; because the protocol mandates at least three generated hypotheses, this approximates candidate-list membership, and for a single-survivor \mbox{\textsc{determined}} verdict it coincides with resolved Top-1; reported alongside, not instead of, resolved Top-1), \textbf{ceiling compliance} (reported confidence within the protocol ceiling of Section~\ref{sec:method}), \textbf{verdict-type compliance} (conclusion type within the scenario's allowed set; a \mbox{\textsc{determined}}-and-wrong verdict counts as \emph{overconfidence}), and a \textbf{protocol-compliance rubric score}---a deterministic, machine-computed checklist (100 points) computed from the pipeline artifacts themselves and measuring conformance to this paper's own protocol rather than correctness (see Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). Top-$k$ is structurally favoured when several scenarios in a family share keyword tokens (the three reactor-cooling TEP faults share ``reactor cooling''); read it as family-level membership, not specific-form identification. The strictest, branch-localised reading would count IDV3 as a miss (its D/E-feed-temperature survivor sits on the stripper-feed branch while the documented truth is the reactor-feed D stream), giving top-$k$ 8/9 under that reading---both readings are reported. For control scenarios we report \textbf{pass} (no confirmed fault) and \textbf{false alarms}. Proportions carry Wilson 95\% confidence intervals at $z{=}1.96$ (the released scorer's constant). We do not report sample-level F1/AUC: they answer a different question and are used only as reference context (comparison protocol below).

The rubric's seven checks are computed strictly from the stage-authored run artifacts: \textbf{R1} artifact integrity (ontology, analysis conclusion, and the schema-valid diagnosis four-piece; 15 points), \textbf{R2} hypothesis structure ($\ge$3 hypotheses, $\ge$2 eliminations each citing substantive contradictory evidence; 15), \textbf{R3} evidence grounding (every surviving chain cites at least one number present in the scenario's own evidence brief; 20; fault scenarios---the check has no fault-relevant targets in a control brief, and the one control deduction under it is recorded as a check mis-specification, not a run defect), \textbf{R4} calibration (confidence within the protocol ceiling; conclusion type within the scenario's allowed set; 15), \textbf{R5} falsifiability (a surviving hypothesis names the observation that would revise it; 10), \textbf{R6} physics verification (automated check valid or explicit manual memo; 10), \textbf{R7} execution proof (event log + finalisation PASS + HTML review gate; 15). The pipeline's internal ten-criterion judge remains the in-pipeline gate (Section~\ref{sec:method})---its scores are recorded with provenance and reported as execution-quality evidence---but it is \emph{not} a benchmark metric: benchmark quality scoring is fully mechanical. The rubric's scope is limited in two ways. First, it is a conformance instrument with limited discriminative power: nine of the twelve scenarios score at ceiling because the structural checks cannot fail for any run that has passed the gates---correctness claims rest on the truth comparison, never on the rubric. Second, ``strict mechanism-keyword hit'' is not mechanism equivalence: the keyword sets are disclosed per scenario, one of them (the IndPenSim process-deviation scenario) includes the generic token ``deviation'', and a hit certifies mechanism-family agreement, not identity. The consequences are quantified in Section~\ref{sec:results}, including an identity-strict Top-1 count that discounts the batch-93 verdict.\footnote{Artifact-field semantics for auditors: the released \texttt{metrics.json} folds verdict-type compliance into its \texttt{calibrated} flag, and \texttt{rubric.json} records the SKAB-valve type miss under its calibration-check key (the failing payload is the allowed-set violation described in Section~\ref{sec:calibration}); Table~\ref{tab:overall} reports calibration and verdict-type compliance as separate metrics.}

\subsection{Comparison Protocol}
We distinguish two comparison registers. \textbf{Register A (same-task direct comparison)}: methods that output one root-cause conclusion per scenario on the same dataset family; the published FaultExplainer per-fault numbers fall here, at scenario granularity. \textbf{Register B (reference only)}: sample-level detection/classification results; they are reported for context and must not be ranked against our scenario-level metrics. Register A comparisons inherit two protocol differences: FaultExplainer supplies candidate root causes in its prompt and accepts alias matches, whereas IDD uses no candidates and scores mechanism keywords strictly; and FaultExplainer scores only the 11 PCA-detectable faults, whereas IDD additionally scores the two faults that screen excludes, IDV3/IDV4. Because the scenario sets differ, no significance test is claimed across systems.

\subsection{Implementation}
The deterministic stages run as versioned scripts (streaming CSV inspection, anomaly profiling, correlation with lag-compensated cross-correlation functions (CCF), validation with Simpson/detrending/outlier-sensitivity/multiple-testing checks, physics checking via the shared scientific Python environment). The reasoning stages execute the full nine-stage agent pipeline under their skill protocols (Section~\ref{sec:arch}); every stage transition is recorded in the event log. The twelve canonical scenarios were executed as concurrent batches under one orchestrating agent (per-run event spans reflect orchestration batching, not isolated agent time); the clean per-scenario figure is the fully sequential random-draw re-execution (Section~\ref{sec:stability}; $\approx$2.0\,h end to end). The deterministic stage takes seconds per scenario. The entire chain (prepare, briefs, pipeline-execution verification, gate-graded scoring, rubric, aggregation, artifact-consistency gate, report) is driven by a single entry point with fail-fast stage gates.\medskip

\textbf{Benchmark configuration disclosure.} All thirteen runs reported here (twelve canonical scenarios plus one random-draw re-execution) were executed on one harness: the ZCode agent CLI (the CLI-driver class of the engine layer, dispatching each stage to an independent sub-agent session; execution form in Section~\ref{sec:framework}) with the GLM model family deployed under the provider's coding-plan configuration (snapshot September 2026). The harness does not expose temperature or top-p control, so provider defaults applied. The vision stage had no vision-capable model in this deployment and executed its documented non-visual degradation in every run (script-signed skeleton visual analysis with caption-based metadata fallback). The execution window was 17--18 September 2026, with one graded execution per scenario plus the drawn re-execution of TEP IDV11. Repair accounting: twelve of the thirteen runs closed with zero diagnosis-layer repair rounds; the exception (TEP IDV3, the hardest fault) spent one judge-driven targeted repair round, and two further scenarios required report-layer corrections after the final audit (Section~\ref{sec:arch})---every repair re-gated to PASS with no verdict text changed. Base-model capability is a confound for any cross-system comparison: the protocol-versus-baseline contrast of Section~\ref{sec:comparison} holds the prompt regime constant but not the model. The pre-registered ablation matrix targeting this attribution question has been partially executed (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}): a single LLM prompted with the identical blinded statistical digest, a classical PCA baseline on all twelve scenarios, and a FaultExplainer-protocol replication on the same model family. The engine-substitution arm and the filter-disabled arm remain future work. The 18 skill packages (the prompts executed by each agent) are released with the repository; Appendix~\ref{app:artifacts} indexes the released per-scenario artifacts.

\subsection{Overall Performance}
\label{sec:overall}
Table~\ref{tab:overall} reports the headline numbers. Executing the full pipeline under truth isolation (one recorded execution per scenario), IDD resolves \textbf{4/9} fault scenarios to a single root cause (Top-1 $=44.4\%$, Wilson 95\% CI $[18.9\%,73.3\%]$); in the remaining five the data cannot separate the surviving mechanisms, and the pipeline returns capped \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}} verdicts---\emph{in every one of the nine scenarios the true mechanism is named within the primary finding or the surviving-hypothesis set} (top-$k$ $=9/9$), so the pipeline's failures on documented faults are failures of \emph{resolution}, not of \emph{localisation}. The keyword instrument needs three disclosures here, because one of the four resolved verdicts sits close to its grading tokens. The SKAB valve scenario's keyword set names the valve in a recording whose loop definitionally contains a valve; excluding it and batch 93, whose set contains the generic token ``deviation'', leaves Top-1 at 3/7 over the remainder, so the resolution pattern does not depend on the near-tautological tokens (top-$k$ on the same clean subset is 7/7); under a branch-localised top-$k$ reading, IDV3 counts as a miss and top-$k$ is 8/9. Batch 93 deserves the fuller statement: its verdict is genuinely execution-side and hits the truth set on \emph{actuator}---plus \emph{heating} and \emph{deviation}---but the diagnosis pins the cooling-water actuator at minimum flow while the documented deviation names the acid-dosing and heating-water flows, so under an identity-strict reading that verdict does not score and Top-1 over the nine faults is 3/9 (Wilson CI $[12.1\%,64.6\%]$). The batch-93 discount does not propagate to top-$k$ at the family level, which stays 9/9: set membership is exactly the mechanism-family instrument, and batch 93's execution-side family match is what that instrument measures. The branch-localised reading of Section~\ref{sec:setup} is stricter on the TEP side and does propagate: it discounts IDV3's stripper-branch membership, giving top-$k$ 8/9. Throughout, a hit certifies mechanism-family agreement, never mechanism identity. Under leave-one-token-out re-scoring of the released keyword-hit records, Top-1 moves to 3/9 only if the single token ``header''---IDV7's documented mechanism word---is removed (IDV1's two hit tokens are spelling variants of one documented label): Top-1 is an instrument-dependent estimate bounded by 3/9--4/9 across the readings this section reports, and the token sets are released so each reading is reproducible.

Determined-verdict precision is 4/4: no verdict is at once \mbox{\textsc{determined}} \emph{and} wrong. Under the identity-strict lens the batch-93 discount propagates here too: precision becomes 3/4 and overconfidence at least one---the asymmetry is reported rather than quoted in each metric's most favourable lens. All 3 control scenarios pass with \textbf{zero false alarms} (two of the three under briefs that disclose the segment's normal status; Section~\ref{sec:setup}). All 12 pipeline executions pass the finalisation gate with valid event logs. The deterministic rubric averages \textbf{94.6/100}, with nine scenarios at 100. The deductions are SKAB inlet-valve throttling $-15$ (R4 verdict-type compliance, analysed in Section~\ref{sec:calibration}), TEP IDV1 $-15$, and the IndPenSim control $-35$. The two calibration deductions arise because reported confidence 0.91 and 0.93 sit $0.01$ and $0.03$ above the machine gate's flat 0.90 non-CS ceiling: the agent-side protocol documents no \mbox{\textsc{det}} cap, so the corpus exhibits the gap between the two cap sets that Section~\ref{sec:method} predicts. The control additionally loses 20 points on R3 grounding, its surviving ``normal operation'' hypothesis not being grounded in the fault-brief numbers the check targets. The in-pipeline judge gate averages 94.7 (range 90--98; all scores are post-threshold by the repair gate's construction), and the artifact-consistency gate verifies that the evaluation is internally consistent. Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark} summarises the outcomes; Section~\ref{sec:ablation} places them against model-controlled baselines.

\subsection{Per-Mechanism Breakdown and Within-Family Discrimination}
\label{sec:permech}
Table~\ref{tab:mech} lists the nine fault scenarios with the verdict each pipeline execution actually produced, the confidence, the rubric score, and the decisive statistical signature the diagnosis rested on. Three observations matter.

\textbf{Within-family form discrimination.} IDV4 (step), IDV11 (random variation) and IDV14 (valve stiction) all perturb the same physical channel---reactor cooling water---yet produce three distinct statistical forms that the pipeline separated, and resolved differently for recorded reasons. IDV4 (step) yields a cooling-triad step fingerprint (reactor temperature spike $z{=}{+}10.5$ with a sustained $+6.8\sigma$ valve step and a flat loop-external channel) whose sub-attribution, unmeasured inlet-temperature step versus sudden exchanger-UA loss, the data cannot separate; the verdict is a capped \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}}. IDV11 (random variation) yields a pure variance-burst signature with \emph{frozen means} (valve-position variance ratio $40.1$, temperature $16.4$, $\Delta$mean $\le 0.01\sigma$, actuator leading temperature by five samples at $r{=}{-}0.64$ while the closed-loop lag-0 sign flip is barred from causal citation) and is capped again on the same inlet-versus-fouling fork. IDV14 (valve stiction) yields a self-excited stiction limit cycle (variance explosions of $\times$189/116/222 on three synchronised channels with \emph{frozen means}, quantised valve stepping at $633/800$ samples, and a lag structure that tightens from one to five steps) and resolves to \mbox{\textsc{determined}}, because the quantised-stepping plus limit-cycle signature is an actuator fingerprint no thermal hypothesis reproduces. Recalling the textbook label ``cooling-water fault'' cannot produce these three different mechanism pictures; only the per-recording statistics can, and the two capped verdicts name the one channel (cooling-water inlet temperature) whose addition would collapse their forks.

\textbf{Reasoning beyond the detection limit.} IDV3 and IDV4 lie beyond classical PCA monitoring and are excluded by FaultExplainer's PCA implementation~\cite{russell2000delay,chiang2001fault,khan2024faultexplainer}. IDV3---commonly regarded as the hardest TEP fault~\cite{russell2000delay}, with a deliberately weak signature (all column mean shifts $\le 0.92\sigma$)---terminates in a capped \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}} (0.55): the exclusion chain eliminates the reactor block (flat: settling $z{=}{-}0.001$), the feed/load family, and sensor artifacts, while variance inflation concentrates in the stripper steam loop (variance ratios $2.1$--$2.7$, Levene $p{<}10^{-4}$, monotone stripper-temperature drift); the fork between a steam-side thermal disturbance and a stripper-feed thermal-state change cannot be resolved without unmeasured steam-header and feed-temperature measurements, and the verdict names them. The true mechanism family remains ranked among the survivors. IDV4 terminates similarly (0.63) with the heat-removal-step family top-ranked on an energy-balance necessity argument (Section~\ref{sec:casestudy}). Both caps fall where classical detection and the published baseline stop; the alternative, forcing a single cause from indistinguishable evidence, is the failure mode the protocol is designed to prevent.

\textbf{Blind-set robustness.} The SKAB cavitation scenario is drawn from the ``other'' experiment group whose fault label is withheld from the scenario description. The pipeline's surviving hypotheses are the two sides of an intermittent flow-collapse instability (76 near-zero-flow rows in forty 1--3\,s bursts with the pump load essentially unchanged at $2.34$ vs $2.40$\,A, same-window vibration rises of $+5\%/+3\%$ on two accelerometers, and stagnation heating of $+0.23$\,K; the drive side and the voltage confound are quantitatively excluded), but the channels that would separate valve-side throttling oscillation from suction-side cavitation or air entrainment---valve position and high-frequency suction pressure---are absent, so the verdict is a capped \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}} (0.60) with the cavitation-type family ranked among the survivors (second of two, behind the valve-side hypothesis), naming the missing channels.

Figure~\ref{fig:calib} plots the reported confidence per scenario. Fault-scenario confidences span 0.55--0.91; the five \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}} verdicts sit in the 0.55--0.63 band because the protocol caps them, and each names its discriminating measurement (valve-position/command channel and pressure direction; suction-side high-frequency pressure; cooling-water inlet temperature for both cooling-loop forks; steam-header and feed-temperature measurements for IDV3). Within the surviving sets the documented truth ranks first in two of the five capped verdicts (IDV4; IDV11 at branch level, where the cooling-water-side disturbance heads the set while its specific random-variation form ranks third of four behind the steam-branch alternative) and second in the remaining three (SKAB inlet-valve throttling and cavitation, each of two survivors; IDV3, of three, ahead of only a weak-excluded residual candidate)---the cap separates equally-evidenced mechanisms, not a truth-relevance ordering---and top-$k$ counts set membership, never ordering. We report this metric as \emph{ceiling compliance} rather than calibration; it is circular by construction, since the agent-side caps are enforced and compliance with them is a property of the protocol's arithmetic.

The non-CS states are where the corpus contributes new evidence: the agent-side protocol documents no \mbox{\textsc{determined}} cap, while the machine grading gate sets a flat 0.90 flag bound---and two verdicts exceed it, TEP IDV1 at 0.91 and the IndPenSim control at 0.93. Both are recorded as R4 calibration deductions by the rubric and plotted in Fig.~\ref{fig:calib}; neither verdict is wrong (the overconfidence metric---\mbox{\textsc{determined}} \emph{and} wrong---never fires), but the two-point gap between the agent-side and machine-side cap sets that Section~\ref{sec:method} predicted is now visible in the corpus rather than hypothetical---a consistency finding about our own two cap artifacts, not external validation. A psychometric calibration study (pre-gate confidence versus outcome correctness, over repeat runs) remains future work.

Under the benchmark's strict typing, one scenario counts as a verdict-type miss: SKAB inlet-valve throttling returns \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}} where the scenario's allowed set admits only \mbox{\textsc{determined}} (the ground truth is known to be valve restriction). The typing is one-directional: the other eight fault scenarios' allowed sets admit both states, so compliance can flag over-capping but never under-capping. The miss stands; the capped verdict is nonetheless the act the protocol demands when the valve-position and controller-command channels that would separate ``valve closing'' from ``commanded duty reduction'' are absent from the recording: the pump's electrical load shows only a directional co-drop with the flow plateau ($-0.115$\,A, $p{=}0.10$), the pressure channel is quantised at 0.327 bar (roughly an order of magnitude above the expected head change), and the flow level shift itself is small and unambiguous in timing but silent about \emph{which} side of the restriction moved. Fig.~\ref{fig:casestudy} makes this scenario fully visible: the annotated recording (flow plateau in the lowest quantisation levels with the pump load essentially flat) alongside the evidence chain as the pipeline recorded it---raw signal, statistics, mechanism, the two surviving hypotheses, and the named missing channels.

Control scenarios demonstrate the complementary discipline: the normal IndPenSim batch contains recipe-dosing impulses reaching $|z|{=}16.2$, which the pipeline attributes to recipe steps and closed-loop control actions rather than to faults; the normal TEP run exhibits feedback signatures at $|r|\ge 0.95$ with the loop's lag-0 peak correctly read as controller response; the normal SKAB run's strong raw correlations ($|r|$ 0.71--0.89) are refuted as slow co-drift artifacts by the detrending and lag-compensation checks. These are the failure modes that produce false alarms in naive correlation-driven diagnosis.

\section{Comparison With Published Baselines, Model-Controlled Ablation, and Verdict Stability}
\label{sec:comparison}

\subsection{Register A: Per-Fault Comparison on TEP}
FaultExplainer~\cite{khan2024faultexplainer} reports, in its root-causes-included regime, root-cause correctness of 7/11 (63.6\%, Wilson CI $[35.4\%,84.8\%]$) for GPT-4o and 9/11 (81.8\%, CI $[52.3\%,94.9\%]$) for o1-preview on the 11 PCA-detectable TEP faults---correctness meaning the true fault or an alias in the model's top-3, with candidates supplied in the prompt; in its no-candidate regime both models reach 8/11 under lenient ``related'' grading. IDD, without candidate lists and scored by strict mechanism keywords, is Top-1 correct on \textbf{3/6} TEP faults (Wilson CI $[18.8\%,81.2\%]$) and ranks the true mechanism among the survivors in all six (top-$k$ 6/6); on the four shared PCA-detectable faults it is correct on 3/4, where GPT-4o is 3/4 (missing IDV1, which IDD resolves) and o1-preview is 4/4 under alias-inclusive scoring. The base models differ and are not controlled, the grading protocols differ (Section~\ref{sec:setup}), and the ablation of Section~\ref{sec:ablation} shows that a bare call to the same model family already scores these documented faults at or above the pipeline (9/9 versus 4/9 resolved)---so we read the per-fault table descriptively, not as a superiority claim. Table~\ref{tab:perfault} (Appendix~\ref{app:artifacts}) transcribes the comparison; Fig.~\ref{fig:perfault} breaks it down per fault against FaultExplainer's Table~1 outcomes and PCA detectability, and Fig.~\ref{fig:forest} shows the aggregates with intervals. Most of the comparison concentrates in three outcomes:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{IDV1 (A/C feed-ratio step):} the GPT-4o baseline mis-diagnoses this fault (o1-preview resolves it); IDD resolves it to \mbox{\textsc{determined}} (0.91) from the actuator--flow signature (A-feed valve and flow in $+18.5\sigma$ lockstep, $r{=}0.9996$, with the C-side feed compensating at $-2.9/-6.9\sigma$), correctly restructuring the disturbance as an A:C supply-weight shift with the stripper steam response trailing as a downstream interlock (lag 2).
\item \textbf{IDV3 and IDV4 (excluded from FE scoring):} both faults are excluded from FaultExplainer's scoring by its PCA implementation. IDD terminates both in capped \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}} verdicts with the true mechanism ranked (Section~\ref{sec:casestudy} details the IDV4 chain).
\item \textbf{IDV7, IDV11, IDV14:} IDD resolves the header-pressure form (0.78: valve--flow gain loss with flow constant $\Rightarrow$ supply-capacity drop) and the stiction form (0.80: quantised-stepping limit cycle), and caps IDV11's random-variation form (0.60) at the inlet-vs-fouling fork---separating the three cooling-family \emph{forms} at the level of distinct quantitative signatures (Section~\ref{sec:permech}), a granularity below fault-identity level.
\end{itemize}

Because the scenario sets, prompt regimes, and grading protocols all differ (top-3 with alias acceptance vs.\ a strict single verdict; with vs.\ without candidate lists; different base models), we present the per-fault table as descriptive evidence and claim no significance test across systems. The comparison is also asymmetric in cost: FE-style prompting runs in minutes per fault, whereas the full gated pipeline's per-scenario agent time is on the order of two hours (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

\subsection{Register B: Reference-Only Context}
For context we report sample-level results from the literature without ranking: TEP classification accuracies of 0.887--0.907 (MLP/GRU/TCN on the 28-class extended TEP taxonomy of Rieth et al.~\cite{rieth2017additional,pozdnyakov2024adversarial}), anomaly-detection F1 of 0.970 (BeatGAN~\cite{hartung2023deep}), SKAB ensemble F1/AUC of 0.85/0.88~\cite{iliopoulos2023detection}, and leakage-controlled bearing AUROC of 0.75--0.93~\cite{vieira2026towards}. These systems answer ``is this sample anomalous / which class''; IDD answers ``what is the mechanism, how confident, with which evidence''. The dimensions are complementary: a deployment would plausibly chain a sample-level detector (first-stage alarm) with a scenario-level RCA system (this work).

\subsection{Model-Controlled Baselines and Pipeline Ablation}\label{sec:ablation}
Because the base model is a confound in any comparison with published LLM baselines (Section~\ref{sec:setup}), we ran three model-controlled baselines with the \emph{same model family and deployment} as the pipeline (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}; the LLM arms are recorded replays of live runs from this deployment, and the TEP fault-free control was not run bare): (i) a classical PCA baseline---normal-control training, $T^2$/Q detection with 99th-percentile alarms, and SPE-contribution diagnostics~\cite{chiang2001fault,qin2012survey}---on all twelve scenarios; (ii) a replication of FaultExplainer's protocol on the six TEP faults, in both of FE's prompt regimes, scored both FE-style (top-3 with alias classes) and strictly; and (iii) the pre-registered single-LLM ablation---one call to the same model family over the identical blinded statistical digest, no ontology, no pipeline---on all nine fault scenarios, scored with the same strict mechanism keywords as IDD. All three baselines are bare single-call executions: no agent orchestration, no gates, no evidence grading---and, like the pipeline runs, each is a single recorded execution rather than a repeated sample. The classical PCA arm is computed by a released deterministic script (pure-Jacobi eigendecomposition, normal-control training, 99th-percentile alarms of the control distribution, TEP fault-window evaluation from row 161); an earlier prototype run agreed with it on every SPE statistic and control rate, with minor $T^2$ differences on the weakest-signature faults, and the prototype output is archived in the released repository for audit.

Three results follow, and they do not all favour the pipeline. \textbf{First}, on keyword-scored accuracy over documented public faults the bare same-model call is not merely equal to the full pipeline but ahead of it: 9/9 strict hits versus the pipeline's 4/9 resolved (five capped verdicts)---including IDV3, plausibly by literature recall of the documented weak-signature fault, and naming valve and cavitation mechanisms on the SKAB scenarios from their signatures---and the recall caveat cuts both ways, since the pipeline's keyword sets derive from the same documented cause table: neither arm's absolute TEP numbers measure contamination-free ability, and the form-discrimination evidence of Section~\ref{sec:permech} together with the novel-disturbance suite is the clean test. The bare arm produces no false alarms on the two of the three controls the bare-LLM arm covered (the TEP fault-free control was not run bare; the released baseline record notes the coverage gap, and only covered controls are scored). The gap between 9/9 and 4/9 is the paper's central cost statement: the pipeline gives up resolution to gain an auditable process, and the capped verdicts are where that cost is visible. The cost is computable from the released gradings without new runs: had the protocol forced commitment to each capped verdict's top-ranked survivor, Top-1 would be 6/9 with three wrong commitments (both SKAB capped cases and IDV3 rank the truth second)---the cap trades three false determinations for two foregone resolutions, which is the trade the three-state protocol is built to make. That the resolution loss locates in the cap-and-elimination policy rather than in orchestration is inferred from this counterfactual, not tested by an arm; the filter-disabled arm remains pre-registered.

\textbf{Second}, the FE official-prompt arm, running on the same GLM model, scores 5/6 on the TEP subset---above IDD's 3/6 Top-1---with a different miss pattern: it misses IDV4 because its PCA $T^2$ features are dominated by feed-composition shifts that mask the cooling-water signal, while IDD caps IDV3, IDV4 and IDV11 (the two cooling-loop forks and the steam-site fork), keeping the true mechanism ranked in each. Read at IDD's closest analogue to FE's top-3 notion---the surviving-hypothesis set---the pipeline's top-$k$ on the TEP subset is 6/6.

\textbf{Third}, the classical PCA baseline reproduces the literature (IDV3 at the detection limit, $T^2$ 2.9\%/SPE 4.8\%; IDV4 ``weak $T^2$, strong $Q$''---SPE 100\% vs $T^2$ 48.8\%) and produces no mechanism verdicts.

The three failures differ in kind, and the difference exposes what benchmark accuracy on documented faults conflates: detection ability, mechanism attribution, and epistemic discipline. The bare call produces nothing the audit plane can verify: no evidence levels, no ceiling compliance, no physics check, no execution proof, no artifact provenance---and its IDV3 answer, being correct, is exactly the kind of unverifiable guess that the three-state protocol is meant to constrain (the pipeline's capped verdict names the steam-header and feed-temperature measurements that would settle the question).

\subsection{Verdict Stability: a Random-Draw Independent Re-execution}\label{sec:stability}
The evaluation framework formalises consistency testing as a draw-then-re-execute protocol: a scenario is selected by a \emph{seeded uniform random draw} over the nine-fault pool (so the selection is reproducible and fixed before comparison, though the seed originates from the evaluation harness rather than an external randomness beacon---a limitation we state alongside the draw record) and then re-executed through the full gated pipeline in a fresh run directory with fresh agent sessions (new ontology build, new statistical stage, new diagnosis, new review cycle, new HTML build and review); the draw record is append-only and replayable from the released seed. In the current corpus the draw selected TEP IDV11 (seed \texttt{1003818694}; the record carries the pool, the raw uniform value, and the resulting index), and the drawn re-execution completed all nine stages end to end in $\approx$2.0\,h, passing every gate (finalisation \textsc{pass}). The framework's rule is that the latest proven execution of a scenario is its canonical graded run, so the re-execution (0.60) superseded the original batch execution (0.62) in every aggregate; both are capped \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}} verdicts with the same signature, so the supersession changes no headline number. Fig.~\ref{fig:consistency} maps the full audit.

The two executions of the drawn scenario---the corpus's single repeat pair ($n{=}1$): the original batch execution \texttt{202609171639527} (run~1) and the drawn re-execution \texttt{202609172247007} (run~2, now canonical)---agree on everything the protocol measures at the mechanism level: the verdict state (capped \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}} in both), the implicated actuator (XMV\_10, the reactor cooling-water valve), the mechanism class (environment-side cooling disturbance), and the top-ranked hypothesis (a cooling-water-side disturbance rather than a valve-internal fault), with the two sessions' confidence estimates 0.02 apart (0.62 vs 0.60, both under the 0.65 INDISTINGUISHABLE cap). Both sessions also agree on the discriminating evidence: the variance-burst topology with frozen means and the actuator-leads-temperature lead structure; the retest additionally tightened the causal-direction argument (lag-5 $r{=}{-}0.636$ with the closed-loop lag-0 sign flip formally barred from causal citation) and bounded the excluded feed and condenser families more sharply. The consistency audit marks the pair \textsc{consistent}---the current corpus holds exactly one case-version pair, and within-era agreement is therefore 1/1 with zero divergences. The agreement is decided at the structured level (verdict type, equipment tag, mechanism class); the two sessions' free-text mechanism strings share only 0.14 of cause tokens, and the re-run's specific form ranks within the same cooling branch---wording drifts, the structure does not. One consistent pair is the beginning of a stability study, not its end. The agreement rule evaluates the coarsest structured fields (verdict type, equipment tag, mechanism class), and the drawn scenario is one whose deterministic signature---a variance burst of ratio 40 with frozen means---makes structural agreement close to mechanical, since both sessions run the same statistics stage; a draw landing on IDV3 or a SKAB case would have stressed the rule harder. With one draw, the protocol's evidential yield is itself unmeasured. The supersession rule (latest proven execution is canonical) was fixed before comparison and will not be applied selectively to disfavour re-runs; the remaining eleven fault-or-control scenarios each hold a single current-era execution, the audit lists them as outstanding re-run contracts rather than assuming their stability, and the released consistency report enumerates them. Expanding the repeat count (all 12 scenarios, $k \ge 3$ repeats, verdict-state stability and confidence distributions) remains pre-registered in the released repository.

\subsection{Case Study: An Auditable Evidence Chain on a Fault Excluded from Classical Scoring}\label{sec:casestudy}
For TEP IDV4---a fault excluded from FaultExplainer's scoring by its PCA implementation, and only weakly detected classically ($T^2$ 48.8\% vs SPE 100\% in our deterministic replication)~\cite{russell2000delay}---the recorded chain reads: (i)~\emph{L3 statistical evidence}: at fault onset the reactor cooling valve XMV\_10 takes a sustained $+6.8\sigma$ step (41.1\%$\to$44.9\% with no ramp) while reactor temperature spikes to $z{=}{+}10.5$ with a transient on its sister thermocouple ($+2.0/-2.3\sigma$) and every loop-external column stays flat (the separator-outlet column at $-0.08\sigma$)---a loop-localised cooling triad; (ii)~\emph{L5 mechanism, with a necessity argument}: an energy balance on the reactor heat-removal loop shows the observed coolant-flow rise with conserved reactor temperature algebraically \emph{requires} an upward shift in the cooling-water inlet temperature (or, indistinguishably, a sudden drop in exchanger UA)---a controller compensating exactly as the physics demands, not a sensor artifact; (iii)~\emph{eliminations}: feed-side, condenser, purge, and ramp-form alternatives are quantitatively excluded (each at $\ge$95\% confidence with cited contradictions), and the feed-composition channel shows no in-window signature; (iv)~\emph{conclusion}: capped \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}} at 0.63 (INDISTINGUISHABLE ceiling 0.65) between ``cooling-water inlet-temperature step'' (top-ranked) and ``sudden UA loss''. The recording contains no cooling-water inlet measurement; the verdict names it, and the audit trail quantifies the fork: conserving the loop temperatures under the UA sub-case requires a pre-existing reactor-to-coolant temperature approach below $1.5$\,K (implying a coolant inlet above $\approx$93\,\textdegree C, against the $\approx$26\,K approach observed in the pre-onset data), which strongly disfavours the pure-UA form---though it stays below the quantitative elimination threshold because a UA loss can redistribute the loop's LMTD rather than move the observed temperatures, which is precisely why the verdict is a capped competing set rather than a determination. The quantification sharpens the ranking within the cap. The full chain, together with the physics-check verdict, the judge's ten-criterion scores (97/100), and the independent HTML review (pass), is persisted in the grading record and released with the evaluation. We read the cap as the finding: on a fault the published baseline cannot even score, the pipeline localises the loop, states the physically necessary mechanism family, quantifies why the two remaining sub-mechanisms cannot be separated, and names the single measurement---the cooling-water inlet thermocouple---whose installation would collapse the set to \mbox{\textsc{determined}}.

\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Deployment Prospects}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Statistical power and single-execution evidence.} With $n{=}9$ fault scenarios, Top-1 $=44.4\%$ supports only a wide interval ($[18.9\%,73.3\%]$ at 95\% confidence); the top-$k$ bound (9/9, $[70.1\%,100\%]$) is the stronger statement the current sample size can support, and it is a lower bound on resolution only because the keyword instrument is strict (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). Scenario selection was purposive rather than random: the suite over-represents documented, public fault episodes, which biases difficulty downward and buys mechanism diversity across families rather than within them, so the intervals in Table~\ref{tab:overall} read as measurement uncertainty on this corpus, not sampling generalisation to a scenario population. Two qualifications attach to these figures. First, every headline number is a \emph{single recorded execution} per scenario---except TEP IDV11, whose drawn re-execution (Section~\ref{sec:stability}) reproduced the verdict state and mechanism at $\Delta$confidence 0.02, the only repeat measurement the corpus currently holds. Second, the bounded repair loop introduces a selection effect, since a reported verdict may be the product of up to five attempts; in this corpus, however, twelve of thirteen runs closed without diagnosis-layer repair, the single exception being disclosed with the configuration (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). The model-controlled ablation (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) already bounds the accuracy claim (9/9 bare versus 4/9 resolved)---so the pipeline's value on documented faults is process quality, not accuracy. Expanding the repeats (all 12 scenarios, $k\ge3$: verdict-type stability, confidence spread, repair counts) remains pre-registered, with eleven scenarios awaiting their second in-era execution---gaps we report rather than fill; extending the corpus (the full 20-class TEP catalogue with the extended simulation dataset~\cite{reinartz2021extended}; bearing datasets; novel-disturbance suites) is the designed path to tighter intervals.

\textbf{Prompt-knowledge contamination.} TEP's documented fault mechanisms appear in textbooks and are therefore plausibly memorised by frontier models. Our protocol mitigates this at the level where memorisation actually operates---but the mitigation has a limit. The documented cause table records not only the disturbed channel but each fault's dynamic form (IDV4 step, IDV11 random variation, IDV14 stiction), so recall of that table alone could reproduce the observed mechanism labels; the statistics-driven reading rests on the recorded reasoning chains, each of which cites the per-recording statistic that discriminates its form. Two residual channels are disclosed. First, the scenario identifier: every TEP case id and run-directory name embeds the documented mechanism and dynamic form (\texttt{tep\_d14\_reactor\_valve\_sticking} names the answer outright), so identifier recall is possible in principle across the whole documented catalogue---IDV14, one of the four resolved verdicts, is the sharpest case. The leakage sentinel machine-checks brief and user-context content, not directory names, so the identifier channel sits outside it. The joint worst case is stated rather than implied: under identifier recall on all TEP-derived outcomes together with the identity-strict batch-93 discount, resolved Top-1 could be as low as 1/9; in Table~\ref{tab:overall} and Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark} the three TEP-derived resolved verdicts should be read as a recall-possible tier until the neutralised-identifier re-run executes. That re-run (six TEP scenarios under opaque hash run identifiers and identifier-stripped brief headers, $\approx$12\,h of agent time by the cost accounting below) is committed as the first post-revision execution; it requires a contained harness extension (hash-id run directories) tracked in the released repository. The briefs themselves carry no fault vocabulary (leakage-sentinel checked), and the form-discrimination evidence of Section~\ref{sec:permech} is the main evidence-backed mitigation among the disclosed residual channels: the mechanism labels the pipeline commits to are read off per-recording statistics, which identifier recall would not require. Under the identifier channel alone, recall could supply both mechanism and dynamic form, so all four TEP-derived outcomes---including the resolved IDV1/IDV7/IDV14---carry the recall-possible tier until the re-run executes. Second, the SKAB ``other''-group scenario, whose fault label is withheld entirely from all natural-language brief content, still yielded the cavitation-type mechanism ranked among the survivors of a capped verdict. The RAG channel was unavailable in every run (Section~\ref{sec:arch-layers}), and no L6-tagged item exists anywhere in the released corpus: no verdict rests on external evidence. A novel-disturbance suite (combination perturbations absent from the literature) is the definitive control and remains under construction.

\textbf{What the five capped verdicts mean.} The five \mbox{\textsc{competing\_set}} outcomes are the protocol working as designed, and they are also the benchmark's most transferable finding: each verdict localises the disturbance to a subsystem, eliminates whole mechanism families with quantitative contradictions, keeps the true mechanism ranked among the survivors, and names the one measurement whose addition would collapse the set to \mbox{\textsc{determined}} (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}). In deployment terms these are not failures but work orders for targeted instrumentation, and the three-state protocol is what converts a diagnosis into that engineering workflow: \mbox{\textsc{determined}} conclusions carry mechanism chains that point to corrective action; capped conclusions carry the named channel. To keep the framing falsifiable, the released scenario definitions record the expected verdict set of every scenario, fixed before execution; SKAB inlet-valve throttling is the one scenario whose expectation was \mbox{\textsc{determined}}-only, and its capped verdict is counted as the benchmark's single compliance miss rather than narrated as a success.

\textbf{Inference authorship and provenance.} Every scored artifact in this benchmark is authored by a gated pipeline stage---the diagnosis by the diagnostician, the quality verdict by the judge, the ENDORSED decision by the physical auditor, the review verdict by the independent HTML reviewer---and each carries a provenance label in the grading record. The runs reported here executed in the production dispatch form (independent per-stage sub-agent sessions; Section~\ref{sec:framework}), so the reviewing stages were physically separate sessions from the authoring stages---separate in session and protocol, though sharing the one model family (Section~\ref{sec:framework}). Because self-declared quality scores are not verifiable, the benchmark's reported quality metric is the deterministic compliance rubric (Section~\ref{sec:setup})---and the rubric, the grading keywords, and the allowed verdict sets remain author-designed instruments measuring conformance to this paper's own protocol; their release with the repository makes them auditable, but independent anchoring (the blinded expert adjudication of the released evidence chains) is the outstanding validation step---so the ``process quality'' margins claimed for the pipeline are architectural properties, not yet measured outcomes. A further provenance caveat: the L1--L7 levels are asserted by the reasoning stage itself; the machine gate checks ceiling arithmetic and grounding, not the correctness of a label, so a mislabelled evidence item could inflate its own ceiling---a disclosed residual risk whose mechanical binding (L1/L3 $\to$ ingestion/statistics files, L5 $\to$ ontology nodes) is a toolchain next step. An internal integrity audit that found (and removed) a scripted shortcut capable of fabricating artifacts and agent events is itself part of the released history; the completeness gate now makes that shortcut unreplayable. The audit plane's trust boundary is internal: the event log proves process conformance, not external attestation---a compromised execution layer could still fabricate events---which is why the grader's division of labour (scripts verify, never author) and the artifact cross-checks carry the weight. What is still missing is a comparison against \emph{human} experts on identical digests; an expert-vs-pipeline blind test is planned.

\textbf{Cost.} The clean per-scenario figure for the full gated pipeline is the sequential drawn re-execution: $\approx$2.0\,h of agent time end to end (117\,min event span), of which the deterministic statistical stage---script-speed, seconds---is a negligible share. Per-stage wall-clock spans across the thirteen released event logs give a median full-pipeline agent span of $\approx$1.9\,h ($\approx$112\,min: ontology $\approx$14\,min, statistics $\approx$22\,min, diagnosis $\approx$20\,min, the two review stages $\approx$29\,min combined, reporting and HTML the remaining $\approx$26\,min). The twelve canonical scenarios were executed as concurrent batches under one orchestrating agent (per-run event spans of $\approx$6\,h therefore reflect batching, not isolated effort; Section~\ref{sec:setup}). The pipeline is orders of magnitude more expensive than a fitted classifier at inference time and far slower than single-prompt baselines. The economic comparison is only meaningful against the \emph{engineering cost of obtaining root causes} (expert hours), and the ontology-asset layer (Section~\ref{sec:arch-layers}) amortises the reasoning cost across a plant's scenario fleet.

\subsection{Industrial Deployment Prospects}
We separate what the benchmark and one operational deployment \emph{demonstrate} from what the architecture \emph{projects}.

\textbf{Observed in one deployment} (artifacts anonymised and not part of the released corpus): a film-line run exercised the pipeline end to end on episodic scratch defects of a biaxially-oriented polyester film line and returned \mbox{\textsc{needs\_data}} with an explicit discriminating-data list after 180 parameter--defect associations failed false-discovery-rate correction, redirecting the investigation from parameter tuning toward surface-inspection and equipment-event data; no cause was fabricated. Projected: the integration, sovereignty, and documentation claims below are design properties of the architecture, not yet benchmarked outcomes.

\textbf{Integration with existing plant infrastructure.} IDD consumes what plants already produce: flat exports from historians (OPC-UA/PI), LIMS quality records, and batch logs. The same pipeline runs interactively through a web application and programmatically through a versioned REST API (\texttt{/api/diagnosis} with engine selection and typed error contracts), so a deployment is an export job plus API integration rather than an instrumentation project---the integration work is real but bounded by the historian export format. Because the deterministic stage is byte-reproducible, results do not drift when the surrounding plant IT systems change.

\textbf{Data-sovereign deployment.} The fourteen-engine execution layer (Section~\ref{sec:arch}) accepts on-premise and private-model agents through uniform dialect adapters. For operators who cannot send process data to external LLM services---defence, pharmaceuticals, semiconductors, national infrastructure---the reasoning stage can be pointed at an internally hosted model without touching the audit, evidence, or gate contracts---no non-default engine has yet executed the full gated pipeline in the released corpus, so this path is design-level, like the amortisation projection; the benchmark protocol itself executes identically against a scripted mock engine, which is how the full chain is exercised in CI.



\textbf{Documentation for regulated operations.} Because every conclusion is bound to graded evidence, physics checks, and a fingerprinted execution trace, the by-product of each run is an RCA report whose traceability serves the documentation expectations of regulated environments (e.g., GxP batch-deviation investigation, in the sense of ALCOA+-style data integrity~\cite{mhra2018} and GAMP~5 computerised-systems thinking~\cite{gamp2022}). This is a documentation property of the artifact, not a computerised-system validation claim; the latter would require a formal qualification exercise.

\textbf{Deployment governance and knowledge compounding.} IDD is positioned as decision support: a \mbox{\textsc{determined}} verdict does not authorise maintenance action---a human engineer signs off. In the current release the sign-off is procedural (no separate human-endorsement artifact exists in the run contract, and all thirteen benchmark runs were fully autonomous); we disclose this as an implementation gap, not a deployed control, and the three-state protocol is precisely the escalation interface between machine conclusion and human decision. Model-change management rides the engine layer's version disclosure (Section~\ref{sec:setup}): a provider silently updating the reasoning model changes the system, so the benchmark configuration disclosure doubles as the deployment pinning record. Finally, the ontology layer compounds knowledge across runs (Section~\ref{sec:arch-layers}): all thirteen benchmark runs published their ontologies to the store on completion, and every field incident becomes a candidate graded scenario, closing the loop between deployment and evaluation. In deployment the marginal cost of a diagnosis therefore falls as the fleet grows (projected: the benchmark pinned full rebuilds, so this amortisation is design-level, not measured)---an economics that sample-level detectors, which must be retrained per asset, do not share.

\textbf{Scope of applicability.} The method requires multivariate time-ordered process data with engineering semantics for the channels (to build the ontology) and applies to continuous, batch, and testbed processes alike---the three benchmark families were chosen to span exactly that range. It does not apply where no time-aligned process data exists, and its root-cause reach is bounded by the sensor set, as the capped verdicts demonstrate.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented IDD, an agentic diagnosis pipeline that turns LLM-based root-cause analysis into an auditable engineering procedure---competing hypotheses with mechanism classes, evidence levels bounding reported confidence, a four-condition anti-spurious filter, physical verification, and quality gates with bounded repair---together with a machine-checked evaluation contract for diagnosis benchmarks: truth isolation, data fingerprinting, stage-authored artifacts with provenance, and gate-graded scoring. On the 12-scenario corpus the headline outcome is deliberately modest: 4/9 faults resolved Top-1, the true mechanism ranked in every fault scenario's surviving set (top-$k$ 9/9; 8/9 when a survivor must carry the specific form or stream branch, not just the family), zero false alarms, five capped verdicts that each name the measurement that would settle them, and every number backed by an execution proof. The model-controlled ablation bounds what the pipeline can claim: a bare call to the same model already exceeds its accuracy (9/9 versus 4/9), so the contribution is the auditable process rather than the accuracy. One seeded re-execution reproduced verdict state, actuator, and mechanism class within two confidence points; the deterministic suite reproduced byte-for-byte; and the per-fault TEP comparison shows IDD resolving the GPT-4o baseline's IDV1 miss while scoring the PCA-excluded pair that baselines exclude, as capped verdicts with the mechanism family ranked.
These claims have limits: resolution of documented faults is the pipeline's weak axis by its own ablation (a bare same-model call scores 9/9 versus the pipeline's 4/9 resolved; accuracy is not the contribution), results are single recorded executions of a stochastic reasoning layer whose configuration is disclosed and whose run identifiers carry the documented mechanism labels (so the ranking claim is not recall-blind), the machine gate's flat 0.90 non-CS ceiling flags two verdicts (0.91 and 0.93) that the agent-side cap set permits, so the two cap sets' gap is measured rather than hidden; and the rubric and keywords are author-designed instruments with independent expert anchoring outstanding. The evidence base is one model family at one deployment on twelve public-dataset scenarios; cross-model and cross-plant generalisation of the process guarantees (in contrast to the accuracy findings, which the ablation already bounds) remains to be demonstrated.
The evaluation framework, per-scenario evidence chains, and consistency artifacts are released to support scrutiny and reuse; the pre-registered repeat and ablation studies, and the novel-disturbance suite, are the immediate next steps.
