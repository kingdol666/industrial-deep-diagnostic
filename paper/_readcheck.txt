Abstract

Large language model (LLM) diagnosis of industrial processes promises training-
free root-cause analysis, yet its conclusions are rarely auditable and its eval-
uation is sample-level where engineers ask scenario-level, mechanism-grounded
questions. We present the Industrial Deep Diagnostic (IDD) pipeline: nine-stage
agentic orchestration--deterministic statistics, competing-hypothesis reasoning,
evidence grading, an anti-spurious-correlation filter, and bounded-repair gates--
wrapped in a machine-checked, provenance-labeled evaluation framework. On
a 12-scenario benchmark spanning SKAB, the Tennessee Eastman Process, and
IndPenSim, IDD resolves four of nine documented faults to a single root cause--
a provisional upper-bound reading (run-directory labels can name the docu-
mented mechanism)--ranks the true mechanism among the survivors in every
fault scenario at the keyword instrument's family level, and returns five capped
competing_set verdicts; no control scenario raises a false alarm (one of the
three blind). A same-model ablation bounds every accuracy claim: a bare LLM
call over the same blind digest commits a correct mechanism in all nine fault
scenarios where the pipeline resolves four and caps five (both arms 9/9 at fam-
ily level). Forced commitment to each capped set's top survivor would score
6/9 with three wrong root causes, whereas each capped verdict keeps the true
mechanism ranked and names the measurement that settles it--an explicit op-
erating point on the risk­coverage curve; blinded expert adjudication remains
the outstanding validation step. One seeded re-execution reproduced a sce-
nario's verdict state and mechanism class; the deterministic suite arms re-run
byte-identically (45/45 recorded outputs, timestamps excluded). Throughout,
trustworthy denotes auditable and capped-honest, not verified correctness.

Keywords: Fault root-cause analysis, Reproducible benchmark, Large
language models, Multi-agent systems, Competing hypotheses, Process
ontology

1. Introduction

    Root-cause analysis (RCA) of industrial process anomalies is a knowledge-
intensive activity: the engineer must decide which of many correlated sensor
deviations represents the true underlying cause, justify that judgment with
evidence, and communicate it with a calibrated degree of confidence. Three
long-standing obstacles keep that automation unsatisfactory in practice.

    First, single-LLM diagnosis is unstructured and unauditable. Re-
cent evaluations of LLM-based fault explanation on the Tennessee Eastman Pro-
cess (TEP) [1] show that large models can articulate plausible mechanisms, but
their answers arrive as free-form text with no machine-checkable structure, no
separation of facts from inference, and no mechanism that prevents a confident-
sounding conclusion from resting on a spurious correlation. A recent review
of agentic approaches to process monitoring and fault diagnosis concludes that
evidence for LLM/multi-agent fault diagnosis remains scarce [2]: the direction
is promising but lacks engineering-grade pipelines.

    Second, classical data-driven fault detection and diagnosis (FDD)
optimizes the wrong target. Principal-component methods [3], deep
anomaly detectors [4, 5], and their benchmarks [6] score per-sample detection
or classification [7]. An engineer, however, asks a per-episode question: given
this anomalous run, what is the root cause, how confident are we, and what
evidence supports it? A 99%-accurate classifier that returns a fault label with-
out mechanism, evidence, or calibration does not answer that question. The
detection­diagnosis gap is not merely theoretical: classical detectability studies
place the documented disturbances IDV3, IDV9 and IDV15 of the fifteen-fault
TEP catalog at the detection limit, with IDV4 weakly detected by the T 2
statistic [8, 9], and the published LLM baseline's own PCA implementation
leaves all four of these faults unscored [1].

    Third, correlation is not causation. In recirculating process loops, al-
most every pair of variables is correlated through control action or material
balance. An RCA method that treats strong correlation as causation will con-
fidently report control-loop artifacts as root causes. Despite decades of causal-
inference literature [10, 11], contemporary LLM-diagnosis studies rarely include
explicit anti-spurious safeguards.

    This paper addresses the three obstacles with the Industrial Deep Diagnostic
(IDD) pipeline and a benchmark built to evaluate it honestly. The deployment
regime this paper targets is the episodic, high-consequence investigation--a unit
trip, a batch deviation, a quality escape--where an engineer must reconstruct
what happened from historian records and then act on the conclusion; the de-
liverable IDD owes that engineer is a verdict that can be audited under time
pressure: a determined root cause with its evidence chain, or a capped set of
survivors that names the measurement which would settle the ambiguity. Sec-
tion 7.1 retraces one complete execution the way that engineer would read it.
Our contributions are:

    Throughout, trustworthy is used in a delimited sense that the paper's evi-
dence is expected to carry: an IDD conclusion is trustworthy to the extent that
it is auditable--every claim decomposes into hypotheses, evidence with levels,
exclusions, and confidence, all machine-checkable from released artifacts--and
capped-honest: when the recording cannot separate the surviving mechanisms,
the pipeline must say so and name the discriminating measurement rather than
commit. Correctness is carried by the truth comparison of Section 5, never
by the label; what the pipeline adds is that its conclusions can be audited,
reproduced, and escalated rather than merely read.

2. Related Work

2.1. Data-Driven Fault Detection and the Benchmark Gap

    Statistical process monitoring on the Tennessee Eastman Process established
the dominant evaluation template [12, 7]: principal-component analysis (PCA)
and projection methods with Hotelling's T 2 (the model-subspace score) and the
residual Q, or squared prediction error, statistic [3, 13], later extended by deep
anomaly detectors [14, 4] and adversarial robustness studies [5]. Within the tri-
partite partition of fault detection and diagnosis into quantitative-model-based,
qualitative-model-based, and process-history methods [15, 16, 17], these fami-
lies occupy the process-history branch, and the quantitative branch is anchored
by model-based analytical redundancy and residual-based fault isolation [18].
All of these branches score sample-level detection or fault-class accuracy. Criti-
cally, they do not evaluate root-cause mechanisms: a classifier that labels a run
"IDV(4)" provides the class identity but neither a causal mechanism, an evidence
trail, nor a confidence statement. The classical detection ceiling (IDV3/9/15 at
the limit, IDV4 weak) is documented [8, 9]. Benchmark methodology has drawn
scrutiny: random segment splits inflate reported AUROC--by up to 15.3 per-
centage points of Macro-AUROC under segmentation-level leakage in Vieira
et al. [19], with a lineage formalized by Hendriks et al. [20]--motivating our
entity-level scenario splits (Section 5). Scenario-level root-cause localization it-
self has a classical lineage that IDD must be read against: Granger-causal and
transfer-entropy methods localize propagation directions in plant-wide oscilla-
tions [10, 11, 21, 22], signed-directed-graph reasoning performs mechanism-chain
elimination [23], reconstruction-based contribution analysis isolates root causes
from propagated effects [24, 25], and digital-twin practice (surveyed in [26])
mirrors model-based diagnosis.

2.2. LLM-Based Industrial Diagnosis

    FaultExplainer [1] is the closest published system: it couples PCA-based
detection with LLM-generated fault explanations on TEP. Its evaluation uses
two prompt regimes. In the root-causes-included regime the prompt car-
ries the documented root-cause list, the model outputs a top-3 candidate
list, and alias matches are accepted (alias classes: IDV1/2/8, IDV3IDV9,
IDV4IDV11IDV14, IDV5IDV12IDV15); here GPT-4o resolves 7/11
and o1-preview 9/11 of the 11 PCA-detectable faults (FaultExplainer's own
PCA implementation excludes IDV3, IDV4, IDV9 and IDV15 from scoring). In
its General-Reasoning regime--no candidate list, designed to simulate unseen
faults--both models place the correct or a related cause in the top-3 for 8/11

                                                   4
faults under lenient "related" grading (FE's own text names faults 10 and
13 as failing yet reports 8/11; we transcribe the reported figure). Protocol
differences therefore condition the comparison: (i) FE's outputs are top-3 lists
with alias/related acceptance, whereas IDD commits to a single mechanism
verdict scored by strict mechanism keywords; (ii) the two PCA-excluded faults
in our subset (IDV3, IDV4) are scored by IDD; (iii) FE's alias policy grades
a cooling-water-family answer as correct for IDV11 and IDV14 alike, whereas
IDD separates the three forms of the same physical channel. Gong et al. [27]
orchestrate multiple LLM agents with difficulty-aware routing, but evaluate on
a multiple-choice QA benchmark (FailureSensorIQ [28]) rather than on real
time-series data.

    Liu et al. [29] combine scene graphs, fine-tuned Llama-3 and augmented
reality for machine-tool maintenance, and causal-graph RCA has been stud-
ied for time-varying processes [30] and knowledge-map-based group decision
settings [31]; a label-free evaluation scheme for diagnosis models has been pro-
posed in the same venue [32]. Multimodal LLM frameworks extend the modal-
ity range: Alsaif et al. [33] couple textual, tabular, and image modalities for
Industry-4.0 fault detection and diagnosis over industrial fault records, and
Merkelbach et al. [34] show that multimodal LLMs can draft the physical models
that automated diagnosis consumes, while documenting significant drawbacks.
Recent work in this journal shows the same momentum and the same gaps.
CausalKGPT couples an industrial causal knowledge graph with an LLM for
quality-problem cause analysis [35]; FD-LLM fine-tunes a large language model
for equipment fault diagnosis [36]; Wen et al. orchestrate LLMs for human­
machine collaborative troubleshooting [37]; and Xu et al. pair a deep digital
twin with a vision-language model for multi-scenario fault diagnosis [38]. Clos-
est to our setting, the contemporaneous AgentRCA (July 2026) builds an agentic
RCA system that maintains a hypothesis table over tool-grounded evidence, re-
porting 40.0% Top-1 and 61.5% Top-2 on its TEP evaluation set (it excludes
IDV3/9/15 for the classical detectability reasons we discuss [39]; its output is a
ranked hypothesis list without calibrated confidence; its evaluation scores Top-
1/Top-2 against TEP ground truth but, like FE's, ships no machine-replayable
external evaluation harness (hence Table 1's "no" on reproducibility); and its
detectability exclusion also covers the unspecified-cause faults IDV16­20). Ta-
ble 1 summarizes the positioning: among published and contemporaneous sys-
tems, none combines real time-series inputs, scenario-level mechanism diagnosis
with an evidence trail, cap-enforced three-state conclusions, and a machine-
verifiable evaluation contract--the last two, in particular, are absent from every
system cited above, with AgentRCA naming uncertainty communication as fu-
ture work; the broader LLM-FDD studies surveyed in [2] share these regime
limitations, applying neither truth isolation nor cap semantics to their reported
conclusions. The claim is scoped to industrial process diagnosis; the contempo-
raneous agentic-RCA line for software microservices targets a different artifact
class (distributed traces and logs) and a different evidence regime, which we do
not survey here [40].

2.3. Multi-Agent Orchestration Frameworks

    Generic agent frameworks (AutoGen [41] and commercial derivatives; ReAct-
style reasoning­acting loops [42]) provide message-passing and role prompting
but leave audit, evidence policy, and evaluation contracts to the application.
IDD's artifact-only communication is a blackboard architecture with schema-
checked writes; recent failure taxonomies for multi-agent LLM systems [43] cat-
alog the failure classes its gates address case by case (fabricated artifacts 
contract C4, Section 5; inter-agent misalignment  checkpoints CP-5/6). Our
position, following the practice of the reviewed agentic-FDD literature [2], is
that industrial diagnosis requires these as first-class, machine-checked pipeline
elements rather than prompt conventions; Section 3 describes how IDD enforces
them through checkpoints, bounded repair loops, and an event-log execution
proof.

2.4. Trust, Automation Levels, and Intellectual Ancestry

    IDD's design decisions sit on well-mapped ground, and we credit the map.
The three-state verdict is a level-of-automation and trust-calibration decision--
what the system concludes versus what it escalates to humans--in the sense
of Parasuraman et al.'s types and levels of automation [44]--a line descend-
ing from Sheridan's early function-allocation analysis [45]--and Lee & See's
appropriate-reliance account of trust [46]: a capped competing_set imple-
ments calibrated transfer in their sense, and the audit trail's effect on its human
reader is two-sided, sustaining appropriate reliance where it is checked while
inviting automation bias where it is not [47, 48]. The competing-hypotheses
discipline descends from Heuer's Analysis of Competing Hypotheses [49], the
four-condition anti-spurious filter from Hill's causation criteria [50], and the ab-
stention option from Chow's recognition-with-reject tradeoff [51], whose statis-
tical counterpart is modern selective prediction [52]; the L1­L7 grounding check
doubles as a faithfulness proxy in the sense of rigorous XAI evaluation [53].

                                                  6
The named-discriminating-measurement exit is the passive counterpart of ac-
tive fault diagnosis [54]: IDD inherits the passive setting of plant recording and
converts its residual ambiguity into an instrumentation request. The elimination
calculus itself descends from model-based diagnosis--Reiter's consistency-based
theory [55] and its multiple-fault extension [56]; IDD supplies the consistency
oracle with per-recording statistics and, where consistency cannot be reached,
returns the three-state verdict instead of forcing a single fault. The ontology-
asset layer continues the OntoCAPE lineage [57] and, behind it, the ISO 15926
plant-data modeling tradition [58]. IDD's contribution with respect to this an-
cestry is a domain-specific, machine-checked instantiation of these frameworks
inside an agentic pipeline--not their invention.

3. System Architecture

3.1. Execution Architecture

    The following terms recur throughout. A skill package is a versioned folder
holding an agent's protocol checklist, JSON Schemas, and validation scripts;
a specialist agent is an independently spawned session that executes one skill
package; an engine is the CLI/RPC harness that runs such a session. Fig. 1
shows the skill execution architecture as five planes, of which the audit plane
is machine-checked. Client surfaces (the web application, CLI drivers, and a
CI harness) submit jobs through the engine layer: fourteen interchangeable
CLI/RPC engines behind one uniform event contract, with pre-flight probing
and typed error codes. The orchestrating main agent holds the domain knowl-
edge as skill packages and dispatches each pipeline stage by passing absolute
paths (SKILL_PATH, RUN_DIR); sub-agents communicate only through workspace
artifacts, never through conversation context.

    Read against the released software, the layer map needs three qualifications.
First, the package contains 18 skill packages executed by 14 specialized agents,
but the diagnostic path reported here (Steps 2­8.5) dispatches eight special-
ist sub-agents--context-builder, data-processor, diagnostician, judge, report-
reviewer, reporter, html-visualizer, html-reviewer--with the VLM visual ana-
lyzer dispatched by the data-processor inside its Phase 5.5; the remaining pack-
ages form optional layers outside the benchmarked path (Section 3.2). Second, a
skill package is not a prompt alone: every dispatched package carries the proto-
col checklist (references/agent-protocol.md) and ships the JSON Schemas
and validation scripts that machine-check its artifacts before the stage may
complete--the structural difference from prompting a model directly. Third,
the engine layer gives the pipeline harness portability: all fourteen engines im-
plement one start/stream/close contract, so an engine substitution is a config-
uration change rather than a re-implementation--pre-registered as an ablation
axis, not yet exercised. Every stage transition appends a structured event to
the audit plane, which the log checker and the finalization gate reduce to a sin-
gle machine verdict; grading (Section 5) reads only artifacts that survived this
plane.

3.2. Knowledge, Web, and Enhancement Layers

    Beyond the diagnostic path sit three further layers. They complete the
description of the released system, but none altered a benchmark result: how
each was exercised, degraded, or left idle is recorded in the run events.

    Semantic-knowledge layer. A retrieval-augmented generation microser-
vice [59] (FastAPI + ChromaDB, rag-retrieval-engine/) supplies semantic
understanding for ontology construction and is the only sanctioned source of
level-L6 external evidence; the orchestrator probes it with a three-second health
check at Step 0. When it is unavailable, the ontology builder falls back to
the shipped parameter_to_physics.json mechanism map plus the data's own
self-description, and the degradation is recorded in the run events. In the bench-
mark corpus the channel was never available: all thirteen runs record a failed
probe and build every ontology through the documented fallback (recorded per
run as a dedicated probe-failure file or a rag_validated:false ontology flag);
the TEP ontologies ground their variable semantics in the published process
description, cross-checked against the data. No L6-tagged item exists in the
released corpus, so no verdict rests on external evidence. Because the fallback
ontology is the knowledge artifact every downstream stage actually consumed,
it is worth showing what it contains (SKAB valve scenario, translated from the
released bilingual artifact under 01_ontology in the run directory; abridged):
the equipment register grounds each component in plant physics--centrifugal
pump, "operating point = intersection of pump and loop-resistance curves";
drive motor, "current follows pump shaft load torque" (derived V ·I  226 W);
throttle valve, "regulates the loop resistance coefficient; position unmeasured--
listed as the key discriminating uncertainty"--and a confounder register records
the load-side and voltage confounders the diagnosis must exclude. The artifact
is schema-validated and machine-checkable, and its variable semantics are what
the ontology gate (CP-2/3) verifies before the statistical stage may run.

    Ontology asset store. Ontologies are published to a portable asset store
(ontology_store.mjs publish) keyed by a schema fingerprint; a later scenario
whose variable schema matches is resolved by deterministic fast-reuse, which
copies the validated ontology verbatim into the run directory in seconds instead
of re-deriving it. All thirteen benchmark ontologies were published to the store
on completion; none was consumed by fast-reuse in this corpus, because the
benchmark configuration pins the ontology step to full rebuilds (the reuse path
is exercised by the production harness outside this benchmark). The store
is deterministic and therefore does not affect the benchmark's truth-isolation
contract (it reuses variable semantics, never scenario verdicts).

    Integration surface. The web application couples an Express backend with a
Vue 3/Vite frontend and an SSE/WebSocket live stream. Its diagnosis endpoint
accepts an engine selection and returns typed pre-flight errors for unknown
or unusable engines, with probing and the real spawn sharing one code path.
The optional enhancement layer (E0­E8, deterministic scripts orchestrated by
a dedicated agent) post-processes completed runs for reporting depth and lies
outside the benchmarked path.

3.4. Execution Layer: Interchangeable Engines With a Uniform Contract

    Reasoning agents are executed by any of fourteen interchangeable engines--
the Claude Agent SDK, several RPC and ACP dialects, and a set of one-shot
CLIs--behind a single start/stream/close contract realized as a capability table
(engines.mjs) plus first-party wiring entries in the service dispatch table. En-
gine availability is probed and validated before execution with typed error codes

4.2. Competing-Hypotheses Protocol

    The diagnostician agent must generate at least three candidate hypotheses
per fault scenario (the protocol's intellectual ancestry is credited in Section 2.4),
assign each a mechanism class (e.g., fluid restriction, cavitation, rotor dynam-
ics, feed-composition step, cooling disturbance, sensor drift), collect for each an
evidence list with explicit levels, identify contradictions, and eliminate hypothe-
ses whose contradictions are stronger than their support. The protocol admits
exactly three conclusion states: determined (a single surviving hypothesis,
bounded by the five-factor assessment; reason-coded per-run ceilings may fur-
ther bind it), competing_set (2 survivors that the data cannot discriminate,
with confidence capped at 0.70; observed caps in this corpus are 0.55­0.63 and
the discriminating measurement is named), and needs_data (evidence insuffi-
cient; missing data itemized). A fourth, implicit state--normal operation with
no confirmed fault--applies to control scenarios. The elimination contract is

                              14
quantitative: every elimination must cite the contradicting evidence, and every
surviving chain must cite evidence numbers present in the statistical digests.

4.3. Evidence Grading and Gate Propagation

    Every piece of evidence carries a level L1­L7 (L1 direct measurement, L2
operating documents, L3 statistical analysis, L4 visual evidence, L5 domain on-
tology/mechanism, L6 external reference, L7 unsupported assumption). The
ordering is a policy choice: direct measurement outranks statistical processing
because a sensor reading cannot inherit a processing error, while visual evidence
is graded below statistics because chart reading is itself an inference. The sce-
nario confidence is bounded by the weakest level contributing to the surviving
chain, and the quality gate flags, for deduction, conclusions whose confidence
exceeds that bound. This converts "hallucination control" from a prompt in-
struction into a checkable arithmetic constraint.

4.4. Anti-Spurious-Correlation Filter

    Four conditions must hold before a correlation may be cited as causal sup-
port: (i) temporal precedence of cause over effect, verified by lag-compensated
cross-correlation; (ii) statistical significance surviving detrending, Simpson-
stratification checks, and multiple-testing correction; (iii) a physical mechanism
annotated in the ontology connecting the variables; and (iv) absence of contra-
dictions under leave-one-out outlier sensitivity analysis, with production-state
(steady/batch-phase) filtering applied before correlation. Correlations failing
the filter are recorded but explicitly barred from hypothesis support; defini-
tional pairs (e.g., valve position and the level it controls, r = 1.0) are classified
as control-loop artifacts. In the benchmark this filter is the mechanism by
which the pipeline bars control-loop correlations from causal citation, and the
reason normal batches with impulsive recipe-dosing spikes (|z| up to 16.2) are
graded as healthy rather than faulty. The corpus does not isolate the filter's
marginal necessity: the filter-free bare-LLM arm of Section 8.3 also passes
its two covered controls, and isolating the filter's contribution requires the
pre-registered filter-disabled arm.

4.5. Automatic Physical Verification

    After the conclusion is drafted, a physics checker recomputes quantitative
feasibility (mass/energy balances, thermal and vibration magnitude envelopes)
from the cleaned data, ontology, and anomaly report, producing a machine-
readable physical_checks verdict. When no automated check applies to a
scenario's column semantics, the pipeline falls back to an explicit L1­L5 manual-
verification memo; silent omission is not permitted.

4.6. Quality Gates, Repair Loops, and Calibration

    A ten-criterion judge--an LLM-as-judge, heir to that instrument's known
biases (self-preference, verbosity [60, 61])--scores the diagnosis (threshold 90);
a parallel physical pre-audit reviews mechanism chains; failures trigger bounded

                                                  15
repair (per-fault rerun 3, global cap 5, with anti-oscillation confidence capping
at 0.50). The final physical audit must return ENDORSED. If the review-repair
budget (2 rounds) exhausts without an endorsement, the run proceeds best-
effort and the finalization gate records the missing endorsement as a critical
failure, so the run is a pipeline failure rather than a gradeable result. Ceil-
ing compliance is then auditable end-to-end: every scenario grading records
whether the reported confidence respects the protocol ceiling, and overconfi-
dence (determined and wrong) is a first-class error metric.

    The released governance keeps a stochastic reasoner bounded by design.
Each repair round snapshots the best-scoring attempt (best_round copy of the
diagnosis set); the second and third rounds run in a declared repair scope that
recomputes only the implicated artifacts and marks the rest as carried over, so
a fix cannot silently rewrite evidence that was not found defective (auditable
from the declared repair scopes and carried-over markers in the released logs).
The loop never halts the run on score alone: if the threshold is still unmet
after the budget, the pipeline delivers a best-effort report with a gate-enforced
capped verdict (and the cap recorded), because failing to deliver is worse than
delivering with a disclosed confidence. The protocol adds two machine-checkable
disciplines. The data-truth mandate requires every number in a diagnosis to be
reproducible from the raw data (chain items carry derived markers and a NOT-
APPLICABLE fallback), and the red-light list makes the principal failure modes
auditable: no forced single verdict from a competing set, no global correlation
cited as causal evidence, no conclusion without an evidence-level tag, no HTML
outside the manifest. Violations are what the judge is instructed to flag, which
is how the audit plane of Section 3 remains a check rather than a claim.

5. A Reproducible Evaluation Framework

    Section 4's gates are only as credible as the evaluation that scores their out-
puts, so the evaluation contract is fixed before any result is produced: IDD's
benchmark framework (released as the project's experience/ evidence repos-
itory and docs/benchmark/ protocol) is designed around five contracts, each
machine-enforced:

    (C1) Truth isolation. Scenario definitions carry ground truth and grad-
ing keywords, but the pipeline's visible inputs contain only the process de-
scription and the data. Ground truth never enters the inference context; the
grader owns it exclusively. The agent-visible brief itself carries exactly three
fields--user question, process description, and a truth-withheld flag; dataset,
role, expected-verdict-set, and grading metadata remain on the grader's side of
the released bundle. A leakage sentinel machine-checks the contract from both
directions: no ground-truth string may appear in any agent-visible brief, and
no fault-vocabulary may appear in the run directories' user-context files. Sce-
nario definitions--including the grading keywords and the allowed verdict set
of every scenario--are versioned in the released repository with their edit his-
tory. The keyword sets used to grade the current corpus are the calibrated set
pinned at commit 221d061--over-generic tokens removed, mechanism synonyms

                                                  16
added--then frozen before any current-era execution. The calibration is truth-
adjacent in two ways that Section 7.2 quantifies: the "v1-era corpus" it was tuned
against denotes the pre-discipline execution batch of the same twelve scenarios
(run directories dated before the 2026-09-14 anti-oscillation and confidence-cap
revision, retained with the released edit history), and the published per-fault
baselines contributed the mechanism synonyms. The sentinel machine-checks
briefs and user-context files, not run-directory names; the identifier channel
is the residual disclosure channel and is treated in Section 9. These keys are
author-designed (Section 9): a hit certifies mechanism-family agreement with
the documented truth, not identity, and one scenario's set includes a generic
token.

    (C2) Data fingerprinting. Every input file is hashed (SHA-256) into a
dataset manifest; the reproducibility gate re-verifies all 153 input fingerprints
and the leakage sentinel returns a PASS verdict before any metric is accepted.

    (C3) Stage-authored artifacts with provenance. Each pipeline
stage writes its own machine-checkable artifacts: the ontology, the statis-
tical analysis conclusion, the four-piece diagnosis, the judge's ten-criterion
feedback, the auditors' ENDORSED verdicts, the report, the page model
(render_manifest.json) behind the HTML deliverable, and the reviewer's
independent verdict. Every scored verdict carries an explicit provenance la-
bel (judge-agent-10-criteria, auditor-agent, html-reviewer-agent), so
downstream consumers can distinguish independently authored judgments from
rendered ones.

    (C4) Gate-graded scoring. The grader accepts a scenario only if its run
directory contains the complete artifact set of an executed pipeline (the fourteen
items in Table 4), the event log passes the log checker, and the finalization gate
returns PASS. Otherwise the scenario is reported as not executed --a pipeline
failure, not a benchmark result. The gate is exercised in practice: an earlier
protocol revision allowed a scripted shortcut that expanded a single reasoning
note into a full artifact set (complete with synthesized agent events); the inter-
nal integrity audit flagged the fabrication, the shortcut was deleted from the
codebase, and the entire benchmark was re-executed through the real pipeline.
The grader's completeness gate makes the shortcut permanently unreplayable.

    (C5) Artifact-consistency gate. A verifier recomputes all aggregate met-
rics from the per-scenario gradings, re-checks dataset hashes, verifies case cover-
age, and requires an execution proof per run; any drift fails the evaluation. The
gate verifies the internal consistency of the recorded execution--recomputed
aggregates, hashes, coverage, proofs--and is deliberately not an independent
re-execution of the reasoning layer; the released report carries the resulting
internally-consistent verdict.

    Execution form. The canonical benchmark runs reported here were exe-

pipeline stage to an independent sub-agent session through the harness's sub-
agent spawn tool, passing absolute paths (SKILL_PATH, RUN_DIR); sub-agents
communicated only through workspace artifacts, never through conversation
context, and each authored its own artifacts under its own skill protocol. The

benchmark studies [19, 20]: each experimental batch/file appears in exactly

splitting is used anywhere.

6.2. Metrics

    For fault scenarios we report resolved Top-1 (a determined verdict nam-
ing the true mechanism; coincides with the FDD community's correct-diagnosis-
rate, CDR), top-k (the true mechanism is named within the primary finding
or the surviving-hypothesis set that the grader reads; because the protocol
mandates at least three generated hypotheses, this approximates candidate-
list membership, and for a single-survivor determined verdict it coincides
with resolved Top-1; reported alongside, not instead of, resolved Top-1), ceil-
ing compliance (reported confidence within the protocol ceiling of Section 4),
verdict-type compliance (conclusion type within the scenario's allowed set;

                    20
a determined-and-wrong verdict counts as overconfidence), and a protocol-
compliance rubric score--a deterministic, machine-computed checklist (100
points) computed from the pipeline artifacts themselves and measuring con-
formance to this paper's own protocol rather than correctness (see Section 9).
Top-k is structurally favored when several scenarios in a family share keyword
tokens (the three reactor-cooling TEP faults share "reactor cooling"); read it as
family-level membership, not specific-form identification. The strictest, branch-
localized reading would count IDV3 as a miss (its D/E-feed-temperature sur-
vivor sits on the stripper-feed branch while the documented truth is the reactor-
feed D stream), giving top-k 8/9 under that reading--both readings are re-
ported. For control scenarios we report pass (no confirmed fault) and false
alarms. Proportions carry Wilson 95% confidence intervals at z=1.96 (the re-
leased scorer's constant). We do not report sample-level F1/AUC: they answer
a different question and are used only as reference context (comparison protocol
below).

    The rubric's seven checks are computed strictly from the stage-authored run
artifacts: R1 artifact integrity (ontology, analysis conclusion, and the schema-
valid diagnosis four-piece; 15 points), R2 hypothesis structure (3 hypotheses,
2 eliminations each citing substantive contradictory evidence; 15), R3 evi-
dence grounding (every surviving chain cites at least one number present in the
scenario's own evidence brief; 20; fault scenarios--the check has no fault-relevant

scenarios by rule rather than by post-hoc exception), R4 calibration (confidence
within the protocol ceiling; conclusion type within the scenario's allowed set;
15), R5 falsifiability (a surviving hypothesis names the observation that would
revise it; 10), R6 physics verification (automated check valid or explicit manual
memo; 10), R7 execution proof (event log + finalization PASS + HTML review
gate; 15). The pipeline's internal ten-criterion judge remains the in-pipeline gate
(Section 4)--its scores are recorded with provenance and reported as execution-
quality evidence--but it is not a benchmark metric: benchmark quality scoring
is fully mechanical. The rubric's scope is limited in two ways. First, it is a
conformance instrument with limited discriminative power: nine of the twelve

that has passed the gates--correctness claims rest on the truth comparison,
never on the rubric. Second, "strict mechanism-keyword hit" is not mechanism
equivalence: the keyword sets are disclosed per scenario, one of them (the Ind-
PenSim process-deviation scenario) includes the generic token "deviation", and
a hit certifies mechanism-family agreement, not identity. The consequences are
quantified in Section 7, including an identity-strict Top-1 count that discounts

6.3. Comparison Protocol

    We distinguish two comparison registers. Register A (same-task direct
comparison): methods that output one root-cause conclusion per scenario on
the same dataset family; the published FaultExplainer per-fault numbers fall
here, at scenario granularity. Register B (reference only): sample-level de-
tection/classification results; they are reported for context and must not be
ranked against our scenario-level metrics. Register A comparisons inherit two
protocol differences: FaultExplainer supplies candidate root causes in its prompt
and accepts alias matches, whereas IDD uses no candidates and scores mecha-
nism keywords strictly; and FaultExplainer scores only the 11 PCA-detectable
faults, whereas IDD additionally scores the two faults that screen excludes,
IDV3/IDV4. Because the scenario sets differ, no significance test is claimed
across systems.

6.4. Implementation

anomaly profiling, correlation with lag-compensated cross-correlation functions

criterion scores (97/100), and the independent HTML review (pass), is persisted
in the grading record and released with the evaluation. We read the cap as the
finding: on a fault the published baseline cannot even score, the pipeline local-
izes the loop, states the physically necessary mechanism family, quantifies why
the two remaining sub-mechanisms cannot be separated, and names the single
measurement--the cooling-water inlet thermocouple--whose installation would
collapse the set to determined.

8.4. Verdict Stability: a Random-Draw Independent Re-execution

    The evaluation framework formalizes consistency testing as a draw-then-re-
execute protocol: a scenario is selected by a seeded uniform random draw over
the nine-fault pool (so the selection is reproducible and fixed before comparison,

                                                  40
though the seed originates from the evaluation harness rather than an external
randomness beacon--a limitation we state alongside the draw record) and then
re-executed through the full gated pipeline in a fresh run directory with fresh
agent sessions (new ontology build, new statistical stage, new diagnosis, new
review cycle, new HTML build and review); the draw record is append-only and
replayable from the released seed. In the current corpus the draw selected TEP
IDV11 (seed 1003818694; the record carries the pool, the raw uniform value,
and the resulting index), and the drawn re-execution completed all nine stages
end to end in 2.0 h, passing every gate (finalization pass). The framework's
rule is that the latest proven execution of a scenario is its canonical graded
run, so the re-execution (0.60) superseded the original batch execution (0.62)
in every aggregate; both are capped competing_set verdicts with the same
signature, so the supersession changes no headline number. Fig. 10 maps the
full audit.

    The two executions of the drawn scenario--the corpus's single repeat pair
(n=1): the original batch execution 202609171639527 (run 1) and the drawn
re-execution 202609172247007 (run 2, now canonical)--agree on everything
the protocol measures at the mechanism level: the verdict state (capped
competing_set in both), the implicated actuator (XMV_10, the reactor
cooling-water valve), the mechanism class (environment-side cooling distur-
bance), and the top-ranked hypothesis (a cooling-water-side disturbance rather

9. Discussion, Limitations, and Deployment Prospects

    This section consolidates boundaries that the results already carry, in four
groups: evidence strength (single executions, purposive sampling, the reading
structure of Section 7.2), prompt-knowledge contamination, the operating se-
mantics of capped verdicts, and the provenance, cost, and deployment boundary
of the audit plane.

    Statistical power and single-execution evidence. With n=9 fault sce-
narios, Top-1 = 44.4% supports only a wide interval ([18.9%, 73.3%] at 95%
confidence); the top-k bound (9/9, [70.1%, 100%]) is the stronger statement
the current sample size can support--read as family-level set membership, not
as a resolution bound: the instrument's strictness is not specificity, since the
protocol mandates at least three hypotheses and the keyword sets derive from
the documented cause table (Section 6); what 9/9 certifies is that no failure
omitted the true family, not how often the protocol could have committed
earlier. Scenario selection was purposive rather than random: the suite over-
represents documented, public fault episodes, which biases difficulty downward
and buys mechanism diversity across families rather than within them, so the
intervals in Table 6 read as measurement uncertainty on this corpus, not sam-
pling generalization to a scenario population. Three qualifications attach to
these figures. First, every headline number is a single recorded execution per
scenario--except TEP IDV11, whose drawn re-execution (Section 8.4) repro-
duced the verdict state and mechanism at confidence 0.02, the only repeat
measurement the corpus currently holds. Second, the bounded repair loop in-
troduces a selection effect, since a reported verdict may be the product of up
to five attempts; in this corpus, however, twelve of thirteen runs closed without
diagnosis-layer repair, the single exception being disclosed with the configuration
(Section 6). Third, the protocol's numeric thresholds--the 2-elimination floor,
the 0.90 exclusion-confidence requirement, the 0.70/0.65/0.50 caps, the judge's
90-point gate--are fixed policy constants applied unchanged across the corpus;
no threshold-sensitivity arm has been run, and the headline counts are condi-
tional on them. The model-controlled ablation (Section 8.3) already bounds the
accuracy claim--so the pipeline's value on documented faults is process qual-
ity, not accuracy. Expanding the repeats (all 12 scenarios, k  3: verdict-type
stability, confidence spread, repair counts) remains pre-registered, with eleven
scenarios awaiting their second in-era execution--gaps we report rather than
fill; extending the corpus (the full 20-class TEP catalog with the extended sim-
ulation dataset [74]; bearing datasets; novel-disturbance suites) is the designed
path to tighter intervals.

    Prompt-knowledge contamination. TEP's documented fault mecha-
nisms appear in textbooks and are therefore plausibly memorized by frontier
models. Our protocol mitigates this at the level where memorization actually
operates--but the mitigation has a limit. The documented cause table records
not only the disturbed channel but each fault's dynamic form (IDV4 step,
IDV11 random variation, IDV14 stiction), so recall of that table alone could
reproduce the observed mechanism labels; the statistics-driven reading rests on

                                                  43
the recorded reasoning chains, each of which cites the per-recording statistic
that discriminates its form. Two residual channels are disclosed. First, the
scenario identifier: every TEP and SKAB case id and run-directory name em-
beds the documented mechanism (and dynamic form where the catalog records
one)--tep_d14_reactor_valve_sticking names the answer outright, and
skab_cavitation_13, the corpus's only brief-blind scenario, is no exception--
so identifier recall is possible in principle across the documented catalog; IDV14,
one of the four resolved verdicts, is the sharpest case. The leakage sentinel
machine-checks brief and user-context content, not directory names, so the iden-
tifier channel sits outside it. A second, adjacent channel is the dispatcher itself:
the twelve canonical scenarios were batched under one orchestrating session,
whose context could in principle carry directory names across scenarios--the
gates bound what stages write, not what the dispatcher has read--so the
committed neutralized-identifier re-run mandates one orchestrating session per
scenario as well.

    The joint worst case is stated rather than implied: no resolved verdict sur-
vives both the identifier-recall discount on the TEP-derived outcomes and the
identity-strict batch-93 discount--the four resolved verdicts are precisely three
TEP hits plus batch 93--so the joint floor is 0/9, and the identity-strict read-
ing alone is 3/9; in Table 6 and Fig. 4 the three TEP-derived resolved ver-
dicts should be read as a recall-possible tier until the neutralized-identifier
re-run executes. That re-run (the six TEP and two SKAB fault scenarios
under opaque hash run identifiers and identifier-stripped brief headers, 16 h
of agent time by the cost accounting below) is committed as the first post-
revision execution; it requires a contained harness extension (hash-id run di-
rectories) tracked in the released repository. The briefs themselves carry no
fault vocabulary (leakage-sentinel checked), and the form-discrimination evi-
dence of Section 7.3 is the main evidence-backed mitigation among the disclosed
residual channels: the mechanism labels the pipeline commits to are read off
per-recording statistics, which identifier recall would not require. Under the
identifier channel alone, recall could supply both the mechanism and its dy-
namic form--sharpening, not replacing, the recall-possible reading of the three
TEP-derived resolved verdicts (IDV1, IDV7, IDV14) stated above. As a partial
counter-check, the SKAB "other"-group scenario, whose fault label is withheld
entirely from all natural-language brief content, still yielded the cavitation-type
mechanism ranked among the survivors of a capped verdict--and the ranking of-
fers weak counter-evidence against recall doing the work: the cap-internal order
tracks five-factor evidence weights (Section 7.4), so a directory-supplied label
with no recording evidence would not be expected to out-rank a statistically
favored survivor--and cavitation ranks second of two, behind the valve-side hy-
pothesis whose statistics the recording favors. Briefs are clean; directory names
are not; both facts are disclosed. The RAG channel was unavailable in every
run (Section 3.2), and no L6-tagged item exists anywhere in the released corpus:
no verdict rests on external evidence--every result in this paper was obtained
in the degraded, no-external-knowledge mode. A novel-disturbance suite (com-
bination perturbations absent from the literature) is the definitive control and

remains under construction.
    What the five capped verdicts mean. The five competing_set out-

comes are the protocol working as designed, and they are also the benchmark's
most transferable finding: each verdict localizes the disturbance to a subsystem,
eliminates whole mechanism families with quantitative contradictions, keeps the
true mechanism ranked among the survivors, and names the one measurement
whose addition would collapse the set to determined (Section 7.4). In deploy-
ment terms these are not failures but work orders for targeted instrumentation--
with one attribution caveat: a deterministic alternative path to the same content
(localize the subsystem from the statistics, then enumerate that subsystem's un-
measured channels from the ontology) has not been tested by a script-only arm,
so attributing this finding to the reasoning protocol rather than to sensor-set
identifiability is a hypothesis; the UA-fork quantification of Section 7.5 is the
current evidence that the reasoning layer adds more than a channel inventory.
The three-state protocol is nonetheless what converts a diagnosis into that engi-
neering workflow: determined conclusions carry mechanism chains that point
to corrective action; capped conclusions carry the named channel. To keep the
framing falsifiable, the released scenario definitions record the expected verdict
set of every scenario, fixed before execution; SKAB inlet-valve throttling is the
one scenario whose expectation was determined-only, and its capped verdict
is counted as the benchmark's single compliance miss rather than narrated as a
success.

    Operating semantics of capped verdicts. A capped verdict is action-
able only if its consumer knows what to do between the verdict and the named
measurement's installation--an instrumentation lead time of weeks or months
during which the anomaly is live. The named channel says what would set-
tle the set, not what to do meanwhile, so a deployment needs three operating
complements that the benchmark does not measure: (i) an interim envelope cov-
ering both survivors (operate to the intersection of the conditions under which
each survivor is safe); (ii) an escalation trigger (the monitored signature whose
drift past a stated bound re-opens or tightens the set); and (iii) a decision-
rights rule mapping states to actions (determined  maintenance-approval
path; competing_set  deferred observation with explicit acceptance crite-
ria; needs_data  a data-acquisition task). The corpus's 5/9 capped base rate
also matters for trust calibration: an operator half of whose verdicts are capped
must sustain appropriate reliance (Section 2.4) across both states, and under-
capping--the costly direction the benchmark's one-directional typing cannot see
(Section 7.4)--is where that reliance would erode first. For IDV4 the envelope
is concrete: operate the reactor temperature inside its pre-onset band with the
cooling valve XMV_10 below its pre-onset ceiling--conditions safe under both
survivors--and treat sustained drift of the reactor-temperature residual beyond
its pre-onset  as the escalation trigger that re-opens the set.

    Cost. The clean per-scenario figure for the full gated pipeline is the sequen-
tial drawn re-execution: 2.0 h of agent time end to end (117 min event span),
of which the deterministic statistical stage--script-speed, seconds--is a negli-

                                                         46
gible share. Per-stage wall-clock spans across the thirteen released event logs
give a median full-pipeline agent span of 1.9 h (112 min: ontology 14 min,
statistics 22 min, diagnosis 20 min, the two review stages 29 min combined,
reporting and HTML the remaining 26 min). The twelve canonical scenar-
ios were executed as concurrent batches under one orchestrating agent (per-run
event spans of 6 h therefore reflect batching, not isolated effort; Section 6).
The pipeline is orders of magnitude more expensive than a fitted classifier at
inference time and far slower than single-prompt baselines. The cost profile
bounds the task fit: episodic, high-consequence investigations--batch-deviation,
GxP, unit-trip RCA, where expert studies already run hours to days--are the
natural regime; minute-scale alarm triage is out of scope by construction, and
needs_data is the designed answer when the recording cannot support the
question. The economic comparison is only meaningful against the engineer-
ing cost of obtaining root causes (expert hours), and the ontology-asset layer
(Section 3.2) amortizes the reasoning cost across a plant's scenario fleet.

9.1. Industrial Deployment Prospects

    We separate what the benchmark and one operational deployment demon-
strate from what the architecture projects.

    One deployment anecdote. This example is self-reported and unaudited
(artifacts anonymized, not part of the released corpus): a film-line run exer-
cised the pipeline end to end on episodic scratch defects of a biaxially-oriented
polyester film line and returned needs_data with an explicit discriminating-
data list after 180 parameter­defect associations failed false-discovery-rate
correction--no cause was fabricated. Projected: the integration, sovereignty,
and documentation claims below are design properties of the architecture, not
yet benchmarked outcomes.

    Integration with existing plant infrastructure. IDD consumes what
plants already produce: flat exports from historians (OPC-UA/PI), LIMS
quality records, and batch logs. The same pipeline runs interactively through
a web application and programmatically through a versioned REST API
(/api/diagnosis with engine selection and typed error contracts), so a de-
ployment is an export job plus API integration rather than an instrumentation
project--the integration work is real but bounded by the historian export
format. One export hazard deserves its own warning: historian compression
(swinging-door and deadband algorithms) systematically destroys or fabri-
cates exactly the statistics this pipeline's signatures rest on--variance ratios,
quantized stepping, frozen means--so an export must request uncompressed
or fixed-period data, and the export's compression settings belong in the run
configuration and its fingerprint. The first site check for a missing channel is
whether the tag exists but is unmapped in the export, which is more common
than true absence. Because the deterministic stage is byte-reproducible, results
do not drift when the surrounding plant IT systems change.

    Data-sovereign deployment. The fourteen-engine execution layer (Sec-
tion 3) accepts on-premise and private-model agents through uniform dialect
adapters. For operators who cannot send process data to external LLM

                                                  47
services--defense, pharmaceuticals, semiconductors, national infrastructure--
the reasoning stage can be pointed at an internally hosted model without
touching the audit, evidence, or gate contracts--no non-default engine has
yet executed the full gated pipeline in the released corpus, so this path is
design-level, like the amortization projection; the benchmark protocol itself
executes identically against a scripted mock engine, which is how the full chain
is exercised in CI.

    Documentation for regulated operations. Because every conclusion is
bound to graded evidence, physics checks, and a fingerprinted execution trace,
the by-product of each run is an RCA report whose traceability serves the doc-
umentation expectations of regulated environments (e.g., GxP batch-deviation
investigation, in the sense of ALCOA+-style data integrity [75] and GAMP 5
computerized-systems thinking [76]). This is a documentation property of the
artifact, not a computerized-system validation claim; the latter would require a
formal qualification exercise.

    Deployment governance and knowledge compounding. IDD is posi-
tioned as decision support: a determined verdict does not authorize mainte-
nance action--a human engineer signs off. In the current release the sign-off is
procedural (no separate human-endorsement artifact exists in the run contract,
and all thirteen benchmark runs were fully autonomous); we disclose this as
an implementation gap, not a deployed control, and the three-state protocol is
precisely the escalation interface between machine conclusion and human deci-
sion. Model-change management rides the engine layer's version disclosure (Sec-
tion 6): a provider silently updating the reasoning model changes the system,
so the benchmark configuration disclosure doubles as the deployment pinning
record. A safety-context boundary is equally explicit: IDD is an off-line advisory
layer for post-event investigation; it must not be connected to any protection or
interlock path--in the sense of safety instrumented functions (IEC 61511, Func-
tional safety--Safety instrumented systems for the process industry sector )--and
the three-state escalation interface presupposes a human decision-maker outside
the loop. The system's own model card (model family and snapshot, protocol
version, known failure modes: identifier recall, one-directional verdict typing,
the two cap sets' gap) ships with the repository. And because the evaluation
harness and this manuscript were produced with AI assistance inside the same
human­AI loop that produced the system, independent deployment validation
is a precondition for safety-relevant use. Finally, the ontology layer compounds
knowledge across runs (Section 3.2): all thirteen benchmark runs published
their ontologies to the store on completion, and every field incident becomes a
candidate graded scenario, closing the loop between deployment and evaluation.
In deployment the marginal cost of a diagnosis therefore falls as the fleet grows
(projected: the benchmark pinned full rebuilds, so this amortization is design-
level, not measured)--an economics that sample-level detectors, which must be
retrained per asset, do not share.

    Scope of applicability. The method requires multivariate time-ordered
process data with engineering semantics for the channels (to build the ontol-
ogy) and applies to continuous, batch, and testbed processes alike--the three

                                                  48
benchmark families were chosen to span exactly that range. It does not apply
where no time-aligned process data exists, and its root-cause reach is bounded
by the sensor set, as the capped verdicts demonstrate.

10. Conclusion

    We presented IDD, an agentic diagnosis pipeline that turns LLM-based root-
cause analysis into an auditable engineering procedure--competing hypothe-
ses with mechanism classes, evidence levels bounding reported confidence, a
four-condition anti-spurious filter, physical verification, and quality gates with
bounded repair--together with a machine-checked evaluation contract for diag-
nosis benchmarks: truth isolation, data fingerprinting, stage-authored artifacts
with provenance, and gate-graded scoring. On the 12-scenario corpus the head-
line outcome is deliberately modest: 4/9 faults resolved Top-1 (a provisional
upper-bound reading under the disclosed identifier channel), the true mecha-
nism ranked in every fault scenario's surviving set (top-k 9/9; 8/9 under the
branch-localized reading, which demands the specific stream branch rather than
the family alone; Section 7.2)--a family-level set-membership statement, not a
specificity-tested resolution bound--zero false alarms (one of three blind), five
capped verdicts that each name the measurement that would settle them, and
every number backed by an execution proof. The model-controlled ablation
bounds what the pipeline can claim: a bare call to the same model already ex-
ceeds its accuracy (9/9 versus 4/9), so the contribution is the auditable process
rather than the accuracy. One seeded re-execution reproduced verdict state,
actuator, and mechanism class within two confidence points; the deterministic
suite reproduced byte-for-byte; and the per-fault TEP comparison shows IDD
resolving the GPT-4o baseline's IDV1 miss while scoring the PCA-excluded pair
that baselines exclude, as capped verdicts with the mechanism family ranked.
These claims have limits: resolution of documented faults is the pipeline's weak
axis by its own ablation (Section 8.3), results are single recorded executions
of a stochastic reasoning layer whose configuration is disclosed and whose run
identifiers embed the documented mechanism labels, so the recall-possible read-
ing of Section 9 applies to every headline verdict; the machine gate's flat 0.90
non-CS ceiling flags two verdicts (0.91 and 0.93) that the agent-side cap set per-
mits, so the two cap sets' gap is measured rather than hidden; and the rubric
and keywords remain author-designed, with expert anchoring outstanding (Sec-
tion 9). The evidence base is one model family at one deployment on twelve
public-dataset scenarios; cross-model and cross-plant generalization of the pro-
cess guarantees (in contrast to the accuracy findings, which the ablation already
bounds) remains to be demonstrated. The evaluation framework, per-scenario
evidence chains, and consistency artifacts are released to support scrutiny and
reuse; the pre-registered repeat and ablation studies, and the novel-disturbance
suite, are the immediate next steps.
